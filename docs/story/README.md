## The story in ten lines

1. Salt Creek, 1882. The Baron wants every drop of water in the valley, and he's patient.
2. He can't use the Army against a quiet town, so he sets out to make it look like one in revolt.
3. A stranger shoots the sheriff over the family's water: not Kett (Sean, 2026-11-13), and by
   the Baron's new foreman (C16, decided). Harlan finds the sheriff on the road, and the
   dying man swears him in.
4. That first night another of the Baron's men, Cody, murders a card player in front of the whole
   saloon, because there's no law left to stop him (Harlan carries the man to the doctor's door, too late: Sean, 2026-11-17), while the foreman, the sheriff's killer, holds
   Harlan in his chair. The same night Kett's crew raids Joe's camp, to start an "outbreak" for the
   Baron's telegrams.
5. Harlan and Joe hunt down Kett, the man in the faded red shirt. Joe asks: are you going to stop me? Arrest him and the law lets him
   go; step aside and Joe has his revenge. Either way someone comes for Joe with a rope, and Harlan
   saves him with the dead sheriff's rifle, or doesn't.
6. Cody walks back into the saloon, and Harlan arrests him: the one man who can tie the Baron to it.
   His own side comes to drop the water tower on him, to free him or to crush him.
7. Harlan learns dynamite from a miner, and takes back the widow's farm from the Colters, hired by
   the Baron now that Kett's crew is gone.
8. The Baron tries to wreck the payroll train. Harlan stops it (or doesn't, and the town goes
   hungry), and either way the dance floor the Baron's men sawed through comes down under them. An
   Army officer on the Baron's pay is waiting with a surgeon and a letter. Next day an Army captain
   comes to see if the town's in revolt.
9. Harlan takes the Baron's papers from his iron coach, blows the trestle under the train that comes
   for the town (Rourke, his old commander, dies on it), breaks into the Baron's house and arrests
   him.
10. The newspaper says what became of the Baron, by the evidence Harlan kept. Then he walks the town,
    and it shows him what he made of it.

## The three strongest setups and payoffs

1. **"Ain't no law left in this valley to save you now."** Cody says it over Earl's body on night
   one. Later he's in Harlan's cell with his own side coming to silence him, and the law is the only
   thing that can save him. No Law, No Justice, I Am the Law: the three titles are one sentence.
   (S2. The payoff line is proposed, not yours yet.)
2. **"Friend."** The dying sheriff says the man who shot him called him "friend" and rode a
   bald-faced sorrel. That night the sorrel is at the saloon's rail and the stranger at Harlan's
   table says "Don't move, friend." The player knows the killer himself, and can do nothing about it;
   nobody tells him. (S40, your pick, C16 a: decided. It replaces the faded red shirt here: the
   shirt is the camp's now, S1, since you decided on 2026-11-13 the two killers are two men.)
3. **The sheriff's rifle.** It rides into town on his horse on day one, the widow hands it over in
   the quiet mission, and it's the rifle that cuts Joe down. (S13, S14. Yours, but for the rifle being on the saddle on day one, which is proposed.)

A fourth that's only proposed, and the best thing in here if you want it: **the band**. They come in
on the payroll's train, among the people Harlan saves at noon. Their piano goes up the saloon stairs
that afternoon. They're playing when the floor goes. (S24.)

## The weakest gaps, and what your picks did to them

1. **The stranger at the table** said "You didn't see a thing tonight" and was never seen again.
   **Answered (C10 b):** he's the Baron's foreman, and he comes back on the widow's lane with the
   deed. Left: his voice, educated or a cowman's (s21), and who sends Harlan to the saloon (s26).
2. **Nothing said why the Baron's men raided Joe's camp.** **Answered (C4 a):** provocation, for an
   "outbreak"; the costume stays and is what spoils it.
3. **Kett's crew was wiped out at the cabin**, and then held the farm. **Answered (C5 b):** the cabin
   finished them; the Baron hires the Colters for the farm. Left: whether Cody is at the cabin (s24).
4. **Payday Night had two designs**, and nothing for a lost pay. **Answered (C1 a, C2 b, C3 b):** two
   barrels; a hungry night if the pay is lost; if he finds out he can only be ready. Left: when the
   girder is cut, and why the floor still falls after a wreck (s23).
5. **The soldiers on the last train.** **Answered (C11 a):** always the Baron's hired men; Rourke
   dies on the trestle. Left: Rourke if the trestle isn't blown under him (s25).
6. **The Colters, the Kid, the bold cowboy and Harlan's own spring** are set up and never paid off
   (`SETUPS.md`, "Missing"). Still open: the picks gave the Colters and the Kid the farm, not an end.

## The mission board (Sean's page for going through the missions)

**https://claude.ai/artifact/W9WEgdsjyofUXrLRdjdQDe**, pinned in Sean's claude.ai sidebar (2026-11-17: "a pinned board
that you go into from the main side menu and from there you can select the missions ... and add notes"). It is
`MISSIONS.md` and the full record as a page: the cross-mission lists first, then every mission by act; tap one to read
it; every line has a note under it, and each mission has one for the whole of it.

- **Reading his notes:** ArtifactData, the URL above, `list` collection `notes`. Each document is
  `{note, about, where, at}`: `about` is the line's own words (so the line can be found if numbering has moved),
  `where` the mission and section. The id says where it sits: `m.<mission>` (the whole mission),
  `m.<mission>.<section>.<n>` (a line), `t.<list>.<n>` (a line of a cross-mission list). They are his: never
  overwrite or delete one. When a note is acted on, write the answer into `MISSIONS.md` and the bible, not the page.
- **The scripts are in it too** (Sean, 2026-11-19: "You didn't put the full scripts in the pinned board"): the sheriff's,
  No Law's and No Justice's missions each have "The script, line by line", built from `docs/scripts/<mission>.json`:
  every line, who says it, when, the condition and the delivery. A note on a script line has the id
  `s.<script>.<line id>` (a scene's setting: `s.<script>.scene.<scene id>`), so it stays on its line however the
  script is reordered; No Law's copy of the sheriff's last day uses the sheriff's ids, so one note shows in both.
  **Script OK and the takes stay on the three script pages** (`docs/scripts/README.md`): read notes from both places.
- **Updating the page** after `MISSIONS.md`, `missions/` or a script changes: `python3 docs/story/board/build_board.py /tmp/missions.json`,
  then publish `docs/story/board/missions_board.html` with `url` (read it first), `files`
  `{"missions.json": "/tmp/missions.json"}`; `capabilities` carries forward (`db`). The JSON is built, not committed.
  A line's note is keyed by its place in its section, so adding lines at the end of a section keeps his notes on
  the right lines; inserting in the middle moves them (the note's `about` says what it was on).


## The people board (Sean's page for going through the people)

**https://claude.ai/artifact/2FKryCBxoS8bhGegoFLR38**, pinned in Sean's claude.ai sidebar (#1309, Sean 2026-11-27: "each character has its own
individual slide for review"). It is [`people.json`](people.json) as a page: everyone in a list (the townsfolk, Sean's own,
the missions' people), each on his own page (the whole sheet; his day from `config/valley_folk.json`, in words; his lines
grouped by where you meet him, with the hours he's there and who's there with him), and a view by place (the saloon's
bar, the store, the street: who's there and everything said there).

- **The sheets** are `people.json`, keyed by the roster's id (`config/valley_folk.json`); its `_doc` has the fields.
  A townsperson's name is a new field, never a new id. `kind`: `townsfolk` (proposed here, Sean's to OK), `cast` (a
  mission's character: only what the bible fixes, in `fixed`; not renamed; open fields are null), `sean` (written by Sean:
  Claude Bérubé, #1207). A line's `place` is a tag of `places`: #1293's overheard talk uses the same tags, and a line of
  `docs/scripts/town.json` with `who` (a person's id) and `place` shows on his page and the place's.
- **Reading his notes:** ArtifactData, the URL above, `list` collection `notes`. Each is `{note, about, where, at}`; the id
  says what it's on: `p.<id>` (the whole person), `p.<id>.<field>` (name, called, age, from, came, work, place, wants,
  secret, habit, voice, body, clothes, day), `p.<id>.likes.<other id>`, `p.<id>.talks.<n>`, `p.<id>.line.<line id>`,
  `a.<n>` (an assumption on the front page). They are his: never overwrite or delete one. Act on a note in `people.json`.
- **Person OK:** collection `ok`, one document a person (`{ok, hash, at, name}`). It counts only while `hash` is the
  sheet's own (the build's hash of his fields and sample lines, not his day or the town script's lines), so any change to
  a sheet asks for his OK again, with nothing to remember to bump. Never write these.
- **Updating the page** after `people.json` or the days change: `python3 docs/story/board/build_people.py --check`, then
  `python3 docs/story/board/build_people.py /tmp/people_board.json`, then publish `docs/story/board/people_board.html`
  with `url` (read it first) and `files` `{"people_board.json": "/tmp/people_board.json"}`; `capabilities` carries forward
  (`db`). The JSON is built, not committed.
