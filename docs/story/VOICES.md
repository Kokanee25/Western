# Voices: who talks how, and who knows what, when

The story bible, part 3 of 5 (#809). Planning only. A sheet for every speaking character: background,
how they talk, what they call Harlan, what they know and from when, five lines, their threads.

**Read this first.**
- Lines marked **(built)** are in the game now (`config/moments/the_sheriff.json`,
  `no_law_murder.json`). Lines marked **(Sean)** are his words or his decided beat. **Every other
  sample line is P·bible:** written here to show the voice, not approved, not a script.
- Status words as in `OUTLINE.md`. Facts about a character are D only where Sean's date is given.
- The period's words and the words to avoid are in `WORDS.md`. A line here that breaks `WORDS.md` is
  wrong.
- **Nobody says what they couldn't know yet.** Each sheet has a timeline; the table at the end has
  the ten secrets and who holds each one from when. A script is checked against both (#807).
- DESIGN.md §9 says "nobody says the player's name". It was written before he had one. He's Thomas
  Harlan (D, 2026-11-09) and the built sheriff calls him Harlan, so the sheets say what each person
  calls him.

## The principals

### Thomas Harlan (the player)
- **Who:** a homesteader, a few months in the valley, with a spring on his claim; about forty; a
  Union army hospital steward in the war (P, DESIGN.md §6). Deputy from B3, never called sheriff (D,
  2026-11-09).
- **How he talks:** almost never. The player answers with the nod and the shake, and by what he does.
  When Harlan does speak (P, #546 P9, "rare lines") it's five words, a steward's orders, in the
  imperative. No speeches. Never explains himself.
- **Knows:** only what the player has seen. He never says a thing the player might have missed.
- **Lines:**
  1. "Now what the hell is going on here?" (Sean's example, 2026-11-07, for the train)
  2. "Press here. Harder. Don't look at it, look at me."
  3. "He's gone. Help me with this one."
  4. "Drop it!" (built, the shout)
  5. "There's law in Salt Creek. You're looking at it." (pays S2; only if Sean wants him to say it)
- **Threads:** all. T8 is his, and P.

### The sheriff (name O)
- **Who:** the county's or the town's law (C12), in his fifties, fourteen years on his claim east of
  town with his wife and two boys (D: the lock; #540). Shot at B0 over the family's water right (D).
  Dies at B3 whatever Harlan does (D, 2026-11-03).
- **How he talks:** orders, even on his back. Plain, dry, no wasted word. Calls a man of forty "son".
  As he loses blood the sentences shorten to a word (D: signature feature 1; three bands built).
- **Calls Harlan:** "Harlan" (built). "Son", once.
- **Knows:** B0, his killer's shirt and manner, not his name: he took him for a traveller and let him
  ride up. That the Baron has wanted his ditch for years (P·bible), so he can guess why. B1, whether
  Harlan helped. He does **not** know about Earl or the camp unless someone tells him at B3.
- **Lines:**
  1. "Man in a faded red shirt. Rode up easy, like he wanted directions. Then he shot me." (built)
  2. "Well? Nod or don't, but don't just stand there." (built)
  3. "That's not the way this works, son." (Sean's beat, 2026-11-09; this wording is #796's)
  4. Day 2, clear: "He's wanted my ditch since the day I cut it. Now he'll go at her. Look to them."
  5. Day 2, fading: "...boot." (P, not picked: S33)
- **Threads:** T1 (he is it), T4.

### The widow (name O)
- **Who:** the sheriff's wife; the claim and its creek right are hers now; two young sons; a dog,
  Blue. Refuses to sell (D). No romance with Harlan, ever (D, 2026-11-03).
- **How she talks:** straight and short. Asks hard questions without softening them. Grief comes out
  as work: the stock, the ditch, the gate. Dry when she's easy; cold when she isn't.
- **Calls Harlan:** "Mr. Harlan", always. To the boys: "the deputy".
- **Knows:** B1, by noon, who brought her husband in, or that a freighter did (as the town tells it:
  the truth of "left him" can reach her later, D). B3, his last words: she's in the room. B4, the
  Baron's offer, from his own mouth. From B6, the stakes at her ditch and the Baron's man watching.
  She never knows more than "a red shirt" about the killer unless Harlan's deeds tell her.
- **Lines:**
  1. "Were you with him?" (Sean's beat, 2026-11-09)
  2. "Did he suffer? Don't dress it up for me."
  3. "He'd want it used." (the rifle; an earlier session's line)
  4. "My husband cut that ditch with a spade and a borrowed mule. That man can offer till he's
     hoarse."
  5. "They know your voice now. Call them, and they'll come."
- **Threads:** T4, T1.

### The doctor ("Doc"; name O)
- **Who:** the town's one doctor, an educated man gone west (from where: O). Maybe the county coroner
  (C12 a). "Knows more about the sheriff's wound than he says" (DESIGN.md; what that is: s19).
- **How he talks:** brisk, whole sentences, no slang, dry. Counts things: hours, beds, pints. Never
  says all he knows, and never raises his voice.
- **Calls Harlan:** "Harlan". In front of the town, after B3, "Deputy".
- **Knows:** B1, that the sheriff will die (D, inventory), and that Harlan has packed wounds before
  (built). B3, the last words. B5, what was done at the camp: Joe is in his office. That the law
  won't punish it (D). B12, that he can't take them all, and what Rourke's paper is for.
- **Lines:**
  1. "Somebody packed that wound, and packed it right. That's bought him some hours. Come back
     tonight." (built)
  2. "He's lost more than he can spare. I'll do what I can. It may not be much." (built)
  3. "Bring him in if you like. I never yet saw a white man hang on an Indian's word."
  4. "I can take four tonight. Not fourteen. Sign his paper, Harlan."
  5. "You've done this before. Where? ... No. Keep it."
- **Threads:** T1, T7, T8.

### Joe (his own name O: see "Names")
- **Who:** a Southern Ute man; his wife was raped, murdered and scalped in the raid at B2 while the
  men were hunting (D). Harlan's one full companion (D). Later "the man with plans" (P, #738). Why he
  knows timber and trains is O (#738).
- **How he talks:** English is his third language, after Ute and Spanish (`WORDS.md`). So it's
  exact and plain, with few contractions: not broken, not grand. Says a thing once. Long quiet. Dry
  humour. His questions are tests. With his own people he speaks Ute, and those lines aren't written
  here: they need a speaker.
- **Calls Harlan:** "Harlan". After an arrest ending, "Deputy", when he means the badge.
- **Knows:** nothing of B2 until he rides in from the hunt. From the survivors: a faded red shirt,
  and which way they went. He doesn't know that man shot the sheriff unless the player's deeds show
  it. In the arrest ending he learns of the release when the town does. B13: he asks Harlan what the
  captain's day means for his people (D).
- **Lines:**
  1. "Are you going to stop me?" (Sean's beat)
  2. "Is that a yes?" (Sean)
  3. "That was mine to take." (Sean)
  4. "A hat and a rag at my neck, and I am nobody's Indian. Only one more man who shot at them."
  5. "A log that size will stop them. If it swings when it should." (after #738's example)
- **Threads:** T2 (he is it), T4, T7.

### The Baron (name O)
- **Who:** the valley's cattle Baron; a masonry town house on the rise west of town (D, #700; brick
  or cut stone, Italianate, by #666) and a ranch to the south-east. Wants every drop of water, "and is patient" (DESIGN.md).
  His hired guns do the work "while the Baron keeps his hands clean" (D, 2026-11-11).
- **How he talks:** educated, courteous, unhurried. Never a threat in words: only prices, and
  regrets. Long sentences where everyone else is short. Doesn't swear. Remembers names.
- **Calls Harlan:** "Mr. Harlan". "Deputy" when it's useful to remind him who signs warrants.
- **Knows:** everything his men do, from B0. What he doesn't know: what the sheriff said on the road,
  and whatever is in the boot.
- **Lines:**
  1. "A hard day, ma'am. When that place grows too much for you, come to me first. You'll not find a
     fairer figure." (the funeral: the offer is Sean's, the words aren't)
  2. "I have never taken a thing in this valley that I did not first offer to buy."
  3. "Men call it patience. It is only arithmetic."
  4. "You were a medical man, Mr. Harlan. You know the look of a thing that can't be saved."
  5. "You've brought half my wall in with you, Deputy. You may as well have a chair."
- **Threads:** T4 (he is it), T5, T1.

### The Baron's foreman (name O)
- **Who:** the man who pays. He walks up the widow's lane with the deed (D, v2) and paid the two who
  pulled the rail (D, v2).
- **How he talks:** a cowman's flat, friendly, practical talk. Smiles when he says "Deputy".
- **Calls Harlan:** "Deputy"; "friend".
- **Knows:** every payment and every errand. Not what the ledger says: that's the bookkeeper's (P).
- **Lines:**
  1. "She puts her name to it, we ride off, nobody's hurt. That's the whole of it, Deputy."
  2. "He don't ask twice. He just asks different."
  3. "Two lengths and go home. Not a spike more." (to the men at the rail; overheard)
  4. "I pay on the first. What a man does with it is his own lookout."
  5. "I ride for the brand. Same as you ride for that badge."
- **Threads:** T4, T5.

### Silas Kett (the red shirt)
- **Who:** the Baron's hired gun (D). Shot the sheriff (D, 2026-11-09). Led the raid; killed Joe's
  wife (D). A coward (D). The town knows him only as "the red shirt" until someone names him (D,
  2026-11-11; who: O).
- **How he talks:** easy, friendly, a drummer's patter. Wheedles when cornered, and bargains with
  what he knows.
- **Calls Harlan:** "Deputy", and makes it sound like a joke.
- **Knows:** all of B0 and B2, and who pays. Not the Army's part.
- **Lines:**
  1. "Evening, Sheriff. Am I right for the ford?" (B0; nobody alive hears it but him)
  2. "I'm done! I ain't heeled! You're law, ain't you? Then take me in!"
  3. "It weren't only me. Ask them. It weren't only me."
  4. "You'll want to hear who sent me, Deputy, before that Indian opens my throat."
  5. "Morning, Deputy. Fine day to be at liberty." (Main Street, the arrest ending)
- **Threads:** T1, T2.

### Cody
- **Who:** one of the Baron's hired guns, in Kett's crew (D, 2026-11-11); "some rough asshole"
  (Sean). Kills Earl at B2 in front of the saloon. Arrested later (C6); the witness (D).
- **How he talks:** loud and short. Brave with drink in him and a crew behind him. In the cell:
  sullen, then frightened, then bargaining.
- **Calls Harlan:** nothing, at B2: he doesn't see him. Later "nester" (to check, `WORDS.md`), then
  "Deputy" when he needs him.
- **Knows:** who shot the sheriff and who raided the camp (D, 2026-11-11), and who pays. At B2 he
  already knows the sheriff is down for good, which is his boast.
- **Lines:**
  1. "Earl. You been dealing off the bottom of that deck." (built)
  2. "Ain't no law left in this valley to save you now, Earl." (built)
  3. "They're coming tonight. Thing is, I can't tell you what for."
  4. "You're the law, ain't you? Then it's on you to keep me breathing."
  5. "I'll say who paid for the sheriff. Not here. Not where he can reach."
- **Threads:** T1, T7, the ending.

### The stranger (who he is: C10)
- **Who:** sits down at Harlan's table at B2 and holds him in his chair. He knew it was coming.
- **How he talks:** calm, low, educated. (Sean wanted his voice "more educated": from the master
  chat, not in the repo; to confirm.) Friendly the way a man is who can afford it.
- **Calls Harlan:** "friend".
- **Lines:**
  1. "Evening. Mind if I sit in a while?" (built)
  2. "Don't move, friend. Whatever happens next, you sit still." (built)
  3. "You didn't see a thing tonight. Nobody did. Remember that." (built)
  4. "Put it up. You'll only get yourself killed." (built)
  5. "You're the man with the spring above the ford. I had hoped we'd meet in better company." (only
     if C10 a)
- **Threads:** T1; T4 if he's the Baron or his man.

### The barkeep (the saloon's owner; name O)
- **Who:** "hears everything, sells it to whoever pays" (DESIGN.md). His first words over a dead man
  are about his chair (D, 2026-11-09).
- **How he talks:** aggrieved, quick, always counting what a thing costs him. Calls every man by his
  name: he knows them all.
- **Calls Harlan:** "Harlan"; "Deputy" once it's worth something.
- **Knows:** who drinks with whom; Cody by name; the payroll's day (D, v2). What he was paid not to
  see.
- **Lines:**
  1. "Jesus, Cody, my chair!" (built)
  2. "Somebody ought to fetch the... hell. Ain't nobody left to fetch." (built)
  3. "Payroll's on Thursday's train. Now, wasn't that worth the price of a drink?"
  4. "I see a deal less than folks think, Deputy. It's cost me, too."
  5. "Lord God. My floor."
- **Threads:** T7, T5, T6.

### Rourke (rank O)
- **Who:** an Army officer the Baron pays to bring soldiers (D, 2026-11-09). Seen on the platform in
  the arrest ending; arrives on Payday Night with a surgeon and a letter (D). Harlan's old commander
  (P, #546, not accepted: C15).
- **How he talks:** parade-ground courtesy. Whole sentences. "Order", "regular", "duty". Speaks of
  the town as a thing to be put right. Never raises his voice; has other men for that.
- **Calls Harlan:** "Deputy". (If C15 a: "Sergeant", in private. A hospital steward was called
  "Steward", so which word is its own small call.)
- **Knows:** the whole plan. That the night would need a surgeon, before it did (the letter, D v6).
- **Lines:**
  1. "My surgeon is at your service, Deputy. I ask only that the thing be done regularly."
  2. "It is a small matter to write your name. Men are dying while you read."
  3. "A town that cannot keep its own peace will have it kept."
  4. "The captain will report what he sees. I have every confidence in what he will see."
  5. "Do your duty, Sergeant." (P, #546 P4)
- **Threads:** T5 (he is it), T8.

### The captain (I Am the Law; name O)
- **Who:** sent by his department commander to see for himself before troops are committed (D,
  2026-11-09). A person under pressure: the Baron's man courts him at supper. Whether he's Rourke's
  own man or independent of him is O on the card.
- **How he talks:** dry and exact. Asks, and writes the answer down. Gives no opinions: they go in
  the wire.
- **Calls Harlan:** "Deputy".
- **Knows:** the telegrams that brought him; Rourke's letter, if signed. After that, only what he
  sees and hears that one day.
- **Lines:**
  1. "I'm sent to see, Deputy. Not to be told."
  2. "Who fired that?"
  3. "How long have you held that badge? And who swore you?"
  4. The wire, a quiet day: "SALT CREEK QUIET. CIVIL OFFICER PRESENT AND SUFFICIENT. SEE NO CAUSE FOR
     TROOPS."
  5. The wire, a violent day: "DISORDER GENERAL. CIVIL AUTHORITY UNEQUAL TO IT. RECOMMEND TROOPS."
- **Threads:** T5, T7.

### The Kid
- **Who:** the Colters' youngest, the timid one (built: the three gunmen). On the porch at the
  farmhouse (D, v2; C5). The one who knows who pays the gang (D, 2026-11-09). Drinking under the cut
  on Payday Night (D, v6). Wants out (P, DESIGN.md).
- **How he talks:** quick, half sentences, eyes on the door. Polite out of fear.
- **Calls Harlan:** "mister"; "sir" when it's bad.
- **Knows:** who pays Brody. Which jobs were the Baron's.
- **Lines:**
  1. "I never fired a shot at that house, mister. I held the horses is all."
  2. "It ain't Brody pays us. It's the man that pays Brody."
  3. "They don't let you quit. Lyle said it laughing, but he said it."
  4. "If I tell you, I'm dead in this valley. You got to see that."
  5. "Is it true you can sew a man up?"
- **Threads:** T6, T4.

### The miner (name O)
- **Who:** a hard-rock man at the silver mine (D, 2026-11-04: silver). Trapped, or digging, in Buried
  Alive; gives Harlan single sticks and then teaches him (D). May pitch the coach trap if Joe is dead
  (P). Knows how to dig and prop for the Sap (D pick).
- **How he talks:** slow, exact, a teacher's patience. A miner's words for everything (`WORDS.md`).
  Maybe Cornish (O).
- **Calls Harlan:** "Deputy".
- **Knows:** the mine and its timber. What the owner saves money on (that the owner pulled supports
  on purpose is **not** approved: Sean's inventory).
- **Lines:**
  1. "Powder don't forgive and it don't hurry. Nor should you."
  2. "Half a stick for a door. A stick for a wall. A bundle when you want the hill to come too."
  3. "Hear that? That's the timber talking. When it stops talking, you run."
  4. "Cut your fuse long. A man's legs are shorter than he thinks."
  5. "Fire in the hole!" (to check: `WORDS.md`)
- **Threads:** T3.

**Joe's name is different.** "Joe" is what the town calls him, which is how it went: the Southern Ute

speaker or the tribe's own culture and language people, not from us. Until then he is Joe, and the
camp's lines are written as what they mean.
