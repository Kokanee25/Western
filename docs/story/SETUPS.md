# Setups and payoffs

The story bible, part 2 of 5 (#809). Planning only. Every piece of foreshadowing: what it is, where
it's planted, where it pays off, which thread. Status words as in `OUTLINE.md`: **D** Sean's, **P**
proposed by Sean or an earlier session, **P·bible** this bible's own (Sean hasn't seen it), **O** open.
A setup's status is the status of its weakest end: a decided plant with a proposed payoff is P.

## The rules

1. **The order is open, so a setup that matters is planted where every player passes, or in two or
   three places** (#809). Every player passes: the sheriff on the road, day 2 at the doctor's (he's
   sworn in either way), No Justice's cabin, Hell on Wheels into Payday Night into I Am the Law, the
   trestle, the raid. Nothing else is safe: a player can skip the saloon, the funeral, the widow.
2. **Nothing is paid off that wasn't set up, and every setup pays off.** The ones that fail this are
   in "Missing" at the end.
3. **A plant is something in the world** (a line, an object, a man's habit, a place), never a
   caption. A second plant is a different kind of thing from the first: if the first is a line, the
   second is an object or a witness.
4. **A script is checked against this file** (#807): each line that plants or pays something names
   its S-number in the script's notes.

**S50. Earl at the doctor's door** · T7, T1 · D (Sean, 2026-11-17, #1056: "Harlan has to carry Earl … to the
doctor's and bang on the door"; the doctor "says, more or less, that this man needs a grave, not a

- **The door.** The doctor is up with the sheriff and slow to come ("Who is it? I've a man dying in
  here."). He opens, looks once: "The man wants burying, not doctoring." (Sean's wording) Earl is laid in the
  front room under a sheet. Payoff, next morning, by the body: "He was dead when you knocked. I'd not
  have you think it was the door." Which is how Harlan knows the doctor has thought about it.
- **The count.** The doctor counts what Harlan carries: "That's two you've carried to this door
  today." If Harlan left the sheriff on the road: "This one you carried."
- **The coat.** Earl's blood is on Harlan's coat in the morning. The doctor: "The sheriff's not been
  told about Earl. Button your coat." The sheriff, if he sees it: "Whose blood is that on you? ...
  That ain't mine.", and the doctor lies to a dying man. In the street: "That his blood on you?";
  "Cold water, Mr. Harlan, not hot." Later (P·bible): the widow at the grave sees the coat is clean or
  isn't; Joe's leg bleeds on the same coat.
- Later still (P·bible): this is the first man Harlan fails to save in front of the town; Payday
  Night's triage is the answer to it.
