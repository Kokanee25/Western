### C1. The payroll is saved: does the Baron's outrage still happen, and how?

Left to this bible by Sean (#744, 2026-11-11): "show 2–3 versions in context". The versions are
written out in `OUTLINE.md`, Act III. Since he asked, he has also decided that the floor falls on
Payday Night whatever happened on the train (#809, 2026-11-11), which answers half of it.

- **a. Two barrels:** the Baron planned the wreck and the floor for the same day. Saving the pay
  changes nothing for him.
- **b. "The deputy stole it":** the pay is saved but comes up short, and the Baron's men say the
  deputy took it (Sean's own twist). The floor still falls.
- **c. It really worked:** saving the pay beat him, and the floor is him trying something else in a
  hurry that afternoon, which is why it leaves traces.

Leans c: it's the only one where the player's best mission changes anything, and the hurry explains
Sean's "one or two hard-to-find ways" to learn of it. b can be added to either later.

### C2. The payroll is lost, or the train is wrecked: what is Payday Night then?

Sean (#809, 2026-11-11): Payday Night "follows the payroll being saved"; he's "pulled in there to
celebrate saving the pay". But Hell on Wheels is decided by physics ("too late and it derails for
real", D), and the Payday card says the crowd comes either way: "a celebration, a hungry night, or a
funeral" (D, 2026-11-09).

- **a. It can't be lost and go on:** a wreck with Harlan aboard kills him (he wakes at the doctor's,
  the day replayed), and missing the train altogether isn't possible once he's heard of it.
- **b. A hungry night:** no pay, an angry crowd in the same room, and the floor still falls. He's
  there as the deputy who failed, not the guest of honour.
- **c. The wreck was the outrage:** no dance. Rourke comes for the wreck's wounded with the same
  letter, and the sawn girder waits, found or not, for another crowd.

### C3. Payday Night: if he finds out, can he stop it? And what of version 6 stays?

Sean left this to the bible (#809, 2026-11-11): "an event that should happen"; "1 or 2 hard to find
ways to find out"; he's "an observer" and "can even be a victim but hopefully not die". The card
Sean adopted two days earlier (v6, "Yeah, took it all") has nine ways, several of which prevent
the collapse, and "if the floor holds, they leave town".

- **a. He can blunt it, not stop it:** the girder is already sawn and the band's piano is already up
  there (P·bible). Knowing lets him get some people off the floor, stand clear, send Joe to the
  stair, have the doctor's made ready. Fewer fall, Rourke's offer is smaller, and Harlan has proof
  from before the fact for the captain next day. v6's triage, the letter and "the proof fights the
  letter" all stay; its ways to prevent become ways to thin.
- **b. He can only be ready:** nobody believes the deputy on payday. Knowing changes where he
  stands and what he has to hand, and nothing in the room.
- **c. He can stop the floor, and the outrage comes another way that night** (a shooting in the
  street). The floor then isn't "an event that happens".

Leans a. **The two hard-to-find ways** this bible places (`SETUPS.md`, S23): the storekeeper's
day-book (a handsaw sold that day to a man he didn't know), and the pale fresh cut seen from below
with a lamp, off the main path. Both are from v6. v6's other traces (sawdust on a coat, the woman
across the street) would make it easy to find, so they go, or become things people only say
afterwards.

### C4. Why did the Baron's men raid Joe's camp?

No source says. Kett's crew are the Baron's hired guns and they raided the camp on night one (D,
2026-11-11). Every line Kett, Cody, Joe and the Baron speak about it depends on the reason.

- **a. Provocation:** the Baron wants a Ute "outbreak" for his telegrams (D, 2026-11-11: "telegrams
  claiming the town or the Ute camp has risen up"; I Am the Law's planted Ute saddle). The raid was
  the first try. Then Joe's revenge is what the Baron wanted, and Joe's cowboy disguise is what
  spoils it.
- **b. Kett off the leash:** the Baron ordered the sheriff shot and nothing else. Kett's crew did
  the camp on their own, drunk on having killed the law. It's the crime that ties the Baron to
  something he never wanted, and Cody can say so.
- **c. The old scalp-hunters' trade** (DESIGN.md's first version; the No Justice card lists it as O:
  "kept, or dropped with the third ending?").

Leans a: it puts Joe's thread inside the Baron's plan from the first night. b is the more human
Baron. Either way it's handled as No Justice is: nothing shown, only what it left.

### C5. Who holds the widow's farm, and who does the Baron have left after the cabin?

Sean (#744, 2026-11-11): the red shirt's gang "raid Joe's camp, hold the farm and do his dirty
work"; the Colters are separate. The farmhouse card (v2, Sean 2026-11-09, and the built mission):
"the Colters on the Baron's pay hold the house, the Kid on the porch", with Brody, Lyle, Dutch,
Hollis, Pike and Wes. And No Justice ends with five or six of Kett's crew beaten at the cabin, yet
the Baron's men go on to saw a girder, pull a rail, goad Joe and come for Cody.

- **a. The Baron has more guns than the cabin held.** The cabin was Kett's own bunch; the rest work
  under the foreman and hold the farm. The Kid and the Colters aren't there, so the Kid needs
  another way into the story, and the built crew is renamed.
- **b. The cabin finished Kett's crew, so the Baron hires the Colters for the farm** ("sometimes
  work for him"). The built mission stands as it is, the Kid is on the porch, and after it the
  Colters cross him (see s6).
- **c. Both:** the foreman and two of his own in the house, the Colters hired for the yard.

Leans b: nothing built changes, and it makes Sean's "sometimes cross the Baron and sometimes work
for him" a story, not a label.

### C6. How and when is Cody arrested?

D (2026-11-11): he's arrested "at some point after Harlan becomes deputy"; O: "how and when". On the
cards (P): he rides with the crew and is one of the six at the cabin.

- **a. At the cabin, in No Justice:** he's one of the men there; taken alive if the player takes him

  player who kills everyone at the cabin has no witness.
- **b. Back in the saloon:** Cody walks in again, as bold as before, after the funeral. Same room,

- **c. On a warrant:** the coroner's inquest on Earl names him, and bringing him in is a small
  mission of its own (the Bounty's shape).

Leans b for the picture; a is cheapest. They can both be true (b if he wasn't at the cabin).

### C8. Who lets Kett go, and by what right?

D (2026-11-09): the prisoner leaves on the train under escort, is released, and comes back; the man
on the platform who lets him go is "Rourke, or his officer". O on the card: who "the proper
authorities" are. And the Army couldn't hold or free a town's prisoner: that is the same law the
whole plot turns on.

- **a. It's a federal matter:** a white man's crime against a Ute woman on the reservation went to
  the United States court, not the county (to check: *United States v. McBratney*, 1881, left that
  with the federal side). He goes by train to the U.S. commissioner under an Army escort from Fort
  Lewis, and is turned loose for want of a witness the court will hear. Then Rourke is already
  posted nearby in Act I, and the Payday card's "on their way to their new post" changes.
- **b. The county lets him go,** and Rourke only stands on the platform beside him, shaking his
  hand. A sighting, not an act.
- **c. Nobody lets him go:** he slips his escort on the train with help, and Rourke's officer looks
  the other way.

### C10. Who is the stranger at the table, and who sends Harlan to the saloon?

Built (No Law): "Don't move, friend. Whatever happens next, you sit still." and "You didn't see a

and #810 leaves "someone to meet in the saloon" for this bible to place.

- **b. He's the Baron's foreman.** Then the man at the table is the man who walks up the widow's

Who sends Harlan there: the stranger's own invitation (a, b), or the doctor, who tells him to come

Leans a: Sean took the judge's "Baron met early" as the offer at the funeral (2026-11-09); this puts

### C11. The trestle: if the train carries soldiers under orders, what has Harlan done?

D: a violent day in I Am the Law brings "soldiers under orders" on the last train; the climax is
"blowing the bridge with the Baron's soldiers on the train"; then "a quiet epilogue". Rourke's fate
is O. Killing United States soldiers under lawful orders isn't something a town walks quietly past.

- **a. They're the Baron's men whatever the wire said.** The wire changes how many, how they're
  armed and what the paper calls them, not who dies. Rourke and a few bought men are the only
  uniforms.
- **b. On that branch they're real soldiers,** blowing the trestle is the dark ending, and the paper
  and the walk judge Harlan for it.
- **c. On that branch there's another way:** the papers or the captain's own eyes turn the soldiers
  (P, #546 P4), and blowing it is a choice.

Rourke: dies on the trestle, is taken with the Baron, or rides away to be named in the paper.

### C12. Is he a sheriff, and what is Harlan after he dies?

D (2026-11-09): Harlan is "temporarily appointed by the dead sheriff" and stays a deputy. O: what
"temporarily" ends with. A sheriff was a county officer; a Colorado law of 1877 has the **coroner**
act as sheriff when a county has none (to check in the 1877 General Laws). And the P plan for Cody
(shipped to the county seat's court) needs Salt Creek not to be the seat.

- **a. County sheriff, seat down the line.** He lived on his claim at Salt Creek. When he dies the
  coroner is acting sheriff, and in a town this size the coroner is the doctor: so the doctor is the
  one who keeps Harlan on, and "temporarily" means until the county commissioners name a man (whom
  the Baron will try to choose).
- **b. Salt Creek is the county seat.** The court and the judge are here. No transfer, and the Water
  Tower night loses its reason.
- **c. He was the town marshal, and everyone says "sheriff".** Simplest; least true.

### C13. How hard do the worst men's words go?

`WORDS.md` needs a rule before any line is voiced. The period's words for Joe's people, the Chinese
workers and Mexican ranchers were ugly and common.

- **a. None.** Contempt is carried by "your Indian", "that Ute", "them", and by what men do.
- **b. A few, from the worst men only** (Kett, Cody, the mob's loudest), at moments that matter, and
  never from the journal, the paper or anyone the game wants liked.
- **c. As the period had them.**

The voice service and the store page are part of this (some lines may not be voiceable).
