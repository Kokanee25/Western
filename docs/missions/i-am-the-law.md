## How you hear of it

- DECIDED: the day after Payday Night (Rourke arrives on that train, DECIDED), a captain gets down at the
  depot with a notebook and an orderly. His department commander has sent him to see for himself before
  troops are committed (the governor has asked; the Army decides: history correction).
- DECIDED: the depot agent has the wire and tells the deputy; the town knows by noon; Joe asks Harlan
  what it means for his people.

## The situation

- DECIDED: the captain walks the town on his own errands (depot, store, saloon, jail, church, street).
  He sees by the light and hears through walls like anyone, and his report is the tally of what he
  actually witnessed.
- DECIDED (judge's idea 1): **he's a person under pressure.** The Baron's man courts him over supper at
  the hotel; he can be offered money or shown things; that evening he wires his report from the depot,
  and Harlan can read it there, so the verdict is a sentence the player sees the same day (and hears
  again at the finale).
- DECIDED (judge's idea 2): **the town testifies.** He asks the storekeeper, the barkeep and the doctor
  about the law here, and their opinion of Harlan goes into his report: what Harlan did before counts,
  not only this day. (Needs story state and long memory to carry; on the day, their current opinion.)
- DECIDED: the Baron's men have the day to give him a revolt: a man shoved until guns are drawn, a shot
  at dusk (no fist fights exist: shoves and drawn guns, build check), and an Indian seen fighting white
  men: they'll goad Joe.
- DECIDED (judge's idea 3): **a staged outbreak the place shows.** Before he rides the East Road, the
  Baron's men leave a stolen Ute saddle and a torn-down fence for him to find: the fear of Ute outbreaks
  after 1879 made real. Harlan can find it first, move it, or show who put it there. Handled with care:
  it's the Baron's lie about Joe's people, and the mission treats it as one.

## The ways to do it

- DECIDED (Sean, 2026-11-09, from Payday Night's judge): **the proof fights the letter**: walk the
  captain to the sawn joist (in the floor or the rubble) or bring him to the witness or the storekeeper's
  day-book; what he sees for himself weighs against Rourke's letter in his tally.

- DECIDED: **head trouble off quietly**: overhear the plan through a wall, find the men first, and arrest
  them out of his sight (in the dark between lamps, behind the livery: "Drop it!" in the dark works
  today).
- DECIDED: **steer him**: walk with him and choose where he looks; he has his own agenda, and he goes to
  a shot.
- DECIDED: **let him see the truth**: put him where he watches the Baron's man start it; his report says
  "provocateurs", but it still counts.
- DECIDED: **deal with the plant**: find the saddle and fence before he does.
- DECIDED: **do nothing**: the Baron's show goes in the report.
- CUT (judge's tick, Sean 2026-11-09): getting him drunk.

## The big choice and its cost

- DECIDED: **Joe.** Ask him to ride out for the day (the nod) and he goes, remembering that the deputy hid
  him the way the law hid his wife's killer; keep him beside you and he's the target; let him choose and
  he stays, in plain sight.
- DECIDED: **the truth or the quiet**: arrests out of sight keep the report clean but are their own lie;
  letting him see the provocation is honest and still counts against the town.
- DECIDED: **the captain's price**: what the Baron offers him, and whether Harlan can match it with
  anything but the truth.

## What happens after, in the world

- DECIDED: **the report shapes the finale** by its tally: a quiet day and Rourke's men come on the last
  train as hired guns with no orders; a violent day and they come as soldiers under orders. The Bridge Is
  Out weighs differently by what the captain saw.
- DECIDED: Joe remembers how he was asked or protected; the men arrested fill the jail (`hold-him`); the
  town remembers who kept the peace.

## True to 1882

- DECIDED (history corrections): the Posse Comitatus Act (1878); Colorado a state from 1876; a governor
  calls out the militia first, and federal troops come on a request to the President through the War
  Department, so the captain is sent by his department commander. The Ute removal of 1881 was of the
  White River and Uncompahgre Utes; the Southern Utes stayed in southwest Colorado. The fear of an
  outbreak after 1879 is the Baron's tool.
