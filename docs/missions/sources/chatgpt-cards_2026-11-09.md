SALT CREEK — MISSION CARDS FOR JUDGE
Working draft · November 9, 2026 · 1882
Reading rules: Every card is DRAFT until approved. ESTABLISHED identifies something already specified in
the mission brief or past Salt Creek discussions. PROPOSED identifies a new connective or gameplay suggestion;
it is not canon. OPEN means deliberately undecided. All times are estimates, not promises.
Reference: Attach the approved No Justice card separately. It is not rewritten here. The two train titles have not
been confirmed against Sean’s original titled notes: the mapping below is PROVISIONAL, even where individual
mechanics are established. Each mission must continue if an important character, prop, or document is lost. Player
action advances story threads; the daily train continues its ordinary schedule, but unresolved mission scenes do not
expire on a hidden timer.




1. THE HANGING
1. Title, status, length
  • The Hanging — DRAFT — 15–25 minutes. Its core setup and fatal rope shot are ESTABLISHED
    in the current brief.

2. Premise
  • A crowd tries to hang Joe in Salt Creek. The mob’s reason depends on how No Justice ended: if Harlan
    arrested the red-shirted killer, Joe later killed that man after his release; if Harlan allowed revenge at the
    cabin, the dead bandits’ kin have come for Joe. Harlan has a narrow chance to intervene from a high position
    with his Winchester 1873.

3. How you hear of it
  • A frightened townsman runs to Harlan; a crowd is shouting near the hanging place; the commotion carries
    through the street. No quest marker.
  • If Harlan turns away, the mob continues. Joe can die. The event becomes an unresolved murder and a
    permanent loss, not a mission reset.
  • PROPOSED: The crowd gathers when Harlan next returns to the town centre after the relevant No Justice
    consequence, not at an arbitrary real-time deadline.

4. Where on the map
  • Existing Salt Creek main street and an adjoining yard or square, exact hanging place OPEN.
  • A usable high spot: PROPOSED hotel balcony, upper saloon room, or stable loft. Clear sightline to rope;
    bad angles from street.
  • Roofs, balcony railings, hanging platform and nearby cover should support climbing, gunfire and falls.

5. The beats, numbered
  1. The crowd: Harlan sees Joe restrained and the mob already committed.
       • Choices: climb for a shot; move through the crowd; try to break it up; leave.
       • Purpose: Put Harlan’s reputation and Joe’s life in conflict immediately.
  2. The high position: The player can take a sightline with the Winchester; a moving body and rope make the
     shot demanding.
       • Choices: aim for the rope; pressure the ringleader from the roof; approach closer at greater risk.
       • Purpose: Make height and precision matter, rather than play a cutscene.
  3. The hanging: The rope tightens. A clean shot through the rope frees Joe. A missed rope shot means
     Joe dies, as specified; do not secretly correct the bullet.
       • Choices: take the shot; try another kind of intervention before the drop; refuse to intervene.
       • Purpose: Deliver a consequential, physical outcome.


                                                         1
  4. The street afterward: The crowd reacts to whom Harlan shot, whether Joe lived and whether anyone else
     was hurt.
       • Choices: arrest the ringleader; let the mob scatter; pursue someone fleeing; tend to Joe if alive.
       • Purpose: Turn a single rescue into a public political event.

6. The ways to do it
  • Precision: Reach the high spot and shoot the rope with the Winchester.
  • Confrontation: Enter the crowd and attempt arrest, intimidation or a close intervention. This is riskier and
    can become a street fight.
  • Abandonment: Walk away. Joe dies and later content adapts.
  • The intended image is the rope shot; alternatives must still work without promising a safe rescue.

7. The big choice
  • Protect Joe in public and risk alienating townspeople or starting bloodshed; uphold a questionable mob’s
    demand and permanently abandon Joe.
  • Made through movement, aiming, firing and possible surrender/arrest gestures. A nod to the ringleader may
    be used if a close conversation happens (PROPOSED); no menu, no morality meter.
  • Even a successful rescue may kill or injure bystanders if Harlan fires recklessly.

8. When it goes wrong: the mission bends, it doesn’t break
  • Key person dies early: Joe’s death is permanent. If the mob leader dies first, other mob members may
    flee, fight or continue; their unity is not tied to one immortal NPC.
  • Companion is hit: Joe is already restrained. A wound can make his rescue more urgent; if he survives, he
    needs treatment. Other companion assumptions do not override his possible scripted-world death here.
  • Player flees or gives up: Joe is hanged; witnesses remember Harlan leaving.
  • Unthinkable: Harlan shoots Joe, helps the mob, or kills surrendered townspeople. The town responds as it
    would to a public killing; there is no invisible prohibition.

9. After: the world remembers
  • Joe saved, mob restrained: Witnesses spread the story. Southern Ute contacts may trust Harlan more;
    some townspeople call him brave, others say he protects a murderer. Joe remains companion, but his legal
    trouble may persist. The ringleader can face custody or later retaliation.
  • Joe saved, bystanders harmed: Newspaper or rumours focus on collateral deaths; family members remem-
    ber. The lawman’s legitimacy falls even if Joe lives.
  • Joe dies: His community learns from witnesses or Harlan; his camp and future companion scenes change.
    Harlan rides without him. This is not a generic game-over screen.
  • Harlan sides with mob: Some hostile townspeople approve, others fear an untrustworthy sheriff. Southern
    Ute relations worsen. The deed can surface again at an inquest or final reckoning (PROPOSED).
  • Who returns: mob survivors, witnesses, Joe if alive, and bereaved relatives if not.

10. Where it leads
  • Direct consequence of No Justice. Changes Joe’s participation in The Farmhouse, The Baron’s Iron
    Coach, The Raid on the Baron’s House and Hell on Wheels.
  • PROPOSED: The public shooting can feed The Inquest if that concept is later approved.

11. Characters
  • Joe: targeted widower; recognizable cowboy disguise and worn hat; existing character.
  • Harlan: sheriff; Winchester 1873 central to the scene.
  • Mob leader: PROPOSED NEW NPC with distinctive work coat and rope or noose; wants revenge or
    punishment.
  • Several townspeople: witnesses, participants or objectors; use existing town population where possible.
  • No Justice victim’s kin: PROPOSED NEW NPCs, or existing outlaws reused; identities OPEN.

12. Systems it needs
  • Winchester aiming and ballistics; severable rope and hanging physics (NEW); crowd AI, witness/rumour prop-
    agation; climbing and height; arrests; wounds and persistent bodies; reactive companion continuity; optional
    nod.
  • NEW: Permanent Joe-death branch and robust dependent mission variants.

13. Keep these details
  • The high-position Winchester rope shot.
  • Two different causes of the hanging based on No Justice.
  • A miss can kill Joe, permanently.

14. True to 1882
  • Winchester 1873 and informal mob violence fit the period, but cutting a loaded rope with a rifle is highly
    improbable and must feel extremely diﬀicult, not like a routine trick shot.
  • Colorado is already a state in 1882; county jurisdiction, lawful arrest authority and how a sheriff would respond
    to a lynch mob need research.
  • The danger to a Southern Ute man should be grounded in local prejudice and personal responsibility, not
    staged as anonymous spectacle. Research local Southern Ute history and historical treatment of accused
    Indigenous people.

15. Design notes
  • This is a short, public consequence mission, unlike No Justice’s remote camp fight.
  • The dramatic high-shot set piece is deliberate; the outcome is not guaranteed by a cutscene.
  • Large development risk: Joe’s death must have meaningful downstream handling, or this mission violates the
    promise of consequence.

16. Still open
  • Exact hanging site and route to the high position.
  • Whether close-range intervention can save Joe once the drop begins; a missed rope shot must still kill
    him.
  • Identities of mob members and whether the town newspaper exists.
  • Whether Joe can be arrested rather than hanged if Harlan intervenes early.




2. THE BARON’S IRON COACH
1. Title, status, length
  • The Baron’s Iron Coach — DRAFT — 25–40 minutes. Armoured coach, rolling log, and document
    choices are ESTABLISHED from the current brief.

2. Premise
  • The Baron moves valuable papers in a privately protected, iron-plated horse-drawn coach. Harlan wants the
    documents, but the coach is diﬀicult to stop and the people around it may be innocent hired workers. The
    papers may later support the Baron’s arrest—or be sold back, used for blackmail or destroyed.

3. How you hear of it
  • PROPOSED: A stable worker notices a coach being fitted with iron plates, or a trusted contact tells Harlan
    when it will leave. Another route is seeing the coach pass through Salt Creek.



                                                         3
  • If ignored, the coach continues along its normal journey. Its papers change location; their loss does not block
    the Baron thread.
  • Repeated coach travel is PROPOSED, not an already approved daily schedule.

4. Where on the map
  • Existing Baron ranch and town house, plus the road between them; exact road and destination OPEN.
  • PROPOSED NEW MICRO-LOCATION: A steep cut in the road with a bank above it, reachable on
    foot or horseback.
  • Height matters: look down from the bank, roll a heavy log onto the coach, or shoot from above. The coach
    may overturn down a short slope.

5. The beats, numbered
  1. The opportunity: Harlan learns the coach will move; its route can be watched in the world.
       • Choices: follow, get ahead, ask questions, ignore.
       • Purpose: Reward preparation without adding an objective marker.
  2. The road: The coach passes below a steep bank where a large log could be dislodged.
       • Choices: use the log; arrange a roadblock; approach as a rider; follow until it stops.
       • Purpose: Show a powerful environmental solution.
  3. The interception: The log can strike, dent and overturn the plates; horses, escorts and driver react inde-
     pendently.
       • Choices: protect the driver; fight escorts; demand surrender; search the wreck; disengage.
       • Purpose: Turn armour from an arbitrary health bar into physical material.
  4. The papers: Harlan finds the locked container, or the papers are carried away by a fleeing survivor.
       • Choices: take, copy or hide papers; sell them back; threaten the Baron; burn them.
       • Purpose: Make the loot a political choice rather than a standard collectible.

6. The ways to do it
  • Environmental ambush: Push/release the large log from above. Fast, spectacular, loud, and dangerous for
    noncombatants.
  • Quiet theft: Shadow the coach, use its rest stop or destination, and steal the strongbox without overturning
    it (PROPOSED stop site).
  • Direct interception: Stop the horses and demand surrender; risk armed resistance.
  • Walk away: The papers remain in circulation, allowing later leads.

7. The big choice
  • What to do with the papers: hold as evidence, use as leverage, sell back, or destroy.
  • Made through physical handling of the papers and later conversations; a nod may confirm a proposed bargain.
  • Holding evidence risks retaliation and implicates witnesses; blackmail compromises Harlan; sale enriches him
    but protects the Baron; destruction removes evidence and may keep sensitive people out of danger. No safe
    option.

8. When it goes wrong: the mission bends, it doesn’t break
  • Key person dies early: A driver or guard can die, surrender, flee or be hurt in the rollover. If the courier
    holding papers dies, his body or overturned coach can be searched; if the container burns, alternate Baron
    evidence is needed later.
  • Companion is hit: Joe can be downed and treated if alive; with Joe absent/dead, Harlan acts alone.
  • Player flees or gives up: The coach continues or survivors recover the documents. The Baron strengthens
    security; another route to proof exists.
  • Unthinkable: Harlan burns the coach with occupied seats, murders surrendering guards, sells papers to the
    Baron or attacks the horses. Witnesses and property evidence drive serious consequences. No soft reset.

  • Papers kept: Baron suspects Harlan; guards and surviving drivers identify the robbery. Town oﬀicials may
    later recognize the evidence if it proves genuine.
  • Papers sold back or used for blackmail: The Baron knows Harlan is compromised and can threaten
    exposure. A witness or record of the exchange may follow Harlan into Arresting the Baron.
  • Papers burned: The Baron may be relieved, but burned wreckage and missing documents leave rumours.
    The later arrest requires testimony or a separate proof route.
  • Bloody ambush: Drivers’ families, road users and townspeople react; employees may refuse to cooperate
    even if the Baron is hated.
  • Clean theft: Less immediate violence, but the Baron quietly increases security and searches for an inside

  • Who returns: coach crew, a surviving escort, possibly an informed stable worker; the Baron reacts in his

  • Supports The Raid on the Baron’s House and Arresting the Baron; may help explain Rourke’s con-

11. Characters
  • Baron: land and cattle power; usually offscreen in this mission unless Sean decides otherwise.
  • Coach driver: PROPOSED NEW NPC, distinctive leather driving coat and whip, wants to survive and
    keep his job.
  • Escort/guard(s): PROPOSED NEW NPCs or reuse the Baron’s men; rifles and recognizable livery or
    armbands PROPOSED.
  • Joe: possible companion; retains protective instincts toward bystanders.

12. Systems it needs
  • Horse-drawn vehicles; horses and harness; road following; armour dents (NEW); heavy rolling log and gravity;
    collision/overturn; deformable or breakable coach panels (NEW); surrender/arrest; papers with persistent

  • Dynamite is not required and should not be introduced here ahead of Buried Alive.

13. Keep these details
  • The huge log released from above the road.
  • Iron plates that dent and a coach that physically overturns.
  • Documents can be evidence, blackmail, returned for money, or destroyed.

14. True to 1882
  • A bespoke plated coach is an unusual, costly private modification, not a standard frontier vehicle; verify
    construction, weight, horse load and door access rather than assuming modern armour.
  • Land conveyances, deeds and business ledgers belong in the period. Document authenticity and chain of
    custody would matter if Harlan later seeks prosecution.
  • Rolling timber can crush a driver or injure horses; stage the human and animal risks honestly.

15. Design notes
  • Physical spectacle first, then an evidence decision. Avoid turning the documents into an invulnerable mission
    key.
  • Unlike The Bridge Is Out, the best solution can be a quiet theft with no gunfight.
  • The exact contents of the papers are intentionally OPEN.

3. THE BRIDGE IS OUT
1. Title, status, length
  • The Bridge Is Out — DRAFT — 35–50 minutes. PROVISIONAL TITLE-TO-PLOT MATCH:
    This card uses the previously approved Runaway Payroll Train design, because Sean’s original notes under
    the new title were not available.

2. Premise
  • Two gangs attack a working train connected to the gold mine’s money. One gang damages the railway west of
    Salt Creek; another takes control aboard the east-to-west train. Harlan must stop the train before it reaches
    the broken line, while deciding how much risk to accept for the people and valuables aboard.

3. How you hear of it
  • Track workers or a rider bring news of damage, or Harlan encounters armed men near the western line. The
    normal train’s sound and passage establish that a train is still due.
  • If Harlan ignores the damage or abandons the chase, the train may reach the hazard; casualties and economic
    damage persist.
  • The train runs on a daily world schedule. The specific mission incident should enter its crisis state through
    observable player/world events, without a hidden countdown for merely postponing the lead.

4. Where on the map
  • Existing western line beyond the Ore Works (sabotage site); Salt Creek depot; the large canyon trestle; the
    small eastern wagon-road railway overpass (boarding point); six traversable train cars and locomotive.
  • Established east-to-west rail order: eastern entry → small overpass → canyon trestle → depot → Ore Works
    → western exit.
  • Height: jump from overpass to roof; fight on roofs; fall through or off carriages; see the train crossing the
    great trestle, which remains intact for the finale.

5. The beats, numbered
  1. The broken line: Harlan arrives at the sabotage scene and fights or evades the first gang.
       • Choices: engage, scare off, arrest survivors, inspect damage or retreat.
       • Purpose: Let Harlan understand the danger without a quest marker.
  2. The chase east: Harlan rides to intercept the train upstream of the break.
       • Choices: use the small overpass, attempt horseback boarding, signal the crew (PROPOSED), or aban-
          don the chase.
       • Purpose: Make known geography pay off.
  3. Boarding: A forgiving but missable leap from overpass to train roof; horseback boarding remains an alter-
     native.
       • Choices: board from above, from side, or use signalling to seek help.
       • Purpose: Deliver a physical, memorable train entry.
  4. Car-to-car fight: Harlan moves from rear toward engine through six cars. Bandits use crates, narrow doors
     and roofs.
       • Choices: fight through, sneak past, surrender/capture individual attackers, move over roofs, protect
          workers.

       • Purpose: Turn the train itself into changing terrain.
  5. Stop the train: The normal controls or crew may be compromised.
       • Choices: use locomotive brakes, handbrakes or crew assistance where physically plausible; separate cars
         if supported (PROPOSED).
       • Purpose: Resolve danger by interacting with machinery, not killing one boss.

6. The ways to do it
  • Fast action: Intercept from overpass, fight across roofs and cars, seize controls.
  • Lower-profile: Signal or contact crew, board from a quieter position, move through cars with fewer fights
    and protect civilians.
  • Partial rescue: Stop only some cars or warn workers if full braking fails. PROPOSED and dependent on
    real train physics.
  • Do nothing: The railway suffers a collision or wreck; the story continues with damage.

7. The big choice
  • Prioritize reaching the engine quickly, leaving threatened passengers/workers or valuables unsecured; or slow
    down to help people while the danger ahead increases.
  • The choice is made through physical route and rescue actions, not a presented menu.
  • OPEN: Whether the cargo is mined gold travelling outward or money/pay packets travelling to miners. The
    final stakes change with this answer.

8. When it goes wrong: the mission bends, it doesn’t break
  • Key person dies early: If the engineer or gang leader dies, train systems and other gang members continue;
    surviving crew may help. If no one can operate controls, Harlan must use machinery or settle for a partial
    rescue.
  • Companion is hit: Joe can be downed/revived if present; if Joe died during The Hanging, use a solo
    version.
  • Player flees or gives up: The train wrecks or is stopped by somebody else at a cost. The destroyed track
    and casualties persist.
  • Unthinkable: Harlan shoots surrendered bandits or innocent crew, steals the money, destroys a car, or
    deliberately speeds the train toward the break. Witnesses and survivor accounts shape later responses.

9. After: the world remembers
  • Train saved, workers protected: railroad workers, passengers and the depot spread Harlan’s reputation.
    The mine thanks him, though the money’s owner may still question missing funds.
  • Train saved, valuables stolen: payroll shortages or missing gold cause anger, hardship and investigation.
    Harlan may profit but lose trust.
  • Partial wreck: Injured passengers, closed track, damaged cars and disrupted supplies appear in the world.
    The Ore Works responds to the interruption.
  • Full wreck or abandonment: Deaths and physical wreckage stay until cleared through visible work; the
    railroad may blame Harlan for not acting. Baron and mine interests exploit the disaster differently, responses
    OPEN.
  • Who returns: surviving conductor/engineer, railway employees, the two gangs’ survivors, injured people
    and families.

10. Where it leads
  • Makes the railway familiar for Hell on Wheels. Establishes small-overpass boarding and the great trestle
    before the finale.
  • Gives a mechanical basis for Woman Tied to the Tracks if that mission survives review.
  • A Payday Night celebration after rescue has been proposed but not locked.

4. HELL ON WHEELS
1. Title, status, length
  • Hell on Wheels — DRAFT — 30–45 minutes. PROVISIONAL TITLE-TO-PLOT MATCH:
    This card uses the established large canyon trestle finale with soldiers on a train. The title assignment is
    OPEN.




                                                        8
2. Premise
  • Near the end of the story, the Baron has called in Army oﬀicer Rourke and his soldiers. Harlan’s planned
    response is to destroy the large railway trestle as the military train crosses it. The action can stop the
    reinforcements but risks killing many people and permanently cutting the railway.

3. How you hear of it
  • Reports of troops moving by rail, a military telegram seen or heard at the depot (PROPOSED), and
    conversations with people who saw the preparations. No quest marker.
  • If ignored, the train and troops enter the valley. The balance of power changes; the story does not freeze at
    the bridge.

4. Where on the map
  • Existing large canyon trestle east of Salt Creek, between the small overpass and depot; canyon floor and
    approaches on both sides; train and surrounding ridge.
  • Height: exposed trestle deck, supporting timbers, cliff vantage points and deep fall into canyon.
  • PROPOSED NEW MICRO-LOCATIONS: hillside approach, watch position and possible path down to
    bridge supports. Do not add another bridge.

5. The beats, numbered
  1. The threat: Evidence confirms that Rourke’s soldiers are approaching by rail.
       • Choices: prepare bridge, warn townspeople, act elsewhere, ignore.
       • Purpose: Make demolition a consequential response to an escalating threat.
  2. The trestle: Harlan reaches the known structure; guards or scouts may be present.
       • Choices: approach quietly, confront defenders, reach supports from above or below.
       • Purpose: Pay off the bridge’s repeated appearances earlier in the game.
  3. Preparation: Harlan places or triggers charges using demolition abilities learned after Buried Alive.
       • Choices: select supports, use fewer/more charges, retreat to a vantage point, abort.
       • Purpose: Express the player’s learned destruction skill.
  4. The crossing: The troop train enters the span, if not already stopped or diverted.
       • Choices: detonate with soldiers aboard; warn/stop the train and risk troops reaching the town; wait and
           destroy an empty bridge at greater strategic cost.
       • Purpose: Give spectacle a moral weight.
  5. Collapse and escape: Damaged beams lose support; train load accelerates failure. Loose timber and cars
     fall into the canyon.
       • Choices: move to safety, rescue someone stranded if feasible, finish without further killing.
       • Purpose: Make the game’s physical bridge destruction a story climax.

6. The ways to do it
  • Stealth demolition: Approach supports unseen and place charges before the train arrives.
  • Open battle: Fight or drive off guards and demolish under fire.
  • Refusal or alternative: Disrupt the soldiers without detonating under the train, or let it cross and face the
    troops later. The immediate threat worsens but the world must continue.
  • The central intended set piece remains the bridge blowing with soldiers aboard; this does not make every
    other player action impossible.

7. The big choice
  • Blow a bridge occupied by soldiers to save Salt Creek from intervention; or spare the train now and allow a
    larger threat to enter the valley. Also consider the cost to railway workers and townspeople who depend on
    the line.
  • Made through charge placement, signalling and the physical act of detonation; an optional nod to Joe could
    confirm his understanding but must not substitute for control of explosives.
  • Neither path is presented as morally correct.

8. When it goes wrong: the mission bends, it doesn’t break
  • Key person dies early: Killing a guard or oﬀicer changes defenders, not the train’s ability to move. If

  • Companion is hit: Joe may be downed/revived; if dead since The Hanging, Harlan undertakes the finale

7. ARRESTING THE BARON
1. Title, status, length
  • Arresting the Baron — DRAFT — 15–30 minutes. A near-end attempt to arrest him is ESTAB-

2. Premise
  • Harlan reaches the point where he can move publicly against the most powerful landholder in the valley.

3. How you hear of it
  • PROPOSED: A clerk, judge, witnesses or the Baron himself responds to Harlan’s accumulated case; a public

    move. This is a state change, not a countdown.

5. The beats, numbered
  1. The case: Harlan confronts the strength or weakness of his evidence.
      • Choices: use iron-coach documents; bring witnesses; rely on direct crimes; abandon legal case; privately
         bargain.
      • Purpose: Make earlier choices matter.
  2. The approach: Harlan finds the Baron among guards and workers.
      • Choices: announce arrest publicly, demand surrender quietly, infiltrate, ambush or walk away.
      • Purpose: Give the Baron human and political shields, not extra hit points.
  3. The confrontation: Baron argues, bargains, flees, orders resistance or surrenders according to the situation.

      • Purpose: Let power, evidence and violence collide.
  4. The town’s judgment: News of arrest, escape or death reaches Salt Creek.
      • Choices: lock prisoner up, share evidence, suppress it, allow transfer or resist outside interference.
      • Purpose: Close a central thread without pretending the whole valley agrees.
