SALT CREEK — Complete Mission Inventory

November 9, 2026 · For Claude Judge review

I've recovered our earlier mission discussions, including some ideas we haven't revisited recently.

There are 10 major story missions or mission concepts we've approved or established, plus the opening sequence, the demolition progression, and several proposed missions that haven't been approved.

I'm separating them into three categories so Claude's Judge doesn't mistake a brainstorm for established story canon.

PART 1 — Established and approved missions

1. Opening — The Wounded Sheriff

Established story direction

* Thomas Harlan wakes at his homestead.
* He discovers the sheriff has been shot.
* The player decides whether to help transport him to the doctor or leave him, using head-shake controls.
* Introduces the player to Salt Creek, its people and the consequences of decisions.

2. The Saloon Shooting

Established story direction

* Harlan visits the saloon while the sheriff is incapacitated.
* A bold cowboy accuses another man of cheating at cards.
* He kicks a chair aside and creates a confrontation.
* He declares that there is no law in Salt Creek and shoots the accused cheater.
* Introduces the player's first major encounter with the town's lawlessness and our environmental combat systems.

3. A New Sheriff

Established story direction

* The doctor informs Harlan that the wounded sheriff will die.
* Harlan inherits the sheriff's gun and becomes the town's new lawman.
* Establishes Harlan's responsibility for the town and the central tension between law and personal morality.
* The sheriff's widow and his murder become important later in the story.

Note: These first three are currently story beats. They could become one larger opening mission rather than three separate missions.

4. No Justice

Approved · Refined November 9

* Harlan learns that Joe's wife was murdered and scalped during a raid on a Southern Ute camp.
* The doctor and bold cowboy warn that the killers will likely escape meaningful punishment.
* Harlan and Joe visit the camp, where survivors recount the attack and identify the main killer by his distinctive red shirt.
* Joe puts on ordinary cowboy clothing to make it harder for the killers to trace him to his community.
* Harlan and Joe ride to the outlaws' remote camp and fight the gang.
* The red-shirted murderer retreats into a building, where he surrenders unarmed.
* Joe draws his knife and asks Harlan whether he intends to stop him.
* Nod yes: Stop Joe, arrest the murderer, who is later released by the authorities.
* Shake no: Allow Joe to kill and scalp the murderer.
* Establishes Joe as Harlan's principal companion.

5. Buried Alive

Approved

* A collapse traps miners underground.
* Harlan enters the mine to rescue survivors.
* Introduces an underground rescue sequence with failing supports, blocked passages and environmental danger.
* A knowledgeable miner becomes important to the mission.
* Introduces the player to dynamite through a practical rescue problem.
* Creates an opportunity to show the human cost of Salt Creek's mining industry.

Development note: Earlier proposals included the mine owner knowingly removing supports, but that particular conspiracy has not been approved.

6. Demolition Mentor / Explosives Training

Approved progression

* Following Buried Alive, an experienced miner teaches Harlan more sophisticated demolition methods.
* Progression includes basic dynamite, half-sticks, linked charges and larger explosive bundles.
* More advanced techniques provide new options during later missions.
* Relevant to the farmhouse rescue, balcony ambush and trestle finale.
* Training should expand the player's capabilities rather than artificially lock missions behind upgrades.

This may be a series of optional activities instead of a dedicated story mission.

7. Hostage at the Farmhouse

Approved · Prototype developed

* Bandits take the widow hostage at her homestead east of Salt Creek.
* The property sits beside the creek, north of the railway.
* Approximately four or five enemies occupy the farmhouse.
* The front approach is dangerous and heavily covered.
* The player can assault the front or circle around the property.
* A preferred creative solution is placing dynamite against the rear wall, blowing a hole in the building and entering through it.
* A stealth approach remains possible if the gang hasn't spotted Harlan.
* The player must defeat the captors and rescue the widow.
* The environment should react to the damage rather than relying on a single scripted breach.

The widow's children may also feature, alongside Blue, the family animal, but the precise family setup remains unresolved.

8. Balcony Ambush

Approved concept

* Harlan encounters armed opponents positioned on an elevated wooden balcony.
* Instead of directly shooting every enemy, the player can destroy the balcony's support posts.
* The structure collapses, bringing the attackers down.
* A demonstration of systemic destruction creating a tactical alternative to ordinary gunplay.
* The precise story setup, antagonist and location have not been finalized.

9. Runaway Payroll Train

Approved · Version A selected

One of our most developed action missions.

* Two outlaw gangs are involved in an attempted payroll train robbery.
* Gang B sabotages a stretch of track to the west.
* Harlan fights the saboteurs, then races east to catch the approaching train.
* Gang A has boarded the train and taken control.
* The preferred boarding method is jumping from the small wagon-road overpass onto the train roof, with horseback boarding as an alternative.
* The player fights through six walkable railcars, progressing from the rear toward the locomotive.
* The player must reach the controls and stop the train before it reaches the destroyed track.
* The mission makes use of moving-train physics, couplers, destructible objects and combat in confined spaces.

Important continuity: The small wooden overpass is the boarding location. It is not the large canyon trestle destroyed in the finale.

10. Woman Tied to the Tracks

Approved direction

* A woman is tied to the railway as part of an outlaw trap.
* The approaching train creates a time-sensitive rescue.
* Harlan must stop or divert the train before it reaches her.
* Boarding the train and applying the brakes is one planned solution.
* The attackers use the situation to ambush Harlan, potentially from elevated positions.
* Builds on the railway mechanics established during the payroll train mission.

The exact method of stopping the train, the woman's identity and the reason she was targeted remain open.

11. The Baron's Shipment

Locked mission

* Harlan learns that the Baron is moving a guarded convoy through the valley.
* The shipment contains deeds, financial ledgers and evidence of the Baron's land theft and payments to Army officer Rourke.
* Harlan and Joe plan a disguised robbery to obtain the evidence.
* Potential approaches include impersonation, disguises, a prepared ambush or direct interception.
* The objective is to recover the documents, not simply eliminate the guards.
* The operation can succeed cleanly, create substantial trouble, or result in only partial evidence being recovered.
* Connects the Baron's criminal operations to Rourke and the larger conspiracy.

12. The Trestle Finale

Established finale

* The conflict with Army officer Rourke and the Baron culminates at the large canyon railway trestle.
* Harlan must destroy the bridge while a train is crossing.
* The player applies demolition techniques learned earlier.
* Explosives damage the timber structure, triggering structural failure and progressive collapse.
* The crossing train contributes dynamic loads to the weakened bridge.
* The destruction is a major gameplay and cinematic payoff for the game's physics systems.

Still open: The full approach to the bridge, the precise train contents, Joe's involvement and the final confrontation with Rourke.

PART 2 — Proposed missions requiring approval

These have been discussed seriously enough to retain, but we have not finalized their complete designs.

| Mission | Current concept | Status |
|---|---|---|
| 13. Payday Night | After the payroll train incident, Salt Creek celebrates. Harlan drinks with townspeople. A serious event interrupts the evening, potentially Rourke's arrival or a shooting. | Direction discussed |
| 14. The Inquest | A formal inquiry into a suspicious death. Harlan examines testimony and physical evidence to determine what actually happened. | Proposed |
| 15. The Wrong Ball | Medical evidence reveals that a victim was shot with a different calibre or from a different position than witnesses claimed. Could implicate a balcony shooter. | Proposed |
| 16. Dig Him Up | An exhumation reveals evidence that changes the understanding of a previous murder. Could connect to the inquest. | Proposed |
| 17. Hold the Jail | Harlan must defend the jail against people attempting to free or kill a prisoner. | Brainstorm |
| 18. Bring Him In Breathing | A law-enforcement mission focused on capturing a dangerous suspect alive rather than killing him. | Brainstorm |
| 19. The Fire | A town fire creates a rescue and environmental destruction scenario. | Brainstorm |
| 20. Dry Creek | A water-related dispute or emergency that uses the valley's creek and water systems. | Brainstorm |

I think Payday Night, The Inquest, The Wrong Ball and Dig Him Up are worth reviewing together because they may form a single connected story arc rather than four independent missions.

Other proposed set pieces

These haven't been developed into full mission outlines.

* Water diversion: Manipulate a dam gate, creek, irrigation channel or mining sluice to change the environment.
* Mine pumping: Use operating machinery and water systems during an emergency.
* Burning livery: A fire involving the town's stables, with opportunities for rescue and destruction.
* Grave digging: Recover a body or buried evidence.
* Early train encounter: Harlan travels under the small railway overpass during one or two early missions, establishing the landmark before the payroll train sequence.

PART 5 — What Claude's Judge should specifically examine

Our mission planning has some substantial gaps. I would direct the Judge to review these, rather than simply score which missions sound entertaining.

| Review area | Main question |
|---|---|
| Story cohesion | Do the missions form one believable story, rather than a collection of Western set pieces? |
| Escalation | Do the Baron and Rourke become threatening gradually enough? |
| Sheriff's role | Are there enough ordinary law-enforcement missions to make Harlan feel like a sheriff? |
| Joe's development | Does he receive enough meaningful development after No Justice? |
| Player agency | Are alternative solutions supported by real systems rather than scripted choices? |
| World reuse | Do the same places and NPCs change as the story progresses? |
| Pacing | Are there sufficient quiet missions between major gunfights? |
| Mechanics | Does each major mission showcase a distinct mechanic without feeling like a tutorial? |
| Consequences | Do decisions produce believable later outcomes? |
| Feasibility | Can the missions work within our small-team, AI-assisted Godot development approach? |

Important continuity constraints for the Judge

The Judge should preserve our existing decisions: Joe is the only full companion; gunfights generally involve small numbers of lethal enemies; dynamite is introduced in Buried Alive, not the opening; the small overpass serves early railway encounters and train boarding; and the large canyon trestle is reserved for the finale.

It should also flag the unresolved family storyline. We established that the widow is the murdered sheriff's wife and that his murder is connected to her. We subsequently considered making her and the children Harlan's own family instead. That potential change must be resolved before the Judge treats their relationships as settled.

My biggest concern is the middle of the game. We have several excellent individual missions, but not enough connecting story to explain why the Baron, the sheriff's murder, Rourke, Joe, and the railway conspiracy all belong to the same narrative.

I'd have Claude's Judge evaluate the eight approved standalone mission concepts first, then identify which proposed missions would strengthen the story and which would merely extend its length.
