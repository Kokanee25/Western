SALT CREEK — NO LAW

1. Title, status, length

Title: No Law
Status: DRAFT — based on established story decisions, with proposed details identified.
Length: 20–30 minutes of active play, spread over the opening days.

Type: Opening town introduction, witnessed murder, character development and funeral.

2. Premise

The sheriff has been shot, leaving Salt Creek without effective law enforcement. Harlan, still unarmed, witnesses a brutal saloon murder committed by a man in a red shirt who believes nobody can stop him.

The following day, the sheriff dies and passes his revolver to Harlan. His death brings Harlan into the lives of the sheriff's family and introduces the responsibility that will define the game.

The red-shirted murderer is also responsible for the rape and murder of Joe's wife. Harlan does not yet know this.

3. How you hear of it

* This mission follows the opening sequence in which Harlan encounters the wounded sheriff.
* Harlan arrives in Salt Creek and can explore the main street, meet residents and enter businesses.
* He encounters a stranger in the saloon and begins talking with him.
* During this visit, the red-shirted man enters and confronts a card player.
* The next day's events come through the doctor or someone sent to find Harlan.
* The funeral is announced by townspeople, the church or the sheriff's family.

If ignored: The player can avoid the saloon. The shooting still happens and becomes town news, but Harlan misses witnessing it firsthand. The sheriff's death still occurs; Harlan learns about it when he next encounters the doctor or townspeople.

The story does not depend on a countdown or quest marker.

4. Where on the map

Salt Creek Main Street

* Introduces the shops, residents and town layout.
* No new location required.

Main saloon

* The murder takes place at a card table.
* The player can observe from the bar, another table or near the entrance.
* The upper floor, if accessible, could offer another viewing position.
* Existing saloon interior.

Doctor's office

* Harlan visits the dying sheriff.
* Meets the sheriff's wife and potentially their children.
* Existing planned location.

Church and cemetery

* Funeral gathering and burial.
* PROPOSED: Cemetery slightly elevated above town, with a view toward the valley.
* Final cemetery location remains open.

5. The beats

Beat 1 — Salt Creek without a sheriff

* Harlan enters town on the first day following the sheriff's shooting.
* Townspeople discuss what happened.
* Some are worried. Others behave more boldly because the sheriff is incapacitated.
* Harlan can explore businesses and speak with residents.
* He eventually enters the saloon.

Player choices:

* Explore town before entering.
* Speak with different people.
* Go directly to the saloon or visit elsewhere first.

Purpose: Introduce the town as a place with ordinary people, routines and problems before the player becomes responsible for it.

Beat 2 — A stranger in the saloon

* Harlan begins talking with a stranger.
* The saloon is operating normally, with drinking, conversation and a card game.
* The stranger provides a natural opportunity to introduce Salt Creek.
* The red-shirted man enters.
* He notices a card player he believes has been cheating.

Player choices:

* Listen to the conversation.
* Watch the card table.
* Move around the saloon or leave.

Purpose: Establish an ordinary social situation before violence interrupts it.

Beat 3 — The murder

* The red-shirted man approaches the card player.
* He suddenly kicks the chair out from beneath him.
* The player falls to the floor.
* The red-shirted man draws his revolver and shoots him.
* He makes a remark about there being no law to save him now.
* The room falls quiet.
* Nobody immediately challenges him.
* The murderer leaves the saloon.

Harlan has not yet received the sheriff's gun. Normally, he can only watch.

Player choices:

* Stand back and witness the shooting.
* Attempt to intervene physically, at significant risk.
* Take cover or retreat.
* Try to assist the victim afterward.

The scene should be extremely quick. It is not designed as a fair fight.

Purpose: Show the consequences of the sheriff's absence. Introduce a memorable villain without revealing his later importance.

Beat 4 — The aftermath

* The card player's body remains.
* Patrons react differently: some leave, some attend to the victim and others act as though nothing unusual happened.
* The stranger reacts to what Harlan witnessed.
* News spreads through town.
* The murderer is not immediately arrested.

Player choices:

* Help with the aftermath.
* Speak to witnesses.
* Leave without getting involved.

Purpose: Show that violence affects people and that the world remembers it.

Beat 5 — The sheriff's final day

* The following day, Harlan learns that the sheriff is dying.
* He visits the doctor's office.
* The sheriff's wife is present.
* PROPOSED: Their children are also present, allowing Harlan to meet them early.
* The sheriff and Harlan share a final moment.
* The sheriff gives Harlan his revolver.
* The sheriff dies.

Player choices:

* Speak with the sheriff and family.
* Respond through the nod when the sheriff asks Harlan to accept responsibility.
* Stay with the family or leave after the death.

Purpose: Transfer responsibility from the dying sheriff to Harlan. Introduce the family as people the player will encounter repeatedly.

Beat 6 — The funeral

* The sheriff's body is prepared for burial.
* Town residents gather for a funeral.
* His wife and possibly children attend.
* The doctor, townspeople and other familiar characters are present.
* The service is quiet rather than a major cinematic spectacle.
* Harlan sees how many people depended on the sheriff.
* After the burial, people return to their ordinary lives.

Player choices:

* Attend the service.
* Speak with the family afterward.
* Observe from a distance.
* Miss the funeral entirely.

Purpose: Make the sheriff's death matter beyond the acquisition of a weapon. Establish the widow and her family before later threats against them.

6. The ways to do it

This mission is mostly observational on purpose.

Approach A — Become involved

* Explore town and meet residents.
* Witness the saloon shooting.
* Attempt to help the victim.
* Visit the dying sheriff.
* Attend the funeral and speak with the family.

Approach B — Remain distant

* Avoid the saloon confrontation or flee.
* Hear about the murder from others.
* Limit contact with the sheriff's family.
* Miss the funeral.
* Begin Harlan's story as someone reluctant to become involved.

The major events remain, but relationships and what Harlan personally witnesses differ.

7. The big choice

This mission does not need a dramatic moral decision comparable to No Justice.

Its central question is whether Harlan accepts the responsibility left by the dying sheriff.

* Nod yes: Harlan accepts the responsibility. The family and doctor see his willingness to step forward.
* Shake no: Harlan expresses reluctance. The sheriff still gives him the revolver, but the family and doctor remember his refusal.

Cost either way: Accepting draws Harlan into the town's problems; refusing leaves those problems unresolved and risks losing people's trust.

No morality meter or displayed judgment.

The transfer of formal legal authority is a separate historical and story question.

8. When it goes wrong: the mission bends, it doesn't break

A key person dies early

* If the red-shirted man is somehow killed during the saloon encounter, the town responds to his death. No Justice must adapt, since its principal murderer is already dead.
* If the card player escapes or survives, the incident still becomes a violent threat against the town.
* If the stranger dies, Harlan loses that relationship, but the story continues.
* The wounded sheriff's death is a foundational story event. The precise treatment of an unusual player-caused interruption requires a separate design decision.

The companion is hit

* Joe is not yet Harlan's companion. Not applicable.
* If the player is injured attempting to intervene, the doctor can treat him.

The player flees or gives up

* Leaving the saloon does not prevent the world from reacting to the shooting.
* Missing the sheriff's death means learning about it afterward, with the gun transferred through the doctor or family.
* Missing the funeral affects how certain residents perceive Harlan.

The player does the unthinkable

* If Harlan attacks innocents or causes major destruction, witnesses report it and townspeople remember.
* If Harlan manages to kill the red-shirted man, this becomes a substantial alternate story branch, not an immediate game over.
* The later No Justice mission must account for the man's death.

9. After: the world remembers

Harlan watches the murder

* Saloon patrons remember his presence.
* The murderer remains free and becomes a source of fear.
* The stranger may comment on what Harlan did or failed to do.
* Later, the revelation about Joe's wife changes how the player understands this scene.

Harlan tries to intervene

* Witnesses remember his courage or recklessness.
* The red-shirted man may recognize Harlan later.
* The town may treat Harlan with greater respect, even if he failed to save the victim.

Harlan avoids the incident

* Townspeople recount inconsistent versions of what happened.
* The victim remains dead or injured according to the simulation.
* Harlan must learn the murderer's identity through other people.

Harlan attends the funeral

* The sheriff's family recognizes his support.
* Townspeople see him participating in their community.
* This provides an early foundation for later relationships with the widow and children.

Harlan skips the funeral

* The family remembers his absence.
* Some townspeople may question whether he cares about Salt Creek.
* Relationships remain repairable through later actions.

Faction effects

* Town: The sheriff's death and saloon shooting increase uncertainty.
* Baron: His men may consider the town easier to influence; no direct involvement is established.
* Mine and Ore Works: Workers discuss the loss of law enforcement; no mandatory faction change.
* Southern Ute community: No direct involvement yet.
* Outlaws: The absence of a sheriff may embolden them.
* Railroad: No immediate gameplay change.

The murdered card player should receive a recorded death, burial or obituary instead of disappearing from the world.

10. Where it leads

No Justice

* The same red-shirted murderer is later identified as responsible for raping and murdering Joe's wife.
* The player recognizes him from the saloon.
* This creates a personal connection to Joe's revenge mission.
* If the murderer dies early, Joe's storyline needs an alternate route involving the surviving attackers and the consequences of the earlier death.

The Farmhouse

* Harlan meets the sheriff's widow before she later becomes a hostage.
* The family is familiar to the player rather than introduced only when threatened.

The Inquest / sheriff murder storyline

* The circumstances surrounding the sheriff's shooting remain available for later investigation.
* The red-shirted man's role in the sheriff's shooting is not established.

General town gameplay

* Harlan receives his first revolver.
* Townspeople begin looking to him for protection.
* The sheriff's grave becomes a persistent world location.

11. Characters

Thomas Harlan

* Player character.
* Initially unarmed.
* Receives the sheriff's Colt revolver.

Red-shirted murderer

* Distinctive red shirt; revolver; aggressive, confident behaviour.
* Murders the card player.
* Later revealed to have raped and murdered Joe's wife.
* Needs a memorable appearance, voice and animations.
* New major character model.

The stranger

* A man Harlan speaks with in the saloon.
* Introduces the atmosphere and social situation.
* Identity and later role are OPEN.

Card player

* Accused of cheating.
* Victim of the saloon murder.
* Whether he was genuinely cheating is OPEN.
* Can use a reusable town character model.

Wounded sheriff

* Older or experienced lawman with a recognizable revolver and badge.
* Dies the following day.
* Previously established character.

Sheriff's wife / widow

* Present at the doctor's office.
* Attends the funeral.
* Important recurring character.
* Clothing and exact appearance OPEN.

Children — PROPOSED

* The sheriff's children.
* Present at the doctor's office and funeral.
* Could be recurring town characters.
* Their family relationships must be confirmed.

Doctor

* Treats the sheriff.
* Provides continuity between the opening shooting, the death and the aftermath.

Funeral participants

* Town residents, a person conducting the service, and possibly an undertaker.
* Most can use existing characters.

12. Systems it needs

Existing or planned

* NPC conversations and daily routines.
* Gun drawing and firing.
* Physical bullet impacts and wounds.
* Ragdoll bodies and persistent deaths.
* Chair-kicking physics.
* Witnesses and rumours.
* Head-nod yes/no responses.
* Town NPC relationships.
* Character animation and crowd reactions.

New or requiring expansion

* Saloon card-game activity and interruption.
* NPC fear and reactions to public murder.
* Funeral gathering and burial.
* Persistent graves and obituary records.
* Remembered attendance and participation at social events.
* Deathbed transfer of an important item.
* Recognition of a recurring character across missions.

No dynamite, train mechanics or major destruction set pieces required.

13. Keep these details

1. The chair kick. The red-shirted man kicks the chair from beneath the card player before shooting him. It should feel sudden and physical.
2. The absence of law. His remark about there being no law to save the victim directly connects the murder to the wounded sheriff.
3. The funeral. Harlan sees the dead sheriff's family grieving and the town paying its respects before fully taking on the role of lawman.

The red-shirted man's return in No Justice is an essential continuity payoff.

14. True to 1882

* Public drunkenness, card games, revolvers and saloon violence fit the setting.
* The response to a murder should reflect the settlement's actual legal arrangements, not assume all Western towns lacked functioning law.
* A sheriff's death would not automatically grant his office to whoever inherited his gun. The legal process for a replacement needs research and an in-story explanation.
* Funeral customs, burial arrangements and the presence of an undertaker should reflect the town's size and local traditions.
* The rape and murder of Joe's wife should be handled through credible testimony and consequences, not visually exploited or made into spectacle.
* The Southern Ute characters and community should receive historically informed treatment and consultation.

15. Design notes

* This is an introduction to people and place, not an action showcase.
* The player should usually witness the saloon killing without being able to prevent it in time. Do not create an artificial invulnerable murderer or disable all player action.
* The red-shirted man must feel dangerous and memorable, but his later connection to Joe should remain unrevealed.
* The sheriff's death should be quiet and personal.
* The funeral should have ordinary details: residents arriving, removing hats, a child standing beside a parent, and the town slowly returning to work.
* Avoid turning the funeral into another gunfight. Its purpose is to let the sheriff's death register.
* The mission can finish without a formal completion screen, as normal town life resumes.
