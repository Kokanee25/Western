**The planning session's design for Payday Night (2026-11-09), revised again for Sean's "make me a 30/35
mission".** Everything is PROPOSED unless marked DECIDED. Versions 2 and 3 scored 27/35, version 4 29/35

**In one line:** the Baron wants an outrage on payday, so his men have sawn the dance floor's joists half
through and spend the evening buying stamping reels; the town leaves traces of it all afternoon; Harlan
can read them, slow the dance, thin it or clear it, and every way costs something; whenever there are
more wounded than the doctor can take, Rourke is there with an army surgeon and a letter already written
for the deputy to sign.

## How you hear of it

- **Payday is the town's night.** The payroll came on the noon train, and Rourke with it (DECIDED): he and
  an army surgeon are on their way to their new post at Fort Lewis, west of the valley, and stop over in
  Salt Creek for the night (the Baron's guests at the hotel). By dusk
  every man with wages is in the saloons; the barkeep wants the deputy on Main Street.
- **The sabotage leaves traces all afternoon**, for anyone, in the world and in people's memories:
  - the storekeeper sold a handsaw that morning to a man he didn't know (he says so when asked, or when
    he hears of a sawn joist);
  - a woman across the street saw two men go up the saloon's outside stair with a saw in the slack of the
    afternoon (a witness, by what she saw);
  - sawdust on the upstairs landing and in the cracks of the floor, under the dancers' boots;
  - from the bar below, the fresh cut shows pale in one joist;
  - **as the load climbs, plaster dust sifts from the ceiling onto the bar and into the drinks**, thicker
    where the cut is, right where the Kid sits (driven by the members' own load: the place pushes the
    sign at anyone below).
- **The building warns:** the floor creaks and bows harder as the load climbs (the creak and the bend
  come from the members' own load).
- **The crash** is heard across town; townsfolk run to it.
- **If Harlan is away**, the night still happens and the wounded are laid in the street; internal bleeding
  goes on for hours, so he can ride into the middle of it.

## The situation

- The spec saloon's upstairs room (built from data: light joists, one sawn half through); about **twelve**
  dancers and a fiddler upstairs, the bar full below. **The load paths decide** when and where it goes:
  each man's weight on the member under his feet, more while the fiddle plays and they dance.
- **The Baron's two men** are in the crowd, one with sawdust still on his coat. **Their routine is there to
  be read:** as the crowd peaks they keep buying the fiddler drinks and calling for stamping reels, and
  they keep near the outside stair, not under the floor. If the floor holds, they make the night bloody
  another way (a shooting in the packed street), because the Baron needs an outrage.
- **The Kid** (DECIDED: the informant) drinks at the bar, under the cut. If it goes, he may be under it.
- **Rourke** is at the hotel with the surgeon and two orderlies, the Baron's guests.
- One doctor.

## The ways to do it

Each runs on a system and none is sure.
1. **Follow the traces.** The saw, the witness, the sawdust, the man with sawdust on his coat: any one
   leads to the others. Take the two men before they act (arrest in the dark works today), or watch
   them. **Arresting them costs:** they've done nothing chargeable yet; their drinking friends push back,
   the payday crowd turns on the deputy who spoils its night (opinions, the escalation ladder), and if
   the case won't hold they walk free in the morning.
2. **Break their routine.** Watch who keeps buying the reels: stop the fiddler (he stops for the deputy),
   or stop the men buying them; the stamping stops and the load drops, for now. The crowd doesn't like
   it, and the two men know they've been read.
3. **Thin it.** Get the barkeep to call a free round downstairs: half the dancers come down. The floor
   carries the rest, or not.
4. **Clear it.** Order them down (the escalation ladder: a drunk payday crowd doesn't like being told).
   Done in time, nobody falls; the Baron's men turn to their street.
5. **Expose it.** Show the cut, the sawdust, the man: the town learns this was done to it, and the Baron's
   men learn you know, and act.
6. **Triage**, if it falls or the street turns bloody: body to body, a tourniquet, a dressing, a splint;
   leave the man who can wait; find the one who's quiet. Two hands; **the physiology decides who lives**;
   the wounds come from the fall, the timbers and the guns, never placed.
7. **Make others help:** shout a townsman to a casualty to hold pressure or drag him to the doctor's.

## The big choice and its cost

**A rule, not a branch:** whenever the wounded outnumber what the doctor can take, from the floor or the
street, Rourke comes with his surgeon and offers him for the deputy's signature on a letter: that the
town's law could not cope and asked the Army's help. The worse the night, the bigger the offer and the
harder to refuse.
- **Rourke's letter is already written** when he offers it: the hotel's paper, the ink long dry, dated that
  afternoon. A player who reads it before signing sees that Rourke expected the night.
- **Sign** and men live tonight who'd have died; Rourke holds a letter in the deputy's hand. It has no
  legal force (the President orders troops in, usually on a governor's request), but it's the political
  ammunition the Baron needs: it goes to the governor, and the captain who comes tomorrow (`i-am-the-law`)
  reads it.
- **The letter shows the same night:** the men the Army's surgeon saves owe the Army, not Harlan (their
  opinions credit whoever treated them), and tomorrow some of them speak for the soldiers to the captain.
- **Refuse** and the town keeps its law, and some of tonight's dead would have lived.
- **The Kid or the next man:** triage by need, or by what the living man knows.
- **Prevent it or let the proof happen:** the cleaner the night, the less anyone believes it was ever in
  danger; the Baron's men still need their outrage, and the street is where they'll look for it.

## What happens after, in the world

Carried by the systems (persistence, opinions, memory, wounds):
- **The people:** the dead buried; the maimed marked for good; the saved owing Harlan; the families
  remembering who he went to first; the witness and the storekeeper remembering what they told him.
- **The building:** the broken floor and its rubble stay, the sawn joist among them for anyone to find.
- **The letter:** if signed, it exists: Rourke has it, the captain counts it, the Baron uses it.
- **The soldiers** camp at the depot (no quartering in peacetime) and are seen in the street.
- **The Kid:** alive, he talks, in his own time; dead, that thread loses its voice.
- **Harlan's name:** the deputy is a steward; people bring him their wounds after this.

## True to 1882 (to check)

- Hospital stewards' work: tourniquets, splints, dressings (carbolic used unevenly in the Army in 1882); sorting the wounded by need was surgeons' practice since Larrey (Napoleonic); the Union's
  Letterman system organised ambulances and field hospitals.
- Army post surgeons often treated civilians in frontier towns. Fort Lewis was moved in 1880 to the La
  Plata valley in southwest Colorado; officers and surgeons travelled to their posts by rail and stage.
  (An Army officer would not be sent about a private railroad's payroll: that was the marshal's, the
  express company's or the railroad's own detectives' job, so Rourke is passing through.)
- Troops came into a state by the President's order, usually on a governor's or legislature's request,
  though RS §§5298–5299 let him act without one in some cases; Posse Comitatus (1878); no quartering in houses in peacetime. The deputy's letter
  is political, not legal.
- A dancing crowd loads a floor more than standing men (check the factor); a joist sawn half through loses
  most of its strength.

**Judge A's ideas:** 1. The proof of sabotage counts against the letter: in I Am the Law the captain can
be walked to the sawn joist or brought to the witness, and what he sees weighs against Rourke's letter. 2.
A face on the choice: the overrun doctor is the one who asks the deputy to sign, and his opinion of Harlan
follows whatever he does. 3. Joe as a second pair of eyes on the stair or the two men (a drunk crowd's
hostility to a Ute man makes where you put him a cost). **Cut:** the storekeeper's saw-sale trace.

**Judge B's ideas:** 1. Prepare for it: shout townsfolk to the doctor's before the crowd peaks; raising the
doctor's capacity can deny Rourke his offer, and the readiness is proof to the town. 2. Witnesses remember
the signing (or the refusal), and the town splits by what it saw. 3. The storekeeper's day-book records the
saw sale, readable where it lies: a second piece of evidence that outlasts the night. **Cut:** the Baron's
men's street shooting if the floor holds: they leave town and the outrage waits ("the story doesn't
advance until it should").

**1882:** in 1882 a surgeon's attendants were a hospital steward and privates detailed (the Hospital Corps
came in 1887); requests for troops went to the governor from the sheriff, the county or the legislature
(the request-based section is RS §5297). **Continuity:** the card says the payroll came safely on the noon
train, but Payday Night follows Hell on Wheels, whose payroll may be lost; and it calls Harlan "the deputy"
(Sean: deputized, temporarily; whether he's sheriff by Act III is OPEN).
