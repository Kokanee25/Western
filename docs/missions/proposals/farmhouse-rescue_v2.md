## How you hear of it

PROPOSED: four ways, none needing the others.
- **She doesn't come to town.** On her day (the store, church), she isn't there; the storekeeper says so.
- **Blue comes alone.** The dog runs into town and barks at people he knows. He can't be hurt, so he
  always arrives.
- **From the line.** The house is beside the railway: the daily train's crew sees armed men in her yard,
  and the depot agent hears it.
- **The saloon.** A rover drinks in town and talks; the saloon owner sells what he said.
- PROPOSED: the peaceful visit (lock) is where Harlan first learned the house, so on the day he knows the
  back door, the kitchen and the yard.

## The situation

- PROPOSED: **the Colters, on the Baron's pay**, hold the house (one answer to #622 item 7, the story
  judge's), the Kid among them on the porch (as built).
- PROPOSED: they aren't there to kill her: **the Baron's foreman is coming up the lane with a bill of
  sale**. She's to sign her water right over. The boys are held in the yard (the root cellar or the
  barn) by the yard men, placed from the start.
- PROPOSED, why her signature: in Colorado the first to put water to use owns the right ("first in time,
  first in right"), the rule the Colorado Supreme Court laid down in *Coffin v. Left Hand Ditch Co.* in
  1882. The sheriff's family dug their ditch first. The Baron can't take that right by buying land
  round it; he needs her name on the paper.

## The ways to do it

PROPOSED. The built ways stay; four more come from people already in the scene.
1. **The front** (built): they warn you off; a kill zone.
2. **The back, quietly** (built), and the silent takedowns Sean noted as an idea (#563).
3. **The wall** (built, after Buried Alive): plant a stick at the kitchen's back wall and hide; the wall
   going up is the first they know.
4. **The foreman.** Stop him on the lane before he reaches the house: no paper, no signing. Or walk him
   to the door at gunpoint: the door opens for the foreman.
5. **The Kid.** If Harlan has met him and he wants out (`kid-wants-out`), the boy on the porch can be
   talked down with a nod ("You with me?"), let Harlan in the back, or turn on the others. If not, he's
   one of the guns.
6. **Let her sign.** Wait. The foreman gets the signature, the gang rides off, and nobody is hurt. The
   Baron has the water.

## The big choice and its cost

PROPOSED: no clean answer.
- **Fight now** and her life is at risk (the guard's clock); **let her sign** and everyone lives, but the
  valley's best water is the Baron's.
- **The men who give up:** one taken to town alive is a witness to who sent them and a prisoner the gang
  will come for (`hold-him`); killing him is clean now and leaves nothing to prove.
- **The Kid:** spared and turned, he's the informant on who pays the gang (Payday Night, DECIDED); shot
  on the porch, that thread loses its voice.

## What happens after, in the world

PROPOSED: all by existing systems (opinions, memory, persistence).
- **The breach costs her home.** The blown wall stays; she and the boys live with the hole. Her opinion
  of Harlan holds what she saw: her door kicked in, her wall blown out, men taken quietly, or him
  standing by while she signed.
- **The paper.** If she signed, the Baron's men start his ditch from her creek frontage (`dry-creek`); if
  the paper burned or was taken, he has to try something louder (the spine: an outrage).
- **The prisoners** fill the jail (`hold-him`); **the Kid**, if turned, is in town and in danger.
- **The town** hears it from her at church or the store, in her words, by what she saw.

**What the references do that this doesn't:** Lady Boyle's gentle option carries its own dark cost;
here the lane ambush is cost-free. A Crack in the Slab shows the consequence happen; here the paper's
effect lands later, off-stage. The Showstopper lets you learn the key fact by overhearing on site.

**Beef it up:** 1. Take away the clean answer: a water deed had to be acknowledged before an officer (a
notary or justice of the peace) to be recorded, so the foreman rides up with a townsman Harlan knows;
stopping them costs that man's opinion and leaves a witness; if the foreman is overdue, the house's own
senses start the guard's clock. 2. Make the errand discoverable: the foreman seen in town having the
paper drawn up and asking the way (the saloon owner sells it); from the overpass, pen and ink set on
her table in the window and a saddled horse at the lane's foot. 3. Carry the paper by systems: a deed
binds only once recorded, so it travels to the county seat by the daily train or the stage, and whether
it arrives, burns or is taken plays out on the train and the gang.

**Cut or simplify:** cut Blue arriving alone as a way to hear of it (a new animal with a can't-be-hurt
rule just so he always arrives); her absence, the train crew and the saloon already do it.

**Doubtful about 1882:** *Coffin v. Left Hand Ditch Co.* and "first in time, first in right" are sound.
The "bill of sale" isn't: a Colorado water right was real property, conveyed by a deed acknowledged
before an officer and recorded; and the priority would normally rest on a court decree under the
1879/1881 adjudication acts. Check both.
