# The reference shelf: famous missions in our card shape

Eight well-known missions written as cards in the same shape as ours, so the judge has a fixed
yardstick (`JUDGE.md`). **Design only**, from what's well documented (the games themselves, their
wikis and walkthroughs). Where this session wasn't sure of a detail it says so or leaves it out;
nothing here is guessed. A fact-check pass (a separate agent with web access) read every card; its
notes are at the end.

**The honest limit** (#739, Sean 2026-11-09): this compares design to design. A shipped mission is
also its voice acting, set dressing and polish, which a paper card can't show. A score here says "is
the design as strong", not "will it feel as good". The play test still decides.

Never copy a reference's content into one of our missions: the judge uses them to show what a design
does, not what ours should contain.

The shelf's scores (the judge's calibration) are in `JUDGE.md`, "Calibration".

## R1. "The Bloody Baron" (The Witcher 3: Wild Hunt, CD Projekt Red, 2015)

- **How you hear of it:** following the main story's trail of a missing young woman, the player comes
  to a garrison whose lord, the "Bloody Baron", will share what he knows only if the player first
  finds his missing wife and daughter. A trade, not a marker.
- **The situation:** a warlord's household has fallen apart. His wife and daughter fled; the servants
  hint why. Investigation (the protagonist's tracking senses, people's accounts) slowly shows he beat
  his wife, and that she lost a pregnancy. The trail runs into a swamp ruled by three witches.
- **The ways to do it:** largely one path through a chain of steps (investigate, track, a drinking
  scene, a fight with a cursed being). The flexibility is in how you treat people along the way, and
  in a side branch about the stillborn child's restless spirit: lay it to rest with a burial rite, or
  kill the monster it became. The burial changes later scenes.
- **The big choice and its cost:** a spirit trapped under a tree asks to be freed; it promises help,
  while the witches want it kept. The player chooses before knowing what it means. The choice decides
  the fate of a group of orphans in the swamp and of the Baron's wife, and through her, the Baron's
  own end (he can leave with her to seek a cure, or take his own life). This session isn't certain of
  the exact pairing of each choice with each outcome, so it's left out.
- **What happens after:** the consequences arrive later in the main story, out of the player's sight,
  long after the choice. The Baron's fate, his wife's and the orphans' are told in the world (a body,
  a letter, a person's account). People talk about it.
- **Where:** a garrison, a war-torn region of villages, a swamp. One region, travelled over many hours.
- **Threads it moves:** the main search; the region's political story; the witches (a thread that
  carries on).
- **Systems it needs:** dialogue choices, investigation vision, combat, quest-state flags. Mostly
  authored, branching scripts.

## R2. "Lady Boyle's Last Party" (Dishonored, Arkane Studios, 2012)

- **How you hear of it:** the player's allies name the target and hand over an invitation. One of the
  game's run of assassination missions.
- **The situation:** a masked ball at a rich family's mansion. The target is one of three sisters, all
  in similar masks and gowns; the player has to find out which one is the right Lady Boyle. The
  player can walk in through the front as a masked "guest".
- **The ways to do it:** find the target by talking to guests, overhearing, reading, doing favours
  for guests (including, by most accounts, being drawn into a duel). Then: kill her at the party,
  lure her away and kill her quietly, or the non-lethal way: deliver her, unconscious, to a guest who
  is obsessed with her and has a boat waiting. Movement powers, stealth through the mansion's back
  rooms, or open violence are all possible.
- **The big choice and its cost:** lethal or non-lethal. The non-lethal option is dark in its own way
  (she disappears into a stranger's hands for good). Killing raises the game's "chaos" level, which
  changes the city's state and later missions.
- **What happens after:** the game tracks chaos (killings, alarms) and shifts guards, plague and the
  ending by it. Characters comment on how you handled her.
- **Where:** one mansion and its grounds, with an open public floor and hidden private spaces.
- **Threads it moves:** the conspiracy's members, one by one.
- **Systems it needs:** stealth and AI suspicion, social disguise (being a guest), conversations with
  clues, a lethal/non-lethal economy, the chaos system.

## R3. "The Showstopper" (Hitman, IO Interactive, 2016, the Paris level)

- **How you hear of it:** a briefing names two targets: the heads of a spy ring, at a fashion show
  they host while secretly auctioning stolen agent identities upstairs.
- **The situation:** a palace on fashion-show night, hundreds of guests, staff, security, a hidden
  auction, two targets with routines that cross the building.
- **The ways to do it:** dozens. Disguises (staff, guards, a famous model, an auction guest) open
  areas; "opportunities" (the game's guided leads, which can be switched off) set up accidents such as
  a lighting rig dropped on a runway, a poisoned drink, a staged private meeting; or improvise with
  objects, routes and timing. Exits vary too.
- **The big choice and its cost:** how, not whether: the targets die. The cost is detection: being
  spotted, bodies found, alarms. Choice is in method, route and disguise.
- **What happens after:** within the level, alarms, searches and the crowd react. Beyond it, almost
  nothing: the level is a self-contained sandbox designed to be replayed with challenges and mastery.
- **Where:** one big building and its grounds, densely layered (catwalks, attic, cellars, a garden).
- **Threads it moves:** the season's conspiracy story, lightly.
- **Systems it needs:** disguises and suspicion by role, NPC routines, AI that investigates and
  searches, accident hooks on props, the opportunity system.

## R4. Liberty Island (Deus Ex, Ion Storm, 2000, the first mission)

- **How you hear of it:** the player's agency briefs him: terrorists have seized the island and the
  statue; their leader is inside.
- **The situation:** a small island under armed occupation, a captured agent to free, the leader at
  the top of the statue.
- **The ways to do it:** the player's brother offers a choice of special weapon at the start, a long
  rifle, a rocket launcher or a tranquilliser crossbow, which hints at play styles. Routes: the front,
  around the sides, through the dock buildings, by climbing; stealth, lockpicking and hacking, or
  fighting. Non-lethal weapons are offered from the first minute.
- **The big choice and its cost:** kill or spare. The terrorist leader can be cornered and spoken with
  instead of killed, by most accounts. Colleagues react: some praise a high body count, the brother
  quietly disapproves of killing, an attitude that matters as the story turns.
- **What happens after:** people comment on how you played; the terrorists' cause becomes the story.
  The island itself is left behind.
- **Where:** one island: the statue, docks, a few buildings, open ground, sewers and back ways.
- **Threads it moves:** the brother and the agency (the game's central turn lies ahead).
- **Systems it needs:** multiple routes in level design, skills and augmentations that open routes,
  lethal/non-lethal weapons, characters reacting to the player's body count.

## R5. "Blessed Are the Peacemakers" (Red Dead Redemption 2, Rockstar, 2018, chapter 3)

- **How you hear of it:** a gang member in camp starts it: the gang's leader is going to meet a rival
  gang's leader about a truce, and the player's character goes along to watch over it.
- **The situation:** a parley between two outlaw gangs in hostile country; distrust on both sides.
- **The ways to do it:** one path. Per walkthroughs, the truce fails, the player's character is
  captured and held, then frees himself (a short swinging prompt), reaches a knife, can take guards
  quietly from behind or fight, and rides out. The mission grades extra objectives with medals.
- **The big choice and its cost:** none. It's a story beat: the rival gang becomes a lasting threat.
- **What happens after:** the feud carries on through the story; the world holds no lasting physical
  change from the mission itself.
- **Where:** the parley site and the rival gang's hideout.
- **Threads it moves:** the gang feud; the leader's judgement (his plans failing).
- **Systems it needs:** authored mission scripting, gunplay, horse riding; few systemic inputs.

## R6. "Who the Hell is Leviticus Cornwall?" (Red Dead Redemption 2, chapter 1: the train robbery)

- **How you hear of it:** the gang's leader plans it in camp, using plans and explosives taken from a
  rival gang in an earlier mission.
- **The situation:** a rich industrialist's private train; the gang is broke and snowbound and needs
  the money.
- **The ways to do it:** one path, with variation in execution: the gang stops and boards the train,
  fights the guards, and breaks into the private car for a lockbox. This session isn't certain of the
  exact boarding sequence and leaves it out. Medal goals reward saving a gang member hanging from the
  train and clearing guards quickly.
- **The big choice and its cost:** the surviving guards are held at gunpoint and the player chooses to
  kill or spare them, which moves the game's honour meter. The industrialist's wealth makes him an
  enemy who later hires detectives against the gang (as the story goes on).
- **What happens after:** the stolen bonds and money feed the gang's story; the industrialist's
  pursuit grows into a main thread.
- **Where:** a stretch of rail line in the mountains.
- **Threads it moves:** the gang's money; the law (detectives) closing in.
- **Systems it needs:** a scripted moving train, gunplay, the honour meter, horses.

## R7. "A Crack in the Slab" (Dishonored 2, Arkane Studios, 2016)

- **How you hear of it:** the main story sends the player to a ruined mansion to learn the villain's
  secret; inside, a strange timepiece is put in the player's hands.
- **The situation:** a mansion that is a ruin in the present and whole in the past, three years
  earlier, the night of a ritual that began the villain's rise. The device lets the player look into
  and step between the two times at will.
- **The ways to do it:** move through the ruin, and where it's blocked, step into the past where the
  doors stand; peek between times to avoid guards in both. Deal with the past's guests and guards by
  stealth or force.
- **The big choice and its cost:** the house's owner, sane in the past, was driven mad by that night.
  Knock him out in the past so he misses the ritual, and the present changes: the mansion is no longer
  ruined, he is sane and alive, and later the world shows it. Leave him, and the present stays as it
  was. The player can see the change happen.
- **What happens after:** the changed present persists into later scenes and the ending's account.
- **Where:** one house in two times.
- **Threads it moves:** the villain's origin; the mansion owner's fate.
- **Systems it needs:** a level built twice and swapped live, stealth AI, lethal/non-lethal.

## R8. "The Clockwork Mansion" (Dishonored 2, Arkane Studios, 2016)

- **How you hear of it:** the main story names the target, an inventor, and a captive he holds who the
  player needs.
- **The situation:** the inventor's mansion is a machine: levers turn whole rooms into new layouts,
  walls slide, clockwork soldiers patrol. A captive scientist is held inside.
- **The ways to do it:** go through the rooms as they reconfigure, or slip behind the walls into the
  machinery's gaps and bypass the house; fight or avoid the clockwork soldiers; a logic puzzle on a
  door is another way into part of the house. The captive must be freed and carried or led out.
- **The big choice and its cost:** kill the inventor, or a non-lethal fate that uses his own
  invention against him (the player has to find and use it). Chaos rises or falls with killing.
- **What happens after:** the inventor's fate is remembered in later dialogue and the ending's account;
  the freed captive joins the player's allies.
- **Where:** one house, which is also the mission's mechanism.
- **Threads it moves:** the coup's members; the player's allies.
- **Systems it needs:** a level built as moving parts, robot enemies, stealth, lethal/non-lethal.
