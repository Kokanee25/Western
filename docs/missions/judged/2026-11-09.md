# Judged 2026-11-09

The first runs of the mission judge (`JUDGE.md`), after its calibration on the reference shelf.
**Design against design, not polish:** a shipped AAA mission is also its voice, dressing and polish,
which a paper card can't show. A score here says "is the design as strong", not "will it feel as
good". Each mission was judged by a fresh agent that saw only its card, the rubric with the
calibration, the scored shelf (`references.md`) and `STORY.md`.

## No Justice (`no-justice`)

Judged on Sean's card of 2026-11-09.

| Criterion | Score | Nearest reference | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Choices | 3 | about the Bloody Baron | Like the Baron: one chain and one choice with no clean answer (nod/shake/shoot/act at the cabin; arrest leads to release, revenge brings the kin). But the only real choice of approach is quiet or loud at one camp, and both endings end at the same rope. Linearity was chosen on purpose and sharpens the choice, but it caps the score at 3: a 4 needs several ways through as well as the big choice. |
| Systems over scripts | 3 | about A Crack in the Slab | The fight, the wound-system scalping, the nod read from the player's head, acting as an answer and the "bends, doesn't break" cases are systems. But beats 1–3 are an authored ride with set talk, and the turns are written (the red shirt flees to the cabin, surrenders unarmed, Joe asks). The linear first half is where most of the script sits. |
| Consequences | 4 | about A Crack in the Slab and Lady Boyle | People and places carry it: the bodies found, the town split, the camp's trust, the released man on Main Street, Joe's memory; murdering the surrendered man goes through witnesses and rumour. Not 5: most of it is hand-written branches ("the town splits", "kin come looking", "Harlan learns he was released"), both endings feed the same hanging, and the abandon-Joe branch is OPEN. |
| Unique | 3 | about Liberty Island | A familiar setup (a revenge ride, a cornered man to kill or spare). The fresh element is answering with a nod while Joe stands with the knife. Among ours it overlaps with Joe on the Rope (it leads straight into it) and partly with Hold Him Till the Marshal Comes (a prisoner in our jail). |
| True to 1882 | 3 | about Lady Boyle | The law failing to punish the killing of a Ute woman, and Joe's disguise to shield his people, are grounded and plausible. What would tie it to 1882 is OPEN: the scalp-bounty trade, by what right Harlan arrests, who "the proper authorities" are and why they release him. |
| Discoverable | 3 | about the Bloody Baron | One way to hear of it: a grieving man in the doctor's office; nothing on the card brings it to you if you never walk in. The showpiece reads from the place itself: the red shirt plain as he runs, Joe asking you to your face, "Is that a yes?" as the fallback. |
| Cost to build | 3 | about Lady Boyle | Several new systems, mostly shared: the nod, begging and escort, a fighting companion, the knife, the barricade, the jail and transfer. One-offs: scalping, the red shirt, the disguise. Unlisted and new: a companion who keeps quiet, readable tracks. |
| **Total** | **22/35** | | |

**What the references do that this doesn't:** Lady Boyle gives many ways to find and deal with the
target, out of the systems; here the way to the cabin is narrated and leads to one door. Lady Boyle's
consequences run on a system (chaos), and A Crack in the Slab shows the change in the world at once;
here the aftermath is mostly written branches and a reported release, and both branches end at the
same set piece.

**Beef it up:**
1. Tie Joe's disguise to the consequence through the witness system. If a bandit escapes, or a
   passer-by sees Joe's face in the fight, the kin go to the Ute camp rather than to Joe. The disguise
   then works or fails by what the player does, instead of being only characterisation.
2. Let the release happen on the town's calendar rather than be learned of. The prisoner leaves on the
   stage or train under escort and comes back the same way, so the player can be on the platform, see
   who lets him go, and act then. His memory of Harlan sets how he behaves when they meet in town.
3. Open the cabin to the structure system instead of one barricaded door: dug out by a kick, a
   planted stick or through a wall of members. His flight is his nerve breaking rather than a scripted
   run: if the cabin is cut off he goes for a horse, and an escaped red shirt becomes a man on the
   road.

**Cut or simplify:** Cut "the bold cowboy" from the doctor's office. He repeats the doctor's warning,
and who he is is an OPEN casting problem (Brody or a townsman). The doctor alone carries the warning,
and the prophecy coming true lands harder from one mouth.

**Sean's picks (2026-11-09):** idea 1 yes ("Disguise matters, yes"); idea 2 yes, by the train
("release to the train"); idea 3 without dynamite ("no dynamite yet that comes in a later mission").
The cut (the bold cowboy) not picked. Also answered: Harlan arrests as the man "temporarily appointed
by the dead sheriff". All on the card.

## No Law (`no-law`)

Judged on the card built from Sean's draft and his chat answers of 2026-11-09. Where they disagree,
the judge scored Sean's chat reading (seated, unarmed, can't act during the murder).

| Criterion | Score | Nearest reference | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Choices | 2 | about Leviticus Cornwall | The murder has no choices; approaches A and B end at the same events; the nod gives the revolver either way, its only cost what the family and doctor remember. "Mostly observational" explains the score but doesn't raise it. |
| Systems over scripts | 2 | about the Bloody Baron | The kick, the shot, the remark, the exit, the sheriff's death and the funeral are authored; the player is held so he can't interfere. The patrons' reactions, witnesses and rumours afterwards are systemic. |
| Consequences | 3 | about Leviticus Cornwall | Later scenes change (the red shirt recognised in No Justice, the widow met before the Farmhouse, the family remembering), but the grave, the free murderer and the revolver happen whatever you do; nothing later plays differently because of your choices. |
| Unique | 2 | about Leviticus Cornwall | A cheat shot at cards and a lawman handing over his gun on his deathbed are stock Western beats; overlaps with The Sheriff on the Road and The Dust Storm. Freshest: the forced helplessness and a funeral that remembers whether you came. |
| True to 1882 | 2 | about Blessed Are the Peacemakers | "Temporarily appointed by the dead sheriff" is the trope the card's own notes flag (a dying sheriff can't pass on his office); the legal process, funeral customs and undertaker are "needs research", which counts as missing. |
| Discoverable | 4 | above the Showstopper (the shelf's best is 3) | Several channels: town talk, the doctor or "someone sent", the church, townspeople or family; the murder in plain view in the main saloon, and "becomes town news" covers a player who isn't there. Not 5: whether you can miss the saloon is OPEN. |
| Cost to build | 3 | about Lady Boyle | New but mostly shared: the card game, the funeral and burial, graves and obituaries, remembered attendance, the held seated view, the nod. Only the deathbed hand-over is one-off. |
| **Total** | **18/35** | | |

**What the references do that this doesn't:** the Bloody Baron and Lady Boyle put a costly choice
inside the key scene; here the key scene is watched and the one choice (the nod) changes nothing
material. The Showstopper lets the player interfere and the systems cope, where this one holds the
player still so the script stays safe. A Crack in the Slab lets the player see the world change
because of what he chose; everything here that persists happens whatever he does.

**Beef it up:**
1. Make the aftermath the hands-on part, with the steward's past and the wound system: the anatomy
   decides whether the card player dies at once or bleeds, so Harlan's first aid on the saloon floor
   is a real result: a living witness who can name the red shirt later, or a death he saw coming.
2. Make talking a choice with a cost, through witnesses and the saloon owner who sells what he hears.
   Name the red shirt openly and it spreads, and the gang's opinion of Harlan carries it (settling
   "does he know Harlan saw it"); stay quiet and he's safer, but the town's versions drift and the
   widow hears he said nothing.
3. Let the saloon hold the murder physically: the broken chair, the blood on the boards, the ball in
   the floor, the card player's burial paid for or left to the town. The place tells the story to a
   player who came late, and the evidence can carry into later threads.

**Cut or simplify:** take the funeral out of No Law as a mission beat and drop the new "remembered
attendance at social events" system: the sheriff's burial happens as town life (the grave persists),
and the family's existing opinion of Harlan simply records whether they saw him there.
