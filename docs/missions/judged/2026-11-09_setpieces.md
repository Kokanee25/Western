## Each, with two ways to raise it and one cut

**Payday Night (5).** Systemic (each man's weight, the creak, bow and dust from the load); player-authored
(reels, thin, clear, arrest, expose, prepare); readable (the best of ours); varies (who falls, how many);
leaves a mark (the floor, the rubble, the sawn joist). Risk: ~12 men falling indoors needs its frame lab.
*Raise:* 1. Shore it: a timber wedged under the cut joist moves the load path; done badly it moves the
break to the next joist. 2. The panic surge: at the first crack the dancers rush the stairhead and load
that end. *Cut:* Joe on watch (the floor needs nothing from him).

**Hell on Wheels (5, low).** Physics decides; six ways against the distance left; readable (the foreman, the
blow-through, known stopping distances); varies strongly; the wreck stays. Risk: step 7's fighting on
moving cars. *Raise:* 1. The break where it can be seen from the train: a straight after a bend, a section
man's red flag at the rail (period practice). 2. The derail grades with speed: slow, the engine ploughs off
the rail end with the cars upright; fast, the cars jackknife and break up. *Cut:* pulling the pin (its
window can't be seen; the air cocks give the same "part the train" choice more readably).

**The Bridge Is Out (4).** Systemic (load paths); player-authored (placement, size, when to fire);
readable place, but the run-up is OPEN; varies (a poor cut holds empty, the engine finishes it); the wreck
saved. Risk: the slow spell in collapses. *Raise:* 1. The engineer's lookout brakes short by day if he
sees damage: charges hidden in the bents, set at night, or fired once the train is past its stopping
distance. 2. The daily train tests the job: a part-cut bent groans and deflects under the ordinary crossing
days before. *Cut:* the choreographed "train flying coupled until it lands" (#658): let `TrainWreck`'s
`fell` carry it.

**The Baron's Shipment v2 (4, close to 5).** A rigid log held by breakable stakes, the coach handed to
rigid bodies, the dents; timing is the skill; hit, block or miss differ; the dented coach stays. Risk: the
log's roll is chaotic. *Raise:* 1. An honest topple: where the log strikes (wheels or the plated side) and
the coach's speed in the bend decide whether it goes over. 2. The deck releases log by log: one stake, one
log; the chain, the whole deck buries the road. *Cut:* "the riflemen thrown and dazed" as a stated outcome
(let the ragdoll decide).

**The Farmhouse v2 (4).** The planted stick, the board-cut hole, the gang dazed, the alarm by hearing (all
built); which wall, the hiding spot, the rovers' timing; varies (won 4–5 of 8). Mark later (saves).
*Raise:* 1. The plant is a read of the house: reflected pressure hurts whoever is behind that wall, so the
bedroom wall can hurt the widow; look through the windows first. 2. Time it to the foreman: while he walks
up the lane the gang faces the front. *Cut:* anything past one stick (a ring or a bundle breaks the frame
and kills the person the mission is about).

**The Balcony Ambush (3).** The parts exist; placement and timing; why, where and how you hear are OPEN;
varies unnamed. *Raise:* 1. The load is the men's own weight (Payday's mirror): a cracked post holds until
a third man steps out, so the player chooses when it's loaded. 2. A partial that asks the mission's
question: one corner drops, men slide onto the railing, one hangs on alive: haul him up and arrest him, or
leave him. *Cut:* posts cut by gunfire (untested, and only kept for an Act I slot it no longer has).

**Buried Alive (3, low).** Mostly new systems underground; dig, shore or blast in one line; readable only
as "timbers settling". Risk: high. *Raise:* 1. The sets talk: caps split and posts bow from their own load,
so the player sees which set goes next and shores it before he blasts. 2. The single stick's believable
partial: a crawl hole frees one man but not the one pinned under a cap; a second stick, or dig. *Cut:* the
gas.

**Joe on the Rope (3).** Ballistics and Joe's clock; pure skill and timing; pass or fail by design. Risk:
high: **a 2 cm rope at 100–150 m is under a pixel at 1280×720, so the 30–60 m range (#734) is the buildable
one**; rope and a hanging body aren't built. *Raise:* 1. Degrees, not a switch: a graze cuts strands and
the rope parts under his weight seconds later, or not; cut down, Joe falls with his hands tied. 2. The mob
answers the first shot: heard late by distance, some scatter, the man on the rope's end hauls or runs; the
first shot changes the second. *Cut:* "ride under Joe and lift him" (needs ridden horses and carrying a
man).
