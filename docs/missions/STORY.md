# Salt Creek in one page (for the mission judge)

The judge reads this, the rubric (`JUDGE.md`), the scored reference shelf (`references.md`) and one
mission card. Nothing else. Kept to a page on purpose; the cards and `DESIGN.md` hold the rest.

**The game.** A first-person immersive-sim Western for PC, 1882, one valley about 4 km across with
the small town of Salt Creek at its foot. Grounded and gritty, with humour. Pillars: systems, not
scripts; consequences that stay; people you can talk to; a small world, deep. No quest markers and
almost no HUD: news comes as rumour, a rider, a letter, a gunshot, a person who asks.

**What's deep already.** Gunfights with men who take cover, flank, rescue each other, lose their
nerve, surrender. Bodies with hidden anatomy: wounds bleed, break bones, stop men without hit points;
ragdolls that keep their momentum. Buildings made of real timber members that splinter, hole and
collapse; dynamite that cuts walls and digs craters; rubble that stays. A daily train on real track
that derails for real, and a timber trestle that falls by its load paths. Fire is built but out of
play for now. People see by light and hear through walls, remember what they saw, and hold opinions
of you; arrests, witnesses and a real trial are in the design.

**Who you are.** Thomas Harlan, a homesteader with a spring on his claim; a former army hospital steward
with a past he doesn't talk about. No classes: sheriff, gang, hired gun or rancher paths open and
close by what you do. Save only by sleeping in a bed; some deaths are for good.

**The story's shape** (Sean, 2026-11-01): open-ended threads, no day count. "The story doesn't
advance until it should advance." A thread moves when its conditions are met; people can nudge you
toward it, never a deadline. The town's own calendar (the daily train, the stage, payday) keeps
running whatever you do.

**The people and the pressures.** Water is the stakes. The cattle Baron wants every drop in the valley
and is patient. The sheriff was murdered over his family's water right (who shot him and who ordered
it are open); his widow, with two young sons, refuses to sell. The Colter gang works the valley (who
they answer to is open). The railroad already runs; a special train of hired guns and soldiers comes
for the town at the end. Joe, a Southern Ute man whose wife was raped, murdered and scalped by a gang of raiders, becomes
Harlan's friend and (proposed) the man with plans who pitches ideas for the big jobs. The town: a doctor, a saloon owner who sells what he hears, a storekeeper, a blacksmith, a
miner who teaches dynamite. A Southern Ute community, a Mexican rancher, Chinese workers: written with
research and care, never props.

**The spine's 1882 correction** (PROPOSED by this session with Hell on Wheels v2; Sean: "Yes fold it in
with all the ticks"; confirm it applies to the spine): after the Posse Comitatus Act (1878) the Army
couldn't simply be sent to keep order in a town. In the Johnson County War (1892) the governor called it
an insurrection and the President sent troops. So the Baron needs Salt Creek to look like a town in
revolt, not only a lawless one.

**The spine** (Sean's picks, 2026-11-09): the Baron wins if Salt Creek looks lawless enough that
soldiers are sent to restore order; Rourke is the officer he pays to bring them. The red shirt is the
Baron's hired gun and shot the sheriff; the Baron offers the widow a price at the sheriff's funeral;
Payday Night (after the train's payroll is attacked) brings Rourke in on the train. Rourke is met
whatever the player chose in No Justice.

**The threads.** T1 the sheriff and the badge · T2 Joe and the revenge · T3 the mine and the miner
(single sticks of dynamite early, bundles at the end) · T4 the Baron and the water · T5 the train and
Rourke (Harlan's old commander, proposed) · T6 the gang · T7 the town's law (mobs, lynching, a trial) ·
T8 Harlan's past (proposed). The finale: the trestle blown under the train, then the raid on the
Baron's masonry house to arrest him (the raid decided 2026-11-04; Sean's inventory of 2026-11-09 ends at
the trestle, so OPEN).

**Sean's working order** (his inventory, 2026-11-09: "a working sequence, not a locked mission
order"): Act I, becoming the sheriff: the opening (the sheriff, the saloon, a new sheriff), No Justice,
the Balcony Ambush (placement open). Act II, learning the valley: Buried Alive, the Demolition Mentor,
the Farmhouse. Act III, the conspiracy emerges: the Runaway Payroll Train, Payday Night / the Inquest
(proposed), the Woman Tied to the Tracks. Act IV, the Baron and Rourke: the Baron's Shipment, more
confrontations (not designed), the Trestle Finale. Rourke is an Army officer the Baron pays.

**Constraints Sean set** (inventory, 2026-11-09): Joe is the only full companion; gunfights are small
numbers of lethal enemies; dynamite arrives in Buried Alive, not the opening; the small wagon-road
overpass is for early train encounters and boarding; the great trestle is kept for the finale. The
evidence and the trial are "a maybe" (Sean, 2026-11-09).

**Every mission, one line each** (to judge "unique among ours"):
- No Law: night one in the saloon, Harlan not yet the law; the man in the red shirt murders a card cheat.
- The Sheriff on the Road: you find him gut-shot at dawn; carry him to the doctor or leave him; on day 2 he deputizes you and dies.
- No Justice: raiders killed Joe's wife; ride with him; the man in the red shirt hides in a cabin; nod to stop Joe, or not.
- Joe on the Rope: a mob hangs Joe; shoot the rope from an overlook, or he dies for good.
- The Widow's Place: a quiet ride out to the sheriff's widow; the boys at play and Blue met; he rides the sheriff's horse home, she shows him the stakes at her ditch, asks if he was with him at the end, and gives him the sheriff's rifle.
- The Farmhouse Rescue: gunmen hold the widow's house; front, back, or a planted stick; then her boys.
- Buried Alive: miners trapped after a blast; dig, shore or blast in; learn dynamite.
- The Baron's Shipment: his deeds and ledgers in a guarded convoy (an iron coach); a log trap, disguises, impersonation or a straight interception.
- The Bank Break-in: at night through the back wall for a document in the safe; too much powder burns it.
- Hell on Wheels (the Runaway Payroll Train): fight one gang at the sabotaged track, jump from the overpass onto the train another gang holds, fight through six cars to the cab, brake in time.
- The Bridge Is Out: the train of hired guns and soldiers; blow the trestle, or stop it another way.
- The Raid on the Baron's House: breach his barricaded stone house and arrest him alive.
- The Trial, the Hanging and the Inquest: the circuit judge, witnesses who remember, a jury; you may hang.
- Hold Him Till the Marshal Comes: a Colter in your jail two nights; the gang, the mob, his brother's money.
- The Lynching at the Railhead: a Chinese worker blamed for a killing; a mob with a rope.
- The Vaquero's Horses: a Mexican rancher's horses rebranded in the Baron's corral.
- The Kid Wants Out: the gang's youngest wants to leave; the gang doesn't let men leave.
- The Fever House: a sick family; a night ride for medicine, or the town drives them out.
- (Cut, Sean 2026-11-09: Tied to the Tracks, a woman bound on the line; it repeated Hell on Wheels.)
- The Night Meeting That's a Trap: an ambush in the feed store at night; burn, or blow out the back wall.
- The Store Robbery: the gang leans on the storekeeper; pay, talk, threaten, fight, arrest.
- The Bank Robbery: the gang's plan for the land money; the alarm, a posse; pick a side.
- The Fire Night: a building alight at night; the bucket line, a rescue, or chase the men who did it.
- The Dust Storm: everyone shut in the saloon for a night; cards, drink, an old grudge.
- A Cattle Drive Hits Town: a dozen paid-off cowboys, drunk; a brawl, maybe a stampede.
- The Stage Held Up: the stage robbed on the road; ride shotgun, chase, or rob it yourself.
- The Bounty: a wanted man holed up in a house on a hill; alive pays more.
- Small Fights: road agents, rustlers, claim jumpers, horse thieves, a feud, out in the valley.
- The Balcony Ambush: later, with dynamite: bring a balcony down on the Baron's men, as they did to the town (the reason open).
- The Demolition Mentor: the miner teaches half-sticks, linked charges and bundles; maybe optional lessons.
- Payday Night: the Baron's men saw the dance floor's joists; read the signs and prevent it, or a night of triage for the steward and Rourke's surgeon for a signature.
- The Inquest and the Wrong Ball: an inquiry, a ball that doesn't match (proposed; evidence a maybe).
- Dry Creek: a fight or an emergency over the valley's water; gates, channels, sluices (brainstorm).
- Dig Him Up: a grave opened at night, by stealth, to find something on the body (whose grave open).
- I Am the Law: an Army captain's one day in town to report on revolt; the Baron's men stage one; keep the peace unseen, and Joe is their target.
