# Salt Creek: the materials judge's rubric

Sean, 2026-11-10 (#701/#721: "we're grading the material, not the scene"). The cheap judge
(`CHEAP.md`) asks about a whole scene; on the material lab's sample panels its scene tells (empty,
edges of the world, too straight, atmosphere) fail whatever the wood is. This judge sees **only
crops of the material** and asks one question per swatch: **does this read as a real material of
this kind and age, made with care, in Red Dead's direction?**

It is a fresh, blind sub-agent. It sees the crops (named by swatch code and age, never by scan)
and, as a yardstick, official screenshots of shipped AAA games read where they are in the private
Character Studio (never copied into this repo, not even as a crop). It does not judge the scene,
the sky, the ground, the people or the light's colour (that's the light lane's), only the
material as the light shows it.

## What it checks (each PASS / FAIL, with a crop box as evidence)

1. **Real stuff:** the grain, figure and colour read as this kind of wood (pine, fir, walnut...),
   not a stock texture, laminate, plastic or a painting of wood.
2. **Age reads:** new / a few years / old are clearly different and each is believable for the
   desert (sun-greying, checking, splits, dirt splash, wear where hands and feet go).
3. **No tile tells:** no repeat, no seam, no square blocks, dots, weave or mush at 1 m.
4. **Board to board:** boards differ the way real ones do (tone, figure, width, set), without
   reading as random noise or stripes.
5. **Edges and joints:** joints, laps and edges read as shadowed gaps and worn arrises, not
   lines drawn on (bright or coloured outlines, dashes).
6. **Holds at distance:** at 5 m it keeps its character without shimmer, mush or a flat card.

## Painted wood (#721's paint round, 2026-11-11)

A painted swatch (codes P1...) is judged by the same six checks and grades, read for a coat of paint
over boards:

1. **Real stuff:** reads as oil house paint on wood (white lead, an iron-oxide red, chrome green, a
   buff, a pale indoor coat): a film with the boards' grain and edges under it, not a flat colour,
   plastic, a decal or a stock "peeling paint" texture.
2. **Age reads:** new is a sound, even coat with a low sheen; a few years chalks and fades where the
   sun works, cracks along the grain, lets go first where water sits (the foot, a lap's lower edge,
   board ends, the arrises) and takes the street's dust low; old has failed in strips and flakes to
   primer and grey bare wood. Indoors: yellowed, rubbed where hands go, scuffed low.
3-6. As for wood; a peel's edge should read as a film's edge (sharp, a little lifted), never as
   dots, noise or a pattern stamped over the boards.

## Floors and furniture (#825)

A floor swatch (codes FL...) is a room's floor by its wall, a doorway in the wall and a worn path
from it; a furniture swatch (codes FN...) is a chest of drawers in that finish, standing on a floor
of another wood. The same six checks and grades, read for:

1. **Real stuff:** floor boards (pine or fir, oiled or bare) of a working building, not a deck or a
   laminate; a joined piece's wood and finish (varnished oak, walnut, painted pine), not a wall's
   boards cut to shape, plastic or a decal.
2. **Age reads:** a floor's path worn paler and duller where feet go and darker, dustier by the wall,
   more so with the years; a piece's finish rubbed through on its edges and round its pulls, grease
   darkening round them, dust on its top, the varnish duller in patches.
3-6. As for wood; the wear should follow use (the path, the edges, the pulls), never read as a
   pattern, a smear or lines drawn along the edges.

## The grade (one per swatch and age)

| grade | what it means |
|---|---|
| **fake** | Reads as a texture: tells everywhere. |
| **game** | Reads as wood, but a game's: one or two clear tells. |
| **near the yardstick** | Reads as real wood; tells only if you hunt. |
| **at the yardstick** | As convincing as the AAA references' wood. |

Then, per swatch: the one change that would move it up most, and which of its ages is best.

## Stone and lime (#860, #857)

A stone swatch (codes S...) is a 2 m stretch of a building's stone wall out in the desert: coursed
rubble with dressed quoins at its corner and a dressed coping; a lime swatch (codes PL...) is a stone
wall's inside face in a room, lime-plastered and whitewashed (PL1) or bare rubble whitewashed (PL2),
a worn floor before it. The same six checks and grades, read for:

1. **Real stuff:** local stone laid by a mason (hammer-squared stones of uneven size in thick lime
   joints), not a brick or block texture, a stone wallpaper or a painting of stone; lime plaster and
   whitewash as a soft coat on a wall, not paint on drywall or a flat colour.
2. **Age reads:** new is clean-cut stone in pale fresh pointing; with the years the joints erode back
   and grey, the stone takes a patina and a dark crust, damp and dirt rise at the foot and streaks run
   under the coping. Inside: the wash thins and flakes, a grimy band where men lean, scuffs low.
3. **No tile tells:** no repeat, no two stones the same, no rows ruled level the length of the wall.
4. **Stone to stone:** stones differ the way real ones do (size, shape, tone, how they sit), without
   reading as random noise.
5. **Edges and joints:** joints read as recessed mortar in shadow, arrises as knocked and worn edges,
   not lines drawn on or rounded loaves.
6. **Holds at distance:** at 5 m it reads as a stone wall, not a pattern or a flat card.
