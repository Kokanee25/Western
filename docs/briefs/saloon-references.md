## The pattern (what most of them share)

- **Narrow and deep:** 20-30 ft wide, 30-60 ft deep, on one lot; the big ones (the Oriental, the
  Crystal Palace, the Board of Trade) on a **corner** or two lots.
- **One storey in a young camp, two in brick once the town settled:** offices, rooms or a club
  room upstairs.
- **The bar down one long side wall from near the door** (the Oriental's to the right as you come
  in), the back bar and mirror on the wall behind it; back bars 12-30 ft long, ~17-22 in deep.
- **Games in a second room** at the back or behind a partition (the "club room"), or upstairs or
  in the basement.
- **A store room behind,** often rougher built; a yard and privies behind that (not documented
  here).
- **The floor at street level or a step off it** (some of Virginia City's below it, on the slope).
- **Doors:** batwing doors were uncommon and regional (where they existed, full doors closed
  outside them); glazed double doors with a transom are the safer period choice (from photographs,
  not checked one by one). **A corner building** often had entrances on both streets and sometimes a
  **diagonal corner entrance** with an iron column at the corner
  ([History Colorado](https://www.historycolorado.org/nineteenth-century-commercial)).

## Bases for the new town's lot

The lot is on the crossroads' north-east corner: 16.4 m (54 ft) along Main Street, 17.1 m (56 ft)
deep, its front facing south, the North-South Street on its west side, a 10.4 m yard to its east.
- **The Oriental and the Crystal Palace (Tombstone):** corner saloons of 1880-82: a bar room with
  the bar to the right of the door and a gaming room; gambling rooms and offices upstairs.
- **The Board of Trade (Leadville, 1883):** a Colorado mining town in the right year, two storeys,
  its rooms in order front to back (antechamber, barroom, club room, back rooms, a lunch counter).
- **Piper's Old Corner Bar (Virginia City):** a two-storey brick corner building, the bar below,
  offices above: the upper floor's model.
- **The Carissa (HABS WY-31):** measured drawings; the back of the house (a rough store room behind).

54 ft is about two period lots: a building that wide would most likely be the saloon on the corner
with another room (a gaming room, a store) beside it. **The moon:** a diagonal corner door on the
south-west corner faces the crossroads, and a door on the west side faces the North-South Street;
the valley's moon arc is a separate dial (see #660).
