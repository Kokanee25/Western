# Joe's people's clothes: plain, true to 1882 (Southern Ute)

Issue #1096. Lane: characters. Docs only; making the clothes is a later session.

**DECIDED** (Sean, 2026-11-19): plain and basic, but true to 1882. **No beads, feathers, headdresses,
hair-pipe breastplates or chokers, no sacred or ceremonial items, no paint.** Nothing for Joe's people
comes from an AI image, Tripo or Rodin (they turn "Native" into Plains stereotypes); the clothes are
sewn by our pattern method (#1013) from documented garments, or taken from Sean's western bundle where it
fits (shirts, vests, trousers and hats were trade clothing everyone wore). The camp is the only scene
that shows it; Joe wears his cowboy costume for most of No Justice (VOICES.md, "Joe"). Everything else here
is PROPOSED.

## On one screen (for the phone)

- **Men:** a plain cotton or calico shirt (often a second shirt over it), a dark vest, a neckerchief, cloth
  leggings over a cloth breechcloth (some in plain trousers), hard-soled moccasins, a wide-brim felt hat or
  bare-headed with two braids, a blanket worn round the body.
- **Women:** a loose ankle-length calico or striped cotton dress, a plain leather belt, a shawl or blanket
  wrapped over it, moccasins, leggings under the dress in winter, hair in two braids.
- **Children:** smaller versions of the same; babies in a plain willow-and-hide cradleboard (a prop; ask).
- **Cloth and colour:** calico prints (small figures on madder red, indigo, brown), hickory stripe and plain
  unbleached cotton for shirts; dark blue or red wool trade cloth for leggings and breechcloths; Navajo
  stripe blankets and plain grey, blue or red issue blankets. Everything worn and faded: it's a camp in
  November, not a portrait studio.
- **From the bundle (once uploaded, check):** shirts, vests, trousers, hats, neckerchiefs. **Made by us:**
  blankets, shawls, cloth leggings, breechcloths, women's dresses, plain moccasins.
- **Left out:** everything the photographs show that isn't plain (below), and anything Pendleton-type (too
  late: 1890s and after).

## What the sources say (and don't)

The southern bands in 1882: the Mouache, Capote and Weeminuche, at the Southern Ute Agency on the Los Pinos
(Pine) River near today's Ignacio since 1878 (before that the Cimarron and Abiquiu agencies in New Mexico)
[CIA reports; UHQ 1971]. **Trap:** the 1868–1880 "Los Pinos agency" at Saguache and on the Uncompahgre served
the Tabeguache/Uncompahgre Utes, so W.H. Jackson's 1874 "Los Pinos" photographs are *not* Southern Utes
[NAA Photo Lot 24; Fort Lewis College P001].

- Men: "deer-skin leggings, a cloth gee-string, a shirt, and Navaho blankets", elk-hide moccasins, two
  braids [Beals, NPS ethnology]. A print shirt under an overshirt, leggings and moccasins [DPL X-33843]. A
  vest and neckerchief on the Weeminuche leader Ignacio [W.H. Jackson, 1870–1890, DPL]. Trousers came in
  slowly under agency pressure; no count found of how many wore them in 1882.
- Women: cloth dresses wrapped in woven blankets [Nast, *Severo and family*, c.1885, DPL X-30721]; a print
  dress with a blanket shawl [DPL X-33843]; "print dress, shawl" and "striped dress, shawl" [DPL, undated].
  Calico was a staple of the trade. Ankle-length dresses in winter [Univ. of Utah collection text].
- Moccasins: a buckskin (or elk) upper sewn to a rawhide sole, the sole replaced when worn [UNC "Colorado
  Indians"; DMNS pair c.1920; NPS/Beals].
- Blankets: the Utes favoured Navajo "chief" stripe blankets [Kansas Historical Society; NMNH blanket bought
  from a Ute leader, c.1873]; New Mexican (Rio Grande) blankets are likely for the region. The agency held a
  blanket issue at the Los Pinos crossing [agency letter, Univ. of Utah].
- Hair: two braids, often wrapped (fur or cloth strips) [O'Sullivan, two Capote men, 1870–1880, DPL; DPL
  captions].

**Not yet sourced** (to check in the tribe's book and the Handbook before the clothes are made): the cut of a
Ute woman's cloth dress (the cape-sleeved "wing dress" is documented for the Nez Perce, not for Southern
Utes, so we don't use it); the moccasin's pattern (one-piece or side seam, the ankle cuff's height); the
issue blankets' colours; hat shapes; any difference between the three bands' dress; children's clothes
specifically. Where unsourced, the brief takes the plainest choice the photographs allow.

## What's left out, and why

Sean's rule, even where the period photographs show it: hair-pipe breastplates and chokers (in the Severo
c.1885 captions), bead necklaces, beaded buckskin shirts and dresses (the best clothes in the portraits),
beaded moccasins and cradleboards, feathers, knee bells, face paint, any headdress, concho (silver) belts, an
"Indian Police" badge (Ignacio's; Joe isn't police). Also left out as wrong for 1882: Pendleton-type
jacquard blankets (1890s on), the Nez Perce wing dress, anything drawn from the Plains look (war bonnets,
fringed "Hollywood" buckskins). Studio portraits show people in their best; the camp shows working days,
so plain is also the likelier truth.

## A letter Sean could send (draft; Sean decides whether to send it)

To: the Southern Ute Cultural Center & Museum (and/or the Southern Ute Indian Tribe's Culture Department),
Ignacio, Colorado.

> Dear friends,
>
> I'm a small independent game maker working on a Western set in southwest Colorado in 1882. One of the
> main characters is a Southern Ute man, and one scene takes place in his people's camp. I want the people
> in it to be dressed as they really were in everyday life then, plainly, and not as the Hollywood or Plains
> stereotype games usually show. We are leaving out beadwork, feathers, headdresses, breastplates, paint and
> anything sacred or ceremonial.
>
> I've put together a short list of the everyday clothes we plan to use (shirts, vests, cloth leggings,
> moccasins, blankets, women's calico dresses and shawls), with the sources we drew it from. Would someone in
> your department be willing to look it over and tell us what's wrong or missing? I'd also be glad to hear
> whether you'd prefer we handle anything differently, including the character's name and any words in the
> Ute language, which we won't write without a speaker.
>
> I'm happy to pay for anyone's time, and to credit the Tribe's help as you'd like.
>
> Thank you for considering it,
> Sean [surname, contact]
