# Brief: the opening (Sean, 2026-10-28)

The game starts at home, not at the saloon table. This page is the opening's plan: its beats, what
each needs from the game, what's missing, and the order to build it in. It replaces DESIGN.md §5's
"It opens at the saloon table" (2026-10-15). It's the start of Gate D (the prologue and the next
morning), reordered to begin here.

## Sean's words

"Start from the beginning of the game, which is awakening in your home. You're to go into town and
find the sheriff shot not far from you. Once you leave your house you hear several gunshots, and
walking towards town you find the shot sheriff. Help him to the doctor, explore the town for a bit,
then end up in the saloon where you meet with someone, and then the card cheat happens because no
sheriff." The someone **is the stranger**. **No gun at the start, so you can't shoot**: no gunfight
in the opening, even after the cheat is shot; maybe you can chase after them.

## The beats

| # | Beat | What happens | What the player can do |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Home** | You wake in your cabin, morning. A quiet minute: what you stand to lose. | Look round, dress, step out. No gun in the house. |
| 2 | **The shots** | Outside, several shots in the distance toward town, heard late, as sound travels. | Go toward them, or not (the act waits, then the sheriff is found by someone else and the town tells you). |
| 3 | **The sheriff** | On the road, not far from your place: the sheriff down, shot, bleeding. What he says follows his blood loss: who, which way, a name or half of one. | Stop the bleeding (pressure, a bandage, a belt), talk to him, help him up. | 5 | **The town** | The town as it is, a little shaken: people ask what happened; word spreads. No timer. | Walk, look, talk (typed lines for now). |
| 6 | **The saloon** | You end up at a table with **the stranger**: friendly, too curious about the sheriff and your spring. | Talk, drink, watch. |
| 7 | **The card cheat** | At the next table the cheat is caught; with **no sheriff** in town, it goes all the way: the chair kicked out, the gun in his mouth, the shot. | No gun: shout, shove or kick (#479), throw a bottle, grab the gun arm, or do nothing. The shooter gets away. **You're a no one: nothing makes you chase him** (Sean); you can follow, unarmed, at your own risk. |

The demo ends on the next morning: the sheriff dead, his gun in your hand (Gate D: the prologue and on into the next morning).

## Why it works

- It starts quiet and teaches the game by playing it: moving, the town, its people, first aid,
  before any fight.
- "Because no sheriff": the card cheat happens because the law is lying at the doctor's. The
  town without law (DESIGN.md's days 2–3) begins on day 1, from something you saw.
- The stranger's twist stays: you carried in the sheriff in the morning and drank with the man who
  may have shot him in the evening. The ball may match his gun, or clear him.
- No gun means every way out of the card cheat is a body, a voice or a thrown thing, which shows
  off the falls, the shove and the room's reactions instead of the trigger.

## The order to build it

1. **Acts** (3.5): missions and scenes as data, beat by beat, handing back to the systems when you
   interfere. Every beat above is written in it.
2. **The card cheat as the first act** (#344), with no gun and the shove (#479). Nearly there.
3. **The sheriff**: the shots, finding him, his words, first aid, walking him to the doctor, the
   doctor's office.
4. **The cabin and the road**: one room, a minute's walk.
5. **The town and the stranger**: the talk at the table, people asking about the shooting.
6. **Stitched together**: played through from waking up, the playtest agent blind, then Sean.

Each step is a session, ends at a green merge and Sean's look, and is judged the usual way (a lab
or a playtest where there's something to measure, a film for Sean).

## Decided (Sean, 2026-10-28)

- **You're a no one.** Nothing obliges you to chase the card cheat's shooter, help, or take a side; the
  game doesn't push you. He gets away unless you choose to go after him.
- **The sheriff dies the next day whatever you do.** You go to see him at the doctor's; he knows
  he's going, **gives you his gun and asks you to help the town** (Sean). That scene always happens,
  so the story has its fixed point. What varies by the systems is everything round it: how well
  you treated him decides how lucid he is and what else he can say (who shot him, a name, which
  way they went), and you answer as you like: take the gun, say yes, say no. If you don't come,
  the doctor sends for you. A no one, holding the lawman's gun and his request.
- **If you left him on the road** (Sean: all three, by what happened), someone else brings him
  in and the scene still happens. The doctor sends for you, and he still gives you the gun and
  asks. Only its tone changes, by what he knows (witnesses and memory, 5.3):
  - **He saw you walk away** (he was conscious when you found him): he gives it anyway, bitter, with
    no one else to give it to ("You walked right past me. Well. You're what's left."). The town
    knows too.
  - **He never saw you** (you left before he came round, or never went near): he asks you not
    knowing. You know.
  - **You took his gun off him on the road** (unarmed, it's right there): you've had it all day.
    The scene is about the ask and what you took; theft or keeping it safe, by what he saw.
  He never refuses you: the opening's ending and the way into day 2 stay.
- **You answer him with your head** (Sean): a nod or a shake, DESIGN.md §9 "Answering with a nod"
  (proposed 2026-10-21), its first real use. Taught earlier with small questions so his isn't the
  first: the sheriff on the road ("Can you get me up?"), the doctor ("Hold him. Got him?"), the
  stranger ("Another?"). Silence and a slow, small nod are answers too.
- **The demo ends the next morning** with his death and the gun.

## Open (Sean)

- **Who shot the sheriff** and why: the story isn't settled (Sean, 2026-10-28); the opening works
