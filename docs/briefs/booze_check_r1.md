## Top fixes

1. **#5 Kessel's**: too near Julius Kessler, the real Denver-to-Leadville whiskey seller of exactly this time. Rename.
2. **#29 Château Montbrisson**: real Château Monbrison, Médoc. Rename.
3. **#20 Hearthstone Cabin Bitters**: Drake's Plantation Bitters' ingredients line, patent idea and "S.T." mark. Change the ingredients, date and initials.
4. **#24 Old Medford Rum**: a real trademark held in 1882. Change the town.
5. **#28 Veuve Lacombe**: real Champagne Georges Lacombe. Rename.
6. **#25 Peyrot**: a real Cognac house. Rename the maker.
7. **#14 Huber's**: a real 19th-century US lager brewer. Rename.
8. **#15 Fothergill's**: Burton Ale vs India pale ale. Pick one.
9. **#13 Kestler**: rename, so it isn't next to Kessel/Kessler (and the Bavaria beer brand).
10. **#23 Pelham's slogan "Old Tom."**: weak; give it a real line.

## Overall

The list is strong on period feel. The patent-medicine claims, the bottle-return line, "Equal To Any Kentucky Goods", "Sold Only In Glass", "The Alcohol Is A Preservative Only" and "Or As Required" are all dry and in-world, and none of the slogans winks at the player. "Not From The Creek" comes closest, but it reads as a brewer's real worry. Nothing reads as modern: "Hand Made" sounds like craft-distillery marketing, but it is a genuine 1880s sour-mash term. The weakness is real-name collisions: the French and Medford names, Huber and Peyrot hit real houses, and Kessel collides with the one whiskey man actually working the Leadville trade in 1882. Of everything here, that collision is the one most likely to be noticed. The fine-grade imports (#9, #13, #14, #15, #26) are plain rather than funny, which is fine for the hotel shelf.
