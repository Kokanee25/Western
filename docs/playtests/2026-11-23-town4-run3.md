## What broke

- **E at the store door broke the glass.** I walked to the general store's door (t 341), faced the
  door's glass pane from 0.7 m. The knock prompt had shown on the way in (t 328.8) but was not
  offered at the door (`knock.at_door` empty). I pressed E (t 360). It shoved the pane and broke it
  (`you shoved ...entry_leaf/pane: it broke`). It shoved no person.
- **Nobody noticed the broken glass.** The storekeeper was 1.7 m away and talked about the weather
  0.3 s later. No one's feeling about me changed.
- **Things broken before I did anything.** At t 0.1 the store's crates and the undertaker's
  embalming table "went loose (fell whole)": about 39 members broken, no shot, me standing at the
  start.
- People sent somewhere "to talk" with someone who had gone indoors stood there talking to nobody
  (townsman at the livery, store wife at the hotel porch).

## What was flat

- Lots of people popped indoors and out. From the street it looks like people vanishing at doors
  and reappearing, especially the liveryman.
- "Wary" from everyone, with nothing moving it. Greetings were friendly but the numbers never moved.
- No "news" all run (empty). Nobody talked about anything that happened that day.
- The storekeeper said five unrelated lines at me while I just stood there; it stops feeling like he's
  noticing me and starts feeling like a radio.

## What was fun

- The town clearly carries on without me: people close up, run errands, walk the length of the
  street, meet at the livery, go for a drink. Nobody got stuck.
- The lines are good: short, dry, period, a little funny ("Too early for the piano. Too late for
  church." / "A second saloon." "I heard different.").
- Pairs answer each other (liveryman and smith on the feed bill; barkeep and miner on the beer).

## Top three to fix

1. E at a door should knock, not break the glass; and the knock should be offered when you stand
   at the door facing it, not only while walking past.
2. Breaking a shopkeeper's door in front of him should get a reaction (a word, a look, a change in
   how he feels about you).
3. People going to meet someone who has already gone in should not stand there talking to nobody;
   and the in-and-out at doors (the liveryman) is too frequent to read as natural.
