# Playtest 2026-11-21: the morning after a killing

Blind playtest, headless (`--world=valley,--no-murder`), driven through the dev bridge. The question:
**"The morning after a killing: what does the town think happened? Is it what happened?"**
No screenshots (headless draws nothing); everything below is from `read people`, `events`, `story`
and, at the very end, `read news`.

## What I did

- **12:00, day 1.** Set the clock to noon. Main Street had only four people listed in the whole valley:
  the loudmouth alone on the saloon porch, a townsman by the hotel, the undertaker at his shop, and the
  engineer on the train. Nobody had anyone within 20 m. Trying other hours, 11:00 was the best: the
  loudmouth and the store wife side by side (0.8 m apart) on the general store's porch, the liveryman at
  the livery across the street. So I acted at about 11:40 instead of noon.
- **11:39, the shot.** I walked to the street in front of the store (-44, -1), 10 m from the porch, drew
  the revolver and shot the loudmouth. Three shots, all mine:
  1. From the hip: missed, hit the boardwalk. The store wife said "Take it! Take whatever you want!" as I
     drew; the loudmouth "Don't shoot! I got a family!"; the liveryman "Get down! Everybody down!"
  2. Aimed, chest: hit him, went through him and hit the store wife in the left arm. Loudmouth: "He shot
     me! Oh God, he shot me!" Store wife: "I'm hit! Lord, I'm hit!" Then, 1.6 s later with no shot
     fired, "store_wife hit in the thigh_l" and "Ah! What hit me?"
  3. Aimed, head: "loudmouth died (brain)", 11:42 game time, on the porch at about (-46, -9.6).
- **Who was where at the shot** (no facings given by the bridge):

  - liveryman: at the livery, 22 m across the street; "shot_seen", ducked and cowered, then gone indoors.
  - smith: at the blacksmith's, 41-47 m west; "shot_seen", carried on working.

  - Nobody else was listed anywhere in town.
- **Right after.** I put the gun away. 11:47 undertaker (150 m off): "Who's shooting?"; 11:53 townsman,

- **12:40-13:57.** Walked to the saloon steps (55 m off) and waited two real minutes. At 13:57 the body

## My view before `read news`

What happened: I shot the loudmouth dead in front of the store, three shots, and wounded the store wife.
What the town thinks: the new man (me) shot the loudmouth by the store, three shots. That is right, and
mostly right for the right reasons:

- The two who were near enough to see (store wife, smith) name me, him, the store, three shots.
- The liveryman, who ducked, knows only "shooting by the store, three shots", not that anyone died. Good.
- The two far men who only heard it (townsman, undertaker) put it at the wagonwright, across the street.
  A believable mistake. The undertaker, who fetched the body, knows it was the loudmouth but not who did it.
- Second-hand people say "they say" and name their source. Good gossip feel.

## Compared with `read news`

- The record is true: killing, day 1 11:40, the store, 3 shots, by me, of the loudmouth.
- 18 people have a version; I could only have seen 7 of them in town. Ten were told by someone I never
  saw them meet: earl, drifter, fiddler, landlady, miner, rancher, storekeeper, churchman, doctor, barkeep.
  The miner (who told the drifter) never appeared in `read people` once; nor did earl, drifter, fiddler,
  landlady, rancher or storekeeper, though earl and drifter talked over cards in the saloon at night.
- Wrong in places, as gossip should be: churchman and earl say 4 shots, drifter 2; three have lost where it
  happened; the undertaker's tellings carry "the wagonwright" on to the churchman and the landlady.
- Undertaker's version says he "saw" it. He was 161 m off and only heard it. He did see the body when he
  fetched it, but his place for it is still "the wagonwright", not the store porch he took the body from.
- `talks: 0` in the news, yet 13 people are marked "told".
- The barkeep was told by the smith. I watched the smith at his forge all afternoon and the barkeep wasn't
  in town; if they met, it was off-screen overnight.

## Answer

The town thinks the new man shot the loudmouth dead by the store, with three shots. That is what happened.
The witnesses know only what they could see, the far listeners get the place wrong, and the second-hand
tellings drift a little (2 or 4 shots). That's believable. What's missing is any feeling about it: nobody
next morning is afraid of the man who did it, nobody mentions the store wife being shot, and they tell the
killer his own deed to his face in a flat, cheerful line.

## What felt wrong or broken

- The town at noon is nearly empty: 4 people in the whole valley, no one within 20 m of anyone else. People
  appear and disappear by the hour instead of walking about (pop in when the clock is set).
- One missed revolver ball broke 183 members of the store's boardwalk and knocked both porch benches loose.
- The store wife was "hit in the thigh_l" 1.6 s after my second shot, with no shot fired.
- The ball through the loudmouth's chest went on and wounded the store wife; afterwards nobody, not the
  town and not the news, ever mentions she was shot. She healed to 0 wounds overnight.
- Nobody went to the body or to the wounded woman while I watched. "Stand back, give him air" was said by a
  man 80 m away walking the other way.
- The body vanished with no event while I was 55 m away. Undertaker never seen carrying anything.
- The morning after, everyone is calm and speaks to me, the killer, as if passing the time of day:
  "You shot the loudmouth dead... Three shots, I counted." No fear, no anger, no sheriff.
- Identical lines from different people ("Three shots, I counted" from the store wife, smith and undertaker).
- The victim is only ever "the loudmouth": no name, no one sorry he's dead.
- The news includes many people I never saw anywhere (miner, earl, drifter, fiddler, landlady, rancher,
  storekeeper); they're told things by people I never saw them meet.
- I couldn't tell which day it was: `time 32` gave 08:00 with no sign of the day; `get day` didn't work.
- `walk pianist` stopped short and `walk barkeep` stopped on the wrong side of the bar.
