## What I did

- Started at the middle of the street. At t=0.1 the log already showed 30 crates at the general store "went loose (fell whole)". I had done nothing yet.
- Walked to the livery (t=23) where the townsman and the loudmouth stood. They were on exactly the same spot, [-23.0, -0.12, 9.6], one inside the other. I stood with them for 30 s. The townsman walked off east down the street.
- Walked to the general store (t=71) and tried to knock (see below). I broke the door's glass by accident. I stayed about 50 s with the storekeeper.
- Walked to the saloon. "walk barkeep" stopped me outside a wall (a framing stud between us). I went round by the steps and got in at t=283. I stood at the bar by the miner, barkeep and loudmouth for about 45 s.
- Walked back to the livery (t=361) and stood by the liveryman for about 140 s. After t=372 nobody said anything at all.
- Stopped the game at t=506.

## The knock

- At t=22 near the livery the screen said "E: bang on the door". At the store I never saw that prompt.
- First `key E` at the store door from 1.9 m (t=77): "you shoved at nothing: a miss". No knock.
- I stepped up to 0.7 m, facing the door's glass. Second `key E` (t=91): "you shoved valley_general_store/front/recess/entry_leaf/pane: it broke". **E at the door shoved it and smashed the glass.** My knock count stayed at 0 both times.
- Who came: the storekeeper. He had been sitting on the bench out front, not inside. At t=98.8 he walked over to 1.7 m from me and said "Near closing. Whatever you want, want it now."
- Odd timing: at t=72.1, right as I arrived and before any E, he said "Directly. I'm in the middle of something." It reads like an answer to a knock that hadn't happened yet.
- When I stayed, he said only "Dry as a bone. We could use a rain." (t=129). He never mentioned the broken glass. Then he went into the store. Later he was back on the bench.

## Lines and who talked to whom

- **25 spoken lines in about 500 s of game time**, all in the first 372 s. In the last 130 s, nothing was said.
- Mostly to each other, in pairs: townsman/loudmouth, storekeeper/store wife, barkeep/townsman, miner/barkeep (twice), loudmouth/barkeep, liveryman/smith, miner/barkeep ("Night").
- The rest were lines said to no one in particular: "Long afternoon.", "Evening.", the saloon girl's two porch lines, the storekeeper's closing and weather lines.
- Only the storekeeper's lines seemed meant for me. Nobody else addressed me.
- Replies that read oddly:
  - Storekeeper to his own wife: "Got a bolt of blue calico in. Your wife'll want it." She answered "Well, I never." He's telling his wife about *her* wife.
  - Barkeep: "You drinking, or holding my bar up?" Townsman: "I heard different." That reply doesn't fit.
  - Liveryman: "Feed bill's due. I'm not a charity." Smith: "Well, I'll be." That's weak but passable.
  - Miner: "That drifter plays cards like he's got nowhere to be." Barkeep: "Ha! That'll be the day." That's a slight miss.
  - Townsman "Long afternoon." and loudmouth "Evening." 30 s apart, at 17:30.
- The miner said "Night, all." about 6 minutes after I started, which felt early for an early evening.

3. **Talk that doesn't fit and people who don't move:**
