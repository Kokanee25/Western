# Blind critic 4: candidates W, X, Y, Z against the character sheet and the two paintings

Crops made in /tmp/claude-0/critic2/ (heads_front, heads_golden, wx, saloon_man, wall, street_top, sheet_coat).

## 1. Against the character sheet (closest first)

1. **Z**: the only one that keeps a realistically modelled face and figure while breaking every surface into small flat patches, which is the sheet's idea, though its patches are ragged blobs rather than the sheet's squares.
2. **Y**: the faces, hands and clothes are as realistic as the sheet's, but there is no mosaic at all; it reads as a plain smooth 3D render.
3. **X**: it has a mosaic, but the blocks on the face are four or five times the sheet's size and wipe out the anatomy. The eyes, nose and moustache become crude slabs.
4. **W**: the same face as X, and its world blocks are bigger and noisier still (the props floor, the noon wall).

## 2. Against the saloon and street paintings (closest first)

1. **X**: square blocks on every surface with the plank seams kept, and the JAIL and SALOON lettering stays crisp. The seated man's bold blocky face is near the saloon painting's man.
2. **W**: just as bold, and closer to the paintings' chunkiness, but its walls go to confetti with no plank lines, and the JAIL sign vanishes from the street shot.
3. **Z**: the light is right, but its smeared patches read as a watercolour filter, not the paintings' squares.
4. **Y**: a smooth, ordinary render. The floor and table grain is blurred streaks, and there are no blocks anywhere.

## 3. Worst failures against the sheet, biggest first

**W**
- heads.jpg, every view of both men: blocks about 1/8 of the face wide. The moustache is a black brick and the eyes are pasted-on whites, where the sheet has fine squares that keep every wrinkle.
- characters.jpg, front views: the coat is a flat orange-tan with little contrast; the sheet's coat is near-black, mud-streaked wool under a hard key.
- facade.jpg, noon wall (right column): big grey, blue and white confetti blocks with no boards, nothing like the sheet's ordered texture swatches.

**X**
- heads.jpg: the same oversized face blocks as W, which give the young man's face a lumpy, half-melted look.
- characters.jpg: clothes too bright, saturated and evenly lit; the shadows don't sink.
- saloon.jpg, the man's 2x crop: jagged cream fragments break up his collar and shirt.

**Y**
- heads.jpg and characters.jpg: no squares anywhere. Skin and cloth are smooth gradients, the opposite of the sheet's mosaic.
- heads.jpg, golden hour: plastic, glossy-lit faces with a hot orange cheek; the sheet's light is hard but the skin stays matte.
- props.jpg and saloon.jpg: the wood is motion-blurred smears, not textured blocks.

**Z**
- heads.jpg, hat bands and brims: the patches are irregular, soft-edged blobs (a Kuwahara-like smear). The sheet's are crisp squares on a grid.
- characters.jpg, coat: a blotchy mid-brown camouflage. The sheet's coat is dark with rust mud and real weave squares.
- props.jpg, the floor: a busy dappled noise that fights the objects standing on it.

## 4. What each does best

- **W**: the boldest, most readable chunky blocks; in a still frame its saloon is the most "pixel art".
- **X**: the most balanced world. The squares sit on the planks, seams and lettering survive, and the boards read as wood.
- **Y**: the cleanest forms and the most believable faces, hands and cloth folds. The young man's face is the best drawn of all four.
- **Z**: the nearest to the sheet's surface feel on the people: real form under a broken, painterly surface, worn and dirty-looking.

## 5. What separates Z (the closest) from the sheet

1. **Surface treatment.** The sheet covers everything (skin, felt, wool, leather, ground) in small crisp squares of a steady size, each a distinct shade. Z's patches are soft, ragged blobs of uneven size, so they read as a filter rather than as squares.
2. **Light and value.** The sheet has a hard warm key with deep, near-black shadows and a dark coat. All the candidates, Z included, are lit flat and bright, with orange-tan clothes and lifted shadows; the sheet's figure has far more contrast.
3. **Colour and material.** The sheet's palette is muted (charcoal, rust, oxblood, cream, with mud and grime streaking the clothes and boots). Z's is a uniform saturated orange-brown with no grime and no dark garments, and its boots don't read as worn leather.

## 6. Where candidates look the same

- W and X are almost identical on the people (the same blocky faces, coats and colours); they differ mainly in world textures.
- Y and X are close on the walls and planks at a distance; Y only loses the squares up close.
- All four share the composition, the lavender sky with orange streaks, the bright flat light on the figures, the saloon's warm murk, the lamp, and the fact that their clothes are too light and saturated against the sheet.
