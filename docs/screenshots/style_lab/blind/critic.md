# Blind critic: candidates X, Y, Z

I judged from the seven boards and the three targets only.

## 1. Ranking (closest to the target style first)

1. **Y**: it is the only one with the targets' mosaic everywhere. Faces, hats, coats, floor and walls are all in visible flat squares, and the boards and floor are a clean, calm grain, not noise.
2. **Z**: the men are the same as Y's, but its world textures are coarser and noisier. The noon wall breaks into blue-white confetti, and the JAIL sign is lost in the street shot.
3. **X**: everything is drawn smooth. The faces and cloth look like plain realistic game renders and the floors are smeared, with almost none of the paintings' square-by-square mosaic.

## 2. Worst failures

**X**
1. heads.jpg, every X cell, and saloon.jpg, the crop of the seated man: smooth, photographic CG faces and coats with no squares at all. The young man's face has smeared texture seams across the nose and cheek (daylight cell).
2. props.jpg, X row, both panels: the floor and back wall are blurred, streaky smears, worst on the close table top. They aren't the painting's crisp board squares.
3. walk.jpg, X row: the floor reads as glossy, streaked plastic, and the seated man looks waxy, not painted.

**Y**
1. heads.jpg and saloon.jpg, the crop of the seated man: the stair-stepped blocks on his jaw, collar and moustache are too big and uneven for the face. The shirt collar breaks into black-and-white jaggies, and the mouth area smears into a mask. The painting's face keeps its drawing under the squares.
2. heads.jpg, the golden-hour and lamplight cells: skin goes a hot, flat orange with hard, poster-like patches. The young man's face splits into flat facets.
3. props.jpg, Y row, close panel: the floor squares are busy and high-contrast, close to confetti, and the wall is dull brown blocks with little grain.

**Z**
1. facade.jpg, the noon wall close-up (right column): the siding is high-contrast blue-white blotches with no board structure. It is the noisiest surface on any board.
2. facade.jpg, the street shot: the JAIL sign is gone or illegible, and the store front at dusk loses its lettering against coarse blocks.
3. heads.jpg and saloon.jpg: the same face problems as Y (blocky jaw and collar, an orange mask). props.jpg: the floor blocks are large and soft, so the close table reads as big blurry tiles.

## 3. What each does best

- **X**: the cleanest, most readable faces and silhouettes, and the closest to the character sheet's realistic forms. The lamp glass is smooth and its glow is nice.
- **Y**: the most faithful mosaic. The hats and coats in squares look like the saloon painting's man, the eyes read as bright whites with dark irises, and the façade boards and floor have a clean drawn grain. The best overall match to the saloon and street paintings.
- **Z**: the bold, flat floor and far-wall blocks in the props and walk boards are nearer the saloon painting's chunky scale than Y's. Its hat crowns read well in blocks.

## 4. Readability in play

- **At a distance** (kid_street.jpg, the far panel): all three read about equally. The young man is clear in every one.
- **Up close**: X's man reads most cleanly. Y's and Z's faces are readable but blotchier.
- **The walk** (walk.jpg): the seated man reads in all three. In Y and Z his face carries visible stair-steps and blocks that look likely to crawl as the camera moves.
- **Smear and mush**: X's floor smears into streaks in the last frames. Z's floor is the flattest and largest-blocked. In stills I see no outright shimmer in any of the three; motion would need checking.

## 5. Where they look the same

- **characters.jpg**: Y and Z are nearly identical, and at this size X differs only by being smoother. All three share the same lighting and colour, including the over-orange golden-hour side views.
- **kid_street.jpg**: the three differ only slightly, mainly on the road surface.
- **facade.jpg**: the street shot and the 2× saloon-front crop are almost the same in all three, apart from Z's lost JAIL sign. The dusk front differs only faintly.
- **saloon.jpg**: the full shot and the card-table crop are close in all three. The real differences are on the seated man's face and on the table and wall behind the lamp.
- **Shared across all three**: the sky, the light and the composition. The candidates differ mostly in how textures and faces are cut into squares.
