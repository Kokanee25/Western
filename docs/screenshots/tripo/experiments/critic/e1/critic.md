# Blind critic: e1b

Overall impression first: A, B and C are almost the same picture. They share the same drawing, the
same lighting and the same failures. They differ only in how some squares are toned and in fine
fringes along edges. All three sit far from the master on every feature. Against the master:

- The squares are slanted, stretched into slivers or trapezoids, and soft-lit inside, not flat
  tiles on a steady grid.
- The features are not drawn: the eyes are dark blobs with star-shaped glints, the ear is a slab,
  and the collar is cream mush.

The scores separate the three panels by half a point at most. (Scores are 1 to 5; .5 means
between two levels.)

## Final ranking (all features)

1. **C:** the squares in the cheek, nose bridge, throat and chin are lit more evenly as single
   tiles. There is no bright sliver down the nose and the throat has fewer blemish spots. It loses
   on the eyes, which have a crumbly fringe of half-lit pixels round the near eye.
2. **B:** almost identical to A, with a slightly weaker nose-bridge streak and fewer throat spots.
   It still has soft, slanted squares, blob eyes, a slab ear and a mushy collar.
3. **A:** it has the most non-square artefacts: the nose-bridge sliver, blemish spots on the
   throat, the busiest striated brim edge and shredded chips at the coat's corner. It is otherwise
   the same as B.

**Common to all three**, and far bigger than the gaps between them:

- The squares are slanted and soft-lit, not a steady grid of flat tiles.
- The key features are not drawn at the finer level the master uses: the eyes have no whites,
  iris or lid; the moustache has no philtrum gap; the ear has no rim or hollow; the collar has no
  edge or shadow.
- Star-shaped specular glints in the eyes read as sparkles.
