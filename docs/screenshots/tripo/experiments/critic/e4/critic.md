# Blind critic, e4

Panels A, B and C are close relatives. B and C are almost the same picture: most of what separates them is a one-pixel shift. Their only real differences are at the collar's lower left and in the coat lapel. A is a different render. Its light is hotter and more saturated, its pale areas are whiter, and its dark shapes break into more fragments.

Scores run from 1 to 5: Reg = square regularity, Crisp = crispness, 3D = 3D form, Read = semantic readability, Style = the master's style.

- **Best (B and C) against the master:** the master's face is a steady grid of square blocks of even size, and the jaw, cheek and nose edges step in those blocks. ## Overall ranking

1. **B:** in every feature its colour is calmer and closer to the master than A's, and its collar and neckerchief are the least fragmented. Its blocks are still slanted and uneven, its eye whites are sparks, and its collar has no points or shadow.
2. **C:** nearly the same render as B. It has a few more stray speckles in the lapel and neckerchief at the collar's lower left, otherwise tied.
3. **A:** hotter, blown highlights on the cheek, nose, ear and shirt. The collar and neckerchief shatter into black islands and white chips, the lapel breaks into a fan of stripes, and the eye glints are scattered stars. It is furthest from the master's calm, even blocks.

## Common to all three

The master's squares are even and lie on one steady grid. The renders' squares slant, stretch and shear along the surfaces: on the cheeks, the brows, the hat brim and the lapel. The finer drawing the master keeps is missing in all of them: no eye almonds with whites, no nostrils, no gap in the moustache, no ear rim, and no collar points with their shadow.
