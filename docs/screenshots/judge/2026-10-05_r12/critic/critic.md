# Blind critic, 2026-10-05_r12

1. **The seated man's face is an orange smear with no eyes** (saloon). The painting's man looks up at you from under the brim: two clear eyes with whites and catchlights, drawn brows, a firm moustache and jaw, all in tidy square tiles, with his hat's studded band crisp above. The render's man has his head tipped down so the brim hides his eyes, and the face is burnt orange and dark brown blotches, the moustache a black stain and the cheek blown out. You can't meet his eye, so the shot loses its centre and the man reads as a mask, not a character. Crop: `critic/1_face.png`
2. **The room behind him is brown murk, not a busy lamplit saloon** (saloon). The painting's back half is full of life: a stair and balcony with a man on it, a stag head, framed pictures, sconces each lighting its own patch of wall, haze in the air, a piano player, men leaning on the bar and a barkeep before a glowing mirror and bottle wall. The render has a dark wall with a few dim sconce glows, a stair you can barely make out, two figures standing as dark shapes in the middle, and a shelf of bottles in shadow. It feels empty and gloomy where the painting feels warm and crowded. Crop: `critic/3_room.png`
3. **The saloon's front is dull, shaded and its sign unreadable** (street). In the painting the big SALOON board is pale sunlit wood with bold red letters you read at once, and the porch posts and fascia catch gold light. In the render the whole front is a flat grey-brown in shade, the lettering a dark scribble you can't make out, and the other fronts (GENERAL STORE, JAIL, LIVERY) are lost or cut off too, so the street doesn't tell you what town it is. Crop: `critic/4_fronts.png`
4. **The sky is pale lavender with thin orange streaks and a small white sun** (street). The painting's sky glows gold to blue, with heaped, sunlit cloud banks, and a big orange sun sits low on the horizon between the red spires, throwing rays and dust down the street. The render's sun is a small white dot high up behind the saloon's roof, the sky a cool grey-violet with flat orange dashes, and the rocks at the end are dark and small. The golden-hour glow that makes the painting is missing. Crop: `critic/2_sky.png`
5. **Surfaces break into streaky smears, not clean squares** (both, worst on the saloon's bar front and table). The painting is cut into even small squares everywhere, so wood grain, the table top and the man's tweed coat all read as a tidy mosaic of close tones. In the render big flat areas break into ragged horizontal smears and blotches (the bar front behind the lamp, the table, the walls), while some things (the lamp's glass, the bottle) are smooth, so it looks like a posterised photo rather than pixel art. Crop: `critic/5_streaks.png`

**What's closest:** the saloon's lamp, whose glass glows near-white over the flame in squares with warm light pooled on the table, and the overall framing of both shots (the man at the table with the card game by the moonlit door; the street running to red rock with the revolver in hand) are already the paintings'. The street's sunlit ochre road with its wheel ruts is close in colour too.
