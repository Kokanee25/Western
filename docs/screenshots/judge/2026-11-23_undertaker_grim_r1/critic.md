# Blind critic, the undertaker's cellar made grim (round 1)

## Per view
- embalming: DIFFERENT: the painting is a whitewashed stone cellar lit by one hanging lantern, crowded with the trade's kit (zinc-topped table, pump, hose, jars, demijohns, linen). The render is a dark, orange, plank-walled room with tiled floor and partitions to further rooms. Its tables are bare and almost nothing on them says embalming.
- stair: DIFFERENT: the painting looks up a worn, dusty stair from inside the cellar, with the lit stone wall, lantern and chair beside it. The render looks down a narrow hatch from a clean, glossy upper floor, so the cellar is only a dark slot at the bottom.

## Used and stained
- embalming: Partly. The right-hand wall planks have scattered dark spots and the floor tiles are mottled, so it isn't brand new. But the tables are clean, flat and uniform. There's no drip staining under the table, no rings, spills or tide-marks round a drain, and no soot or damp on the walls. The darkness hides more than it shows, so it reads as a gloomy storeroom rather than years of grim work.
- stair: No. The upper floor is evenly varnished and has a sheen, like a polished hall. The stair treads are greyer and slightly scuffed, but the dust, cobwebs, worn tread edges and whitewash grime that carry the painting are missing. The cellar below is too dark to show anything.

## The five biggest differences
1. **Wall material: whitewashed rubble stone vs dark wood planking** (embalming `780 180 340 330`): the painting's walls are lumpy, lime-washed stone with brown damp and smudges, and they glow pale under the lantern. The render's walls are vertical brown boards, so the room reads as timber and very dark instead of a pale stone cellar.
2. **The embalming table and its kit are missing** (embalming `440 390 480 200`): the painting's centrepiece is a galvanised, raised-lip tray table with a drain hole and a bucket under it, next to a pump, red hose, instruments on linen, jars and amber bottles. The render's table is a flat, dark, featureless slab with slatted shelves below. Nothing on it or near it says bodies are prepared here.
3. **Camera: looking down a hatch from above vs looking up the stair from the cellar** (stair `500 130 300 430`): the painting shows the steep, worn stair from below, with the lit cellar corner (lantern, chair under a black cloth, stand, table) taking up half the frame. The render shows the stair as a narrow, dark slot in a big floor, with a baluster rail the painting doesn't have, and no cellar contents can be read.
4. **Floor and clutter: dirt, planks, baskets and demijohns vs bare tiles** (embalming `820 470 460 250`): the painting's floor is packed earth with a floor drain and a strip of planks, crowded with wicker-cased jugs of pale fluid. The render's floor is clean square tiles with a little stray clutter, mostly empty and lit orange.
5. **Light: one bright hanging lantern on pale stone vs a small wall sconce in heavy orange murk** (embalming `840 240 110 130`): the painting's lantern hangs from the beam at the centre and throws warm, readable light over the whole room. The render's light is a small sconce on the right wall. Most of the frame, the left third especially, falls into near-black brown, and the colour is a uniform orange rather than warm light against cool grey stone.
