# Blind critic, 2026-10-05_r6

1. **The man's face is in the dark** (saloon). The painting's man is the brightest thing in the frame after the lamp: warm peach skin on the lamp side, white eyeballs with dark irises looking straight at you, a clean dark moustache, the hat's crown and studded band catching light. The render's man is a near-black silhouette with a muddy red-brown face, no eye whites, the eyes a dark smudge under the brim, his coat the same dull brown as the wall behind him. The lamp beside him lights the table top bright orange but not him, so the picture's subject reads as unlit and unfriendly where the painting's is a lit portrait. Crop: `critic/1_man_face.png`
2. **The sky is pale blue with white confetti clouds, not a gold sunset** (street). The painting's sky runs from a big orange sun on the horizon between the red spires, with rays and dust, up through heaped gold-pink clouds to a deep blue; it is what the whole picture is lit by. The render's is a lavender-grey blue with flat cream and white streaks scattered like torn paper in huge blocks, and the sun is a small white blob low over the roofs on the left with no spires behind it. The mood is late afternoon haze instead of golden hour. Crop: `critic/2_sky.png`
3. **The room behind him is brown murk** (saloon). In the painting the upper half of the room glows: lamps on sconces with warm halos lighting their own patches of wood, a balcony railing with a man on it, a window of night sky, smoke, pictures, the stair, all in warm reddish wood. The render has the balcony and lamps, but the wood is one flat mud-brown, the ceiling is black, the lamps are pale blobs that light nothing round them, and there is no smoke or warmth in the air. A viewer sees a dark box with a bar on the right rather than a busy lamplit saloon. Crop: `critic/3_room_light.png`
4. **The saloon's front is in dull shade and the sign can't be read; the whole street is paler and flatter** (street). The painting's left third is a lit cream and red false front with SALOON in crisp red letters, lamps burning on the porch, a man leaning in the doorway. The render's is a grey-brown wedge of shadow with streaky wood grain and the sign a smear of darker brown nobody could read; the far fronts, the people and the horse are dark silhouettes, and the near road is a washed salmon-pink. The painting is deep orange-gold with rich dark shadows; the render is lighter, less saturated and lower in contrast everywhere. Crop: `critic/4_saloon_front.png`
5. **The squares are not one tidy mosaic** (both). The paintings are cut into small, even, crisp squares of flat colour, each thing drawn in them (the road's ruts are rows of blocks, the clouds heaps of blocks). The render's block size swings wildly: the saloon's doorway is a giant white square moon over huge grey-blue chequers (`critic/5_doorway_moon.png`), the sky's clouds are big rectangular streaks, while the road is a fine dark-red speckle with no readable ruts and the wood of the fronts breaks into horizontal streaks rather than squares. It reads as noise and compression rather than a drawn pixel picture. Crop: `critic/6_ground.png`

**What's closest:** the saloon's framing and set are the painting's (the round table, the tall glass lamp with its bright flame and dark brass foot, the bottle, the card game by the door, the bottle wall and barman on the right), and the street's layout, the revolver at the hip, the rail with horses and the water trough are in the right places; the lamp's glass and flame are the one thing in the saloon render that already looks like the painting's.
