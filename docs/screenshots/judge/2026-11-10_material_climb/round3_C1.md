# Materials judge: C1 (planed pine lap siding), sets P and Q

Blind judgment of the crops in this folder against the yardstick's wood (RDR2 street and saloon,
Hunt 1896, KCD2). Crop boxes are `x y w h` in the named crop's own pixels.

Noted once, not graded: the sun's shadow along the laps is saw-toothed in every near crop (e.g.
`C1_few_Q_near.png` 0 235 1400 20). In `C1_old_P_near.png` one lap's shadow is also a row of soft
blobs (0 475 1400 35); I took that as the same fault of the light.

Also noted: both `few` far crops have two or three bright orange, glossy, strongly figured boards
at the top (0 0 560 65). If they belong to the swatch they fail "Board to board" (they read as a
different, varnished wood); I have counted them there. If they are a neighbouring swatch caught in
the crop, that FAIL falls away for both sets equally and no grade changes.

## C1_few, set P

| check | result | evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Real stuff | PASS | Reads as weathered softwood: knots, raised grain. Small violet/pink flecks if hunted (near 1370 240 30 40). |
| 2 Age reads | PASS | Clearly younger than old P: no checks, no nail rust. |
| 3 No tile tells | FAIL | Far: the same U-shaped knot mark twice on one board, about 400 px apart (far 40 225 30 35 and 435 225 30 35); again on the board at y 88 (far 105 78 25 25 and 505 78 25 25). |
| 4 Board to board | FAIL | Far: top boards a different glossy orange wood (far 0 0 560 65). Below them the boards are almost one tone (near 0 110 1400 480). |
| 5 Edges and joints | PASS | Laps read as shadowed gaps, no drawn outline. |
| 6 Holds at distance | PASS | Keeps grain and knots at 5 m, no shimmer or card. |

**Grade: game.** Change that would move it up most: break the repeat along the board (offset or
swap the figure per board so the same knot never appears twice in one course) and give boards real
tone differences.

## C1_few, set Q

| check | result | evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Real stuff | FAIL | Blue-violet cast and blotches over the grey read as a tint, not as sun-greyed pine (near 60 150 420 140; near 1050 480 250 80). Pale chips and orange dots look stuck on (near 380 325 60 45). |
| 2 Age reads | FAIL | Barely differs from old Q: same grey-violet board, old only adds nail dots. |
| 3 No tile tells | FAIL | Far: the same small mark repeats about 400 px apart on one board (far 0 255 25 20 and 398 255 25 20). |
| 4 Board to board | FAIL | Far: top boards a different glossy orange wood (far 0 0 560 65); the rest one tone. |
| 5 Edges and joints | PASS | Laps are shadowed gaps. |
| 6 Holds at distance | FAIL | Far: the lower boards go to a fine dotted/woven mush, grain lost (far 0 225 560 110). |

**Grade: game** (low; close to fake). Change that would move it up most: take the blue-violet out
of the grey so it reads as silvered pine, warm in the hollows.

**few: P is better than Q, within a grade** (both game; P is a firm game, Q a weak one).

## C1_old, set P

| check | result | evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Real stuff | PASS | Reads as old softwood siding. The checks are even-width dark pen lines with no depth or lit edge if hunted (near 870 135 160 25). |
| 2 Age reads | PASS | Clearly older than few P: checks and splits along the grain, rusted nail heads in stud lines, darker dirt at the laps. Not much sun-greying. |
| 3 No tile tells | PASS | No repeat, seam or weave found near or far. |
| 4 Board to board | PASS | Borderline: boards differ in checks and figure, little in tone. |
| 5 Edges and joints | PASS | Laps are shadowed gaps (blobby shadow noted above as the light's). |
| 6 Holds at distance | FAIL | Far: the checks become wavy scribbled lines on nearly every board, all one weight (far 0 0 560 110; far 380 250 180 100). Reads as drawn on. |

**Grade: game** (high; the best of the four). Change that would move it up most: fewer, straighter
checks that follow the grain with a shadowed side and a lit side, on some boards and not others.

## C1_old, set Q

| check | result | evidence |

| 5 Edges and joints | PASS | Laps are shadowed gaps. |
| 6 Holds at distance | PASS | Borderline: grain is soft but it stays boards, less dotted than few Q. |

**Grade: game** (low). Change that would move it up most: give it real age (checks and splits, as P
has, done with depth) and take out the violet.

**old: P is better than Q, within a grade** (both game, but P is near the top of it and Q near the
bottom; almost a full grade).

## Overall

P is the better set at both ages. Neither set reaches "near the yardstick": the references' old
boards (Hunt's hull planks, RDR2's porch post and deck) have open grain with relief, silver-grey
without violet, and checks with depth. Best age: old P.
