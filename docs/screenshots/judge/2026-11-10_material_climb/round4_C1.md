# Materials judge: q4_C1

Blind judgment of the crops in this folder against the AAA yardstick (RDR2, Hunt 1896, KCD2 wood).
Noted once: the saw-toothed edge of the sun's shadow under the deep lap (near crops, y ~245) is the
light's known fault and is not counted against either set.

## C1_new (Planed pine lap siding), set P

Crops: `C1_new_P_near.png`, `C1_new_P_far.png`

| check | result | evidence (x y w h) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Real stuff | PASS | Reads as flat-sawn softwood. Weak pass: the latewood is so contrasty and raised that it reads brushed or rotary-peeled (a plywood face) more than planed pine. |
| 2 Age reads | PASS | One age only. Clean, unweathered, yellow: believable as new. No handling dirt or mill marks at all, which is thin but not wrong for new. |
| 3 No tile tells | PASS | No repeat, seam, block or weave found at 1 m. |
| 4 Board to board | FAIL | near `440 250 320 240`: the cathedral figure runs across the lap at y ~370 as if two boards were one sheet. Every board is the same tone and the same wild figure; none is quiet, straight-grained, darker or paler. |
| 5 Edges and joints | FAIL | near `0 470 1400 40` and `0 575 1400 30`: these laps are hairlines with no board thickness or shadow, against the one deep lap at y ~245; the wall's laps are inconsistent. Nails `410 30 60 50`: flat blue-grey discs, no dimple, no rust, no shadow, in a ruled grid. |
| 6 Holds at distance | FAIL | far `0 0 560 360`: at 5 m it is a flat orange card of printed grain. The figure is far too large and loud for the board height (swirls `180 150 220 60`), the laps are pencil lines, and it reads as laminate or plywood panelling, not siding. |

**Grade: game** (low in the grade: it reads as wood up close, but three clear tells).

## C1_new: P against Q

**Q is better, within a grade only.** Q's grain is finer and calmer, its figure doesn't visibly run
across a lap, and two of its laps carry a little more shadow. The same three checks fail in both.

**The one change that would move C1_new up most:** make it read as separate boards at 5 m: give
each board its own tone and figure (some quiet and straight-grained, a few darker or paler), with
the figure scaled down to the board, and give every lap the same real thickness and shadow. That
turns the far crop from a printed panel into siding, and fixes checks 4, 5 and 6 together. (Next:
nails as dark, slightly sunk heads with a rust weep, not blue dots.)

Best age: only "new" was given.
