# Materials judge: q1_C1 (C1, planed pine lap siding; sets P and Q)

Blind judgment against the yardstick's wood (RDR2 street noon/golden and saloon, Hunt 1896 street
and saloon, KCD2 street). Crop boxes are `x y w h` in the named crop.

Noted once, not graded: the sun's shadow along the laps is saw-toothed (a twisted-rope edge) in
every near crop, e.g. `C1_new_Q_near.png` 0 235 1400 25. A fault of the light, not the material.

## C1_new

### Set P

1. Real stuff: PASS (marginal). Real scanned grain with soft late-wood edges, but the cathedral
   figure is very bold and broad for planed pine: it leans to ash or rotary-cut plywood veneer.
2. Age reads: PASS. Clearly new wood, clean and warm; far apart from the other two ages.
3. No tile tells: PASS. No repeat, seam, blocks or weave found at 1 m.
4. Board to board: PASS. Boards differ in figure and tone; widths vary.
5. Edges and joints: FAIL. Only one lap casts a shadow; the others are just a jump in the grain
   with no gap, shadow or arris, so the wall reads as one printed sheet.
   `C1_new_P_near.png` 0 470 1400 150 (also 0 85 1400 40).
6. Holds at distance: PASS. Keeps strong figure at 5 m with no shimmer; reads rather varnished and
   orange, and the laps nearly vanish (`C1_new_P_far.png` 0 150 560 60).

Grade: **game**

### Set Q

1. Real stuff: FAIL. Pale pink-peach with a faint, even, embossed-looking grain: reads as
   wood-grain laminate or vinyl siding, not pine. `C1_new_Q_near.png` 0 250 700 250.
2. Age reads: PASS. Reads new.
3. No tile tells: PASS.
4. Board to board: PASS (marginal). Near, boards are almost one tone; at 5 m some tonal banding
   board to board saves it.
5. Edges and joints: PASS. Every lap has a shadowed gap at an even pitch (saw-toothed by the light).
6. Holds at distance: FAIL. At 5 m it is a flat peach card with ruled lines and nail dots; the
   grain is all but gone. `C1_new_Q_far.png` 0 0 560 360.

Grade: **game** (low end)

**Better: P, within a grade.** P reads as real wood with weak joints; Q has the joints but reads as
laminate.

**One change that would move C1_new up most:** give every lap of P a real shadowed step (Q's
joints on P's wood), and calm P's figure to a tighter, plainer pine grain.

## C1_few

### Set P

1. Real stuff: FAIL. The weathered grain itself is convincing, but violet-blue smudges sit over it
   that no wood has. `C1_few_P_near.png` 540 270 120 60; 1060 490 110 50.
2. Age reads: FAIL. Reads as old, dark grey-brown, heavily checked wood; near it is all but the same
   picture as C1_old (which only adds rusty nails). Nothing says "a few years" (some gold left,
   early greying). `C1_few_P_near.png` 0 0 1400 780.
3. No tile tells: PASS.
4. Board to board: PASS (marginal). Little change in tone board to board near; at 5 m a fresh
   orange board sits over weathered ones, which reads as a repair.
5. Edges and joints: PASS (marginal). Thin shadowed laps; faint, but not drawn on.
6. Holds at distance: PASS. Keeps knots, checks and chips at 5 m without shimmer.

Grade: **game**

### Set Q

1. Real stuff: FAIL. Same violet-blue smudges. `C1_few_Q_near.png` 540 270 120 60.
2. Age reads: FAIL. As P: reads old, not a few years; and the bright fresh strip on every board
   contradicts the weathered face. `C1_few_Q_near.png` 0 60 1400 200.
3. No tile tells: PASS.
4. Board to board: FAIL. Every board carries the same saturated orange-brown band, so the wall
   reads as stripes. `C1_few_Q_near.png` 0 60 1400 700.
5. Edges and joints: FAIL. The orange band along the bottom of each board is a coloured outline,
   and it sits on the exposed lower edge, the most weathered place on real lap siding (the sheltered
   strip would be tucked up under the board above). `C1_few_Q_near.png` 0 205 1400 45; 0 450 1400 40.
6. Holds at distance: FAIL. At 5 m the bands become bright orange pinstripes across the whole
   wall. `C1_few_Q_far.png` 0 120 560 240.

Grade: **fake**

**Better: P, by a full grade.**

**One change that would move C1_few up most:** make it a real middle age: lighter, with warm
pine still showing through an early silver-grey and only light checking, clearly between new and
old (and drop Q's orange bands and the violet smudges).

## C1_old

### Set P

1. Real stuff: FAIL. Deep checking, raised grain, knots and rust-bled nails read as real old
   softwood, but the same violet-blue smudges show. `C1_old_P_near.png` 1200 220 80 60;
   730 480 60 40; 110 40 100 50.
2. Age reads: PASS. Believably old: eroded grain, rust runs under the nails, chips. Would be
   stronger sun-silvered than this dark brown-grey; and it is too close to C1_few.
3. No tile tells: PASS.
4. Board to board: PASS (marginal). Boards barely differ near; at 5 m one fresher board breaks it up.
5. Edges and joints: FAIL. Several laps almost disappear; boards run into each other as one
   surface. `C1_old_P_near.png` 0 570 1400 50; 0 95 1400 30. At 5 m the lower half loses its
   boards (`C1_old_P_far.png` 0 200 560 160).
6. Holds at distance: PASS. Keeps character and knots, no shimmer; a little soft low down.

Grade: **game** (top of game: the best of the six)

### Set Q

1. Real stuff: FAIL. Same violet-blue smudges. `C1_old_Q_near.png` 1200 220 80 60.
2. Age reads: PASS. As P.
3. No tile tells: PASS.
4. Board to board: PASS (marginal). The brown bands are softer and uneven here, so it does not
   quite stripe near.
5. Edges and joints: FAIL. Warm brown bands along the lower edge of several boards; softer than
   in C1_few, but still colour laid along a joint in the wrong place.
   `C1_old_Q_near.png` 0 220 1400 40; 0 455 1400 45.
6. Holds at distance: PASS (marginal). The bands show as thin orange lines at 5 m
   (`C1_old_Q_far.png` 0 120 560 60) but the boards read more clearly than P's.

Grade: **game**

**Better: P, within a grade.** Q's laps separate the boards better, but by a coloured line; P has
no drawn-on tell, only weak joints.

**One change that would move C1_old up most:** deepen the lap shadow and add a worn, lighter
arris so each board reads as its own piece (no coloured band), then take out the violet smudges.

## Across the ages

New is clearly apart; few and old are nearly the same picture near (old adds rusty nails and a
little more relief). Best age: **C1_old set P**, then C1_new set P. None reaches the yardstick:
the references' boards each have their own shadowed edge and tone, and no off-colour patches.
