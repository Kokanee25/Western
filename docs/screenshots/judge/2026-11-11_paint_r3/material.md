# Materials judge: paint round 3 (2026-11-11_paint_r3)

Blind judge. Read: `docs/concept/judge/MATERIAL.md`, the six yardstick screenshots (RDR2 street noon,
street golden, saloon night; Hunt 1896 street noon, saloon night; KCD2 street noon), and the 24 crops
named in the brief. Crop boxes are `x y w h` in that crop's own pixels (near crops 1400x780, far crops
560x360).

**Yardstick, painted and bare wood:** RDR2's and Hunt's boards carry broad tonal variation board to
board, grime and water-staining concentrated low and at joints, and paint failure that follows the
wood (long strips at laps and ends, bare grey wood showing in sheets). Nothing in them is a uniform
scatter of small flecks.

**Known light fault, noted once:** the saw-toothed sun-shadow edge under every lap in P1 (and the
batten edges in P2) is visible in every near crop. Not counted against any swatch.

**General observations, all swatches:**
- The far crops of set P and set Q are pixel-identical for P1 (both ages) and P2 (both ages); only P4's
  far crops differ. P1/P2 far grades are therefore the same for P and Q.
- Set Q adds a fine craquelure (paint checking). On the few-years ages it is even and elongated along
  the grain, which is right. On P1_old and P4_old it is laid in discrete clumps/patches spaced at a
  regular interval across the boards, which reads as a stamped overlay.
- Every age, both sets: the chips are a near-uniform scatter of small flakes over the whole face of
  every board ("confetti"), not concentrated where water sits (a lap's lower edge, board ends, the foot).
  On the old ages some flakes do gather at the lap edges, which is the right idea, but the field
  scatter remains.
- Every "old" is still a sound film over ~85-95% of the face. None has failed in strips to primer and
  grey bare wood; old reads as "a few years, neglected", not old.

## P1: White lead in linseed oil, on planed pine lap siding

### P1_few, set P
1. Real stuff: **PASS**. A white film over planed pine; the figure shows faintly through (a little
   print-like, not a fail).
2. Age reads: **FAIL**. No chalking or checking along the grain; the loss is a uniform scatter of flecks
   over the boards' faces rather than at the lap edges. Box `0 120 1400 230`.
3. No tile tells: **PASS**. The faint grey nail-head discs line up in columns (x≈440, 890, 1290), which is
   where studs would be, so this is not a repeat.
4. Board to board: **PASS** (one even coat, the figure varies board to board).
5. Edges and joints: **PASS**. The laps read as shadowed gaps.
6. Holds at distance: **PASS**. Keeps its boards and specks, no shimmer. A little flat.
- **Grade: game.**

### P1_few, set Q
1. Real stuff: **PASS**.
2. Age reads: **FAIL**. The checking is now there (good, crop box `0 0 1400 360` shows it along the grain
   on the upper boards), but the flake scatter over the field is unchanged. Box `0 380 1400 220`.
3. No tile tells: **PASS**. The craquelure is even and follows the grain.
4. Board to board: **PASS**.
5. Edges and joints: **PASS**.
6. Holds at distance: **PASS** (identical to P's far crop).
- **Grade: game.**

**P vs Q (few):** Q is better, within a grade. The checking is the few-years signature P lacks.

### P1_old, set P
1. Real stuff: **FAIL**. The cracks are uniform-width black squiggles drawn across the boards, not
   following the grain: `1090 170 260 30`, `780 395 120 20`, `320 710 310 25`, `990 690 300 25`. The nail
   stains are identical pale-orange pin heads with tails (decals): `250 205 20 40`, `720 200 20 35`.
2. Age reads: **FAIL**. Distinct from few-years, but the film is still ~85% intact and white, with no
   strips lifted to grey bare wood. Box `0 250 1400 380`.
3. No tile tells: **PASS**.
4. Board to board: **PASS**. The raised figure varies board to board.
5. Edges and joints: **PASS**. The flakes gathered along the lap edges read as a film letting go
   there (the best part of this swatch).
6. Holds at distance: **PASS**. At 5 m the lap-edge flaking and the cracks keep it readable, with no
   shimmer.
- **Grade: game.**

### P1_old, set Q
1. Real stuff: **FAIL**. The same drawn squiggle cracks and pin decals as P (same boxes).
2. Age reads: **FAIL**. Same as P.
3. No tile tells: **FAIL**. The craquelure sits in grey clumps with clean gaps between them, repeating
   about every 150 px across the upper boards, so it reads as stamped patches: `230 0 150 95`,
   `560 0 140 95`, `850 0 150 95`, `1150 0 150 95`, and the whole band `0 0 1400 380`.
4. Board to board: **PASS**.
5. Edges and joints: **PASS**.
6. Holds at distance: **PASS** (identical to P's far crop).
- **Grade: game** (the bottom of it).

**P vs Q (old):** P is better, within a grade (close to a full one). Q's patches add a tell you can see
without hunting.

**P1, the change that moves it most:** let the old coat fail the way paint does. Lift long strips along
each lap's lower edge and at the board ends down to grey weathered wood, with primer at the film's
edge, and take the confetti flecks off the open field. Replace the drawn squiggles with short checks
that follow the grain. **Best age: few (Q).**
