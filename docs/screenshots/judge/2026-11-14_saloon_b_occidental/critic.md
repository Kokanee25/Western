# Blind critic, 2026-11-14_saloon_b_occidental (saloon B, the sign and the walks' lamps)

## The five biggest differences a viewer would notice
1. **The night corner is black; the painting is a lit blue dusk** (pair_corner `910 0 900 506`): the painting has a deep-blue evening sky, wall boards readable everywhere, warm light flooding out of the big ground-floor windows onto the boardwalk and street, and lit upper windows (mean luma about 54). The render is close to pure black (mean luma about 3): no sky gradient, only stars; the building is a silhouette; the wall lamps make small orange pools on the walk and nothing reaches the street, the upper storey, the gallery or the sign. Cause: light. Cheapest change: bring the dusk sky and ambient/fill up to a blue evening level (or render this view earlier in the dusk) so the walls and gallery read, and let the window glow spill onto the boards.
2. **The siding is dark oxblood paint, not weathered sun-bleached timber** (pair_front `1250 160 250 40` vs painting `100 60 150 60`): the painting's boards are pale grey-tan, warm in the sun, with grain and streaks (wall about RGB 200,172,138). The render's walls are a flat dark maroon (about RGB 38,28,30) that reads as a painted barn, almost black in the corner and dusk shots; at street distance (c.png `540 140 330 280`) it is one smooth red slab. Cause: material. Cheapest change: switch the saloon's wall material to the weathered grey-tan board (or a heavily faded, peeling paint over it).
3. **The building sits alone on a blank white plain** (pair_front `910 330 900 176`, c.png `0 380 1280 340`): the painting's street is warm rutted dirt with hoofprints, horses at a hitching rail, men on the walk, barrels, and other false fronts and a gallery-lined street receding. The render's ground is a smooth pale grey-white (about RGB 194,181,168, reading as snow or concrete), the neighbours are untextured grey boxes, and nobody but one or two figures stands about. Cause: dressing (and the ground material). Cheapest change: give the street the dusty tan, rutted ground texture and put a hitching rail with a horse or two and a few loafers at the saloon's walk.
4. **The camera does not match the painting** (pair_front `910 0 900 506`): the painting is a low, close three-quarter view on the corner entrance with the sign big at the top of the frame; the render's front view is straight-on from far across the street, so the building fills a third of the frame and the sign is a small dark plate. The corner render is from much farther back and wider. Cause: camera. Cheapest change: move the judge cameras to the painting's position, height and field of view (close, low, on the corner door).
5. **Missing the deep two-storey gallery and the corner entrance** (pair_front `1450 100 360 90`, painting `0 0 450 220`): the painting's balcony is a deep, heavy gallery on stout posts wrapping both street faces, with a big glazed corner door under a pediment and "SALT CREEK" painted on the tall windows. The render's front has a thin, shallow balcony across only the front, a plain small door at the left, small uniform windows, and posts that look spindly; the gallery roof is a thin edge. Cause: geometry. Cheapest change: deepen the gallery and carry it round the corner, with a wider glazed double door at the corner.

## What already matches
- Two storeys, balcony with turned/picket railing, posts down to a raised plank boardwalk with steps: the basic type is right.
- Wall lamps either side of the windows, warm-coloured, in the right places (front `1140 280 20 20`, c.png `420 330 80 30`).
- Windows have pale trim and multi-pane sashes; interiors show warm, dressed rooms through the glass in c.png (`500 300 60 80`).
- The sign is a framed board on the front wall below the cornice, as in the painting, and the false-front cornice with brackets is there in c.png.
- c.png's dusk sky and low sun are attractive and close to the painting's warm, hazy front-view light.

## Any outright faults
- Corner view is near-black: a viewer cannot make out the sign, the boards, or the upper floor at all (pair_corner `910 0 900 506`).
- The ground looks like snow or concrete, not a dirt street; blue shadows on it strengthen the snow reading (pair_front `1000 420 700 80`, c.png `0 420 1280 300`).
- Neighbouring buildings are untextured grey placeholder boxes (pair_front `910 100 160 150`, `1700 110 110 100`; c.png `0 300 380 120`).
- The gallery floor in the front view shows a strip of very dark, cool-blue planks against the bright ground, nearly a void under the overhang (pair_front `1100 340 520 50`).

## The sign
- **pair_front (day):** readable on a close look: small cream-gold letters on a dark board, "THE NEW / OCCIDENTAL" (crop `1270 120 230 60`). But it is a low-contrast dark-on-dark plate at about one tenth of the frame's width; it does not catch the eye. The painting's "THE SALT CREEK" is a large, bright, gold-on-black board edged in light, the strongest thing in the frame.
- **pair_corner (night):** not readable and not even visible; the sign area is black (`1300 100 300 130`, brightened 4x shows only the gallery rail and a lit upper window). The painting's sign at dusk still reads clearly, picked out against the lit boards.
- **c.png (dusk, ~25 m):** just readable at 1280x720 as "THE NEW OCCIDENTAL" (`580 280 140 90`), the "THE NEW" line already blurring; dark gold on a slate board on a dark red wall, so it holds the eye weakly. Compared with the painting it needs much more contrast: lighter or brighter letters, a light border, larger size, or a lamp on it at dusk.
