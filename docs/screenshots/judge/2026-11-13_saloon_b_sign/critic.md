# Blind critic, 2026-11-13_saloon_b_sign (saloon B, the sign and the walks' lamps)

## The five biggest differences a viewer would notice
1. **The sign can't be read and doesn't hold the eye** (pair_front `1290 120 200 50`; pair_front `440 10 370 80` for the painting; pair_corner `1100 20 560 200`). The painting's sign is the loudest thing in the frame: a long dark oxblood board with a raised moulded frame and big cream-gold serif capitals, "THE SALT CREEK", hung on the balcony rail right over the corner entrance. The render's board is small and high up on the parapet. Its letters are grey on a grey-brown board, about the same value, and at this distance they blur into a smudge that only just reads as "THE SALT CREEK". At dusk (pair_corner) there's no sign to be seen at all: the parapet is a black silhouette. Cause: material (and placement). Cheapest change: paint the board deep red-brown with bright cream/ochre letters (strong value contrast) and make it about twice the size. If it has to stay on the parapet, give it a lamp or a lit edge so it shows at dusk.
2. **The walls and trim are all one colour** (pair_front `1090 190 540 200`). In the painting the clapboard is a faded barn-red with clear shadow lines under each board, and every post, bracket, window frame, door and rail is bleached silver-tan wood. That light frame against the red wall gives the building its whole structure. In the render the walls, posts, rails, window frames and boardwalk are all the same dark mauve-brown, the boards don't show and the trim disappears. Cause: material. Cheapest change: give the posts, rails, frames and boardwalk a light weathered grey-tan wood, and put a darker shadow line under each clapboard.
3. **The daylight is flat and cool, not low and golden** (pair_front, full right half). The painting has a low warm sun from the left. It puts gold rims on the posts and rails, throws long hard shadows across the boardwalk and street, leaves a dusty haze down the street and warms the whole sandy ground. The render is evenly lit and grey-lilac, the ground is nearly white, the façade facing us is in dull shade and the sky has blue cel-painted clouds. It looks like a cloudy noon, not late afternoon. Cause: light. Cheapest change: lower the sun and warm it (amber), raise its contrast against a cooler, darker fill, and give the ground a warm sand tint.
4. **The lamps and windows don't light the dusk scene** (pair_corner `1100 200 560 180`). In the painting the big plate-glass windows blaze amber from inside, and each brass wall lamp throws a soft warm pool across the clapboard. Together they light the boardwalk, the posts and the men's faces, so the corner glows against a blue-hour sky. In the render the lamps are there and lit (about six small orange flames in pairs beside the windows), but each one only puts a tight, reddish smear on the wall about a lamp's height across. The windows are dim brown, the boardwalk and posts are almost black, and the building reads as a black block under a purple night sky with stars. Cause: light. Cheapest change: give the wall lamps a wider, brighter, more yellow-amber light that reaches the boardwalk floor (about 3–4 m), and turn the interior light behind the windows up well past the lamps.
5. **The corner building's shape and framing are different** (pair_corner `1100 20 560 200`; pair_front `1090 190 540 200`). The painting is a close three-quarter view from low down. It looks into a chamfered corner entrance with glazed double doors, tall lettered shop windows, wide steps, turned posts with curved brackets, and a gallery that wraps round under a bracketed cornice. The render looks square at a flat front from far off. Its corner is a plain right angle with a post on it, the windows are ordinary sashes with no lettering, the posts have no brackets, and a tall false front stands up above the side roof (pair_corner). Cause: geometry and camera. Cheapest change: put the camera low at the corner in three-quarter view, as in the painting, and add curved brackets at the post heads. The chamfered entrance is the bigger job.

## What already matches
- The basic program: a two-storey building with a full-width balcony and railings over a covered boardwalk on posts, upper doors and windows behind the balcony, wall lamps between the ground-floor windows.
- The lamp layout is right in spirit: paired wall lamps beside the windows, and at dusk they are lit with visible flames.
- Warm curtained light in some upper windows and the open doorway (pair_front), close to the painting's feel.
- The dusk sky has a cold blue-violet against warm lamps, the same contrast idea as the painting, though pushed too dark.
- (People, horses, the hitching rail and the street around are missing, as expected.)

## Any outright faults
- The boardwalk looks as if it's on stilts: its outer edge stands clear of the ground, with dark gaps and post stubs underneath (pair_front `1090 340 540 40`). The painting's boardwalk sits on a low closed skirt with the steps meeting the ground.
- The night stars are crisp white triangle-shaped dots and the clouds are flat cel shapes (pair_corner `1100 20 560 200`). They clash with the realistic target.
- At dusk the sign and the whole parapet are lost in silhouette, so the building's name can't be seen at all in the corner view.
- In daylight the ground is close to white and lit more brightly than the façade, so the building looks pasted onto a blank plane.
