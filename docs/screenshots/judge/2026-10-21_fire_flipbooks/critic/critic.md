# Blind critic, 2026-10-21_fire_flipbooks

1. **The smoke is a detached dark blob, not a rising column** (fire). The painting's smoke pours straight up out of the flames in soft, streaky charcoal billows, lit orange from beneath, thinning and drifting right across the sky. The render's smoke is one heavy maroon mass floating above the roof with a gap of sky under it, a lumpy cratered surface, coarse stepped edges and a pale fringe where it meets the sky, and the sparks sit on it like a sprinkle of dots. It reads as a cloud pasted on, not smoke coming off a fire. Crop: `critic/1_smoke_blob.png`
2. **Flames are flat sheets on an unlit, intact barn** (fire). In the painting the building is a burning skeleton: the walls glow orange from the fire, charred posts and rafters stand black against a white-yellow core, timbers are falling, and the flame tongues curl up and around the frame. In the render the flames are vertical orange curtains standing in front of one wall with a few bare rafters, while the rest of the barn, roof included, is a near-black solid box untouched by the fire's own light. The fire looks stuck onto the building rather than eating it. Crop: `critic/2_flame_sheets.png`
3. **The fire doesn't light the street** (fire). The painting's ground is wet mud with puddles reflecting orange flame and purple sky, and the people, barrels and trough have hot orange rims on their fire side. The render's ground is an even rust-red everywhere, as bright far from the fire as near it, with no pool of firelight, no reflections, and the grass beside the blaze barely warmer than the rest. The blaze doesn't feel hot or near. Crop: `critic/3_ground_firelight.png`
4. **Nobody is fighting the fire** (fire). The painting is a crowd scene: a bucket line of a dozen townsfolk passing pails from a trough, a horse led out of the doors, another rearing, debris and planks all over the street. The render's street is empty, one horse standing by the saloon and a few specks far off, so a burning building in the middle of town reads as calm and unwatched. Crop: `critic/4_empty_street.png`
5. **The sky competes with the fire** (fire). The painting's dusk sky is a deep violet with a thin orange-red band low down, dark enough that the fire is clearly the brightest, warmest thing in the picture. The render's sky is a busy pattern of bright orange streaks over lilac across the whole top half, nearly as warm and bright as the flames, so the eye doesn't go to the fire and it isn't clearly dusk. Crop: `critic/5_busy_sky.png`

**What's closest:** The overall palette, orange fire against a purple-and-orange dusk with a dark silhouetted town, is the painting's, and the flames' bright yellow-white base fading to orange tongues reads as fire at a glance. The saloon porch's warm lamplit interior on the left also matches the painting's lit-window mood.
