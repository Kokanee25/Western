# The jail: the blind critic, round 2, 2026-11-12

I looked at the eight pair images (painting left, render right) and cut zoomed crops of the render's facade, corner, yard, lane wall, office, cell corridor, cage and roof (in `/tmp/claude-0/critic_crops2/`). Only the form was judged. The exclusions hide a fair amount. The placeholder stone is laid in big, even, dark blocks, so it is hard to tell whether quoins, window surrounds and a plinth are missing as shapes or only as paint. The target's whitewashed plaster and pale rubble would make every interior much brighter, so part of the render's dark, blue rooms may be the material and not the light. Without the desk, stove, bunks, outhouse, woodshed and woodpile, nothing in the render gives a sense of scale, which makes the office in particular read as too big and empty. The bunk boards and the sheds are also built forms in the painting. The grey neighbours and the orange sky hide how the jail sits in the street and how warm the sun is meant to be on its walls.

## The five biggest differences a viewer would notice

1. **The silhouette is a flat box: no stepped parapet and no lower cell wing** (front, crop `1130 140 460 60` against the painting's `110 30 530 120`; corner, crop `1050 130 560 220`). The painting's jail has a raised centre panel and end piers with coping on the front parapet, and its office block is clearly taller than the cell wing, which has its own cornice. The render's top edge is a straight line, and from the corner the whole building reads as one height. To match: build the stepped parapet with piers and a coping course, and drop the cell wing's roof and parapet below the office's, with a cornice at the step.
2. **The yard doesn't read as an enclosed yard, and the lane wall hides the building** (yard, crop `910 150 900 350`; lane, crop `910 190 900 220`). From the east the painting looks into a walled court bounded by the cell wing, the office's rear wall (door and window) and tall coped walls with a plank gate. The render shows open ground with low walls far off. From the lane, the render's yard wall is nearly as tall as the jail itself, so the barred cell-wing end wall never shows. To match: set the yard walls to enclose the court on all sides at about head height (lower than the cell wing's end wall), give them coping, put a plank gate in the rear wall, and make the office's rear wall and door visible from inside the yard.
3. **The openings have no stone dressing** (front, crop `1190 230 340 110`; corner, crop `1150 220 450 90`). The painting frames every window and door with projecting lintels and sills and puts a transom over the front door, with dressed quoins up each corner. In the render, the openings are plain holes in the wall face, and the side windows are small and narrow. To match: add lintel and sill blocks and corner quoins as geometry, add a transom light over the door, and make the office's side windows the painting's tall proportion.
4. **The interiors are dark and blue, with no sun shafts, and the office windows are wrong** (office, crop `1150 150 660 180`; cells, crop `1280 0 530 320`). The painting lights the office through two tall twelve-light sash windows in deep, splayed reveals, so warm bars of sun cross the floor, and the cell corridor is lit by high barred windows down its left wall. The render's office is long and dim, with thin green-framed windows and joists running lengthwise rather than heavy cross beams. The corridor's left wall shows no windows. To match: build proper multi-pane sashes with deep reveals in the office, run the beams across the room and shorten it, cut the high barred windows into the corridor wall, and let the sun through them.
5. **The cages are wrong: too fine, open-topped and short of the ceiling** (cells, crop `1280 0 530 320`; cage, crop `1100 0 450 300`). The painting's cages are riveted flat iron straps in wide squares that run floor to ceiling, with fold-down bunk boards hung on chains. The render's cages are a dense, fine lattice that looks like wood, their tops stop well below the ceiling with bar ends sticking up, and they have no bunks. To match: widen the strap spacing, make the straps read as flat iron with rivets at the crossings, carry the cages up to the ceiling (or close them with a lattice top), and hang a bunk board on chains in each cell.
