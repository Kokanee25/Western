# Blind critic, the undertaker's round 1

## Per view
- front: SIMILAR: right form (white clapboard false front, sign board, porch roof, two windows flanking a central door), but the shop sits small and dim at dusk-blue light instead of warm low sun, the blank sign carries a smeared dark texture, and a railed porch replaces the open boardwalk; the plain white neighbour block at left reads unfinished.
- yard: SIMILAR: long white side wall, side door with ramp and gabled shed are all there, but the slanted cellar doors read as two flat white panels with no stone cheek walls, the shed's opening is a blank white slab, and there's no raking sunlight; the whole yard is in flat shade.
- workroom: DIFFERENT: the painting is a small, sunlit, cluttered workshop with an open door to the yard; the render is a big empty multi-room hall with posts and beams, vertical-board walls and an open stair hole, with no bench, stove, tools, boards or shavings.
- stair: DIFFERENT: the painting looks down worn wooden steps into a lantern-lit whitewashed stone cellar; the render looks straight down a hole in an orange floor at a narrow stair landing on a bright blue-and-yellow pixel-camouflage tile, with no stone, lantern glow or darkness.
- embalming: DIFFERENT: the painting is a low whitewashed rubble-stone room with a packed-earth floor, warm lantern light and a zinc table on trestles; the render has board walls, blue/yellow camouflage-pattern floor and wall surfaces, dark plain tables, a hard horizontal black band across the frame, and cool dim light.

## The five biggest differences
1. **Cellar surfaces show a placeholder blue camouflage texture** (embalming `0 260 1280 460`, also stair `540 180 230 360`): the painting has whitewashed rubble-stone walls and a packed-earth floor with a few boards in warm lamplight; the render has a saturated blue/yellow/purple pixel-noise pattern on the floor tiles and on the left wall. Cause: material (a missing or fallback texture on the cellar floor and wall).
2. **The cellar is boarded timber, not stone** (embalming `340 170 940 330`): the painting's cellar is all thick lime-washed stone with heavy dark joists low overhead; the render's walls are vertical grey-brown planks with doorways into further rooms, so it reads as a basement shed rather than a cold stone cellar. Cause: form and material (walls built as board partitions; stone not used).
3. **A hard black band slices the embalming view** (embalming `0 0 1280 265`, edge at y~265): the painting is one continuous lantern-lit room; the render has a sharp horizontal line above which everything is much darker, as if a slab or shadow cut the frame (probably the near plane clipping a floor or a light/shadow seam). Cause: light or form (camera inside geometry, or a shadow cascade/occluder edge).
4. **The workroom is a vast empty hall instead of a cramped workshop** (workroom `0 0 1280 720`): the painting has a low white-board room tightly filled with a workbench, coffin-in-progress, sawhorses, stove, boards against walls, tools hung on rails, shavings everywhere, and bright sun through an open door; the render is a deep, bare space with central posts, a long low beam, several doorways, a stair hole and almost nothing in it, lit dimly. Cause: missing dressing (bench, stove, lumber, tools, shavings) and form (room proportions far bigger and more open than the painting's). Props such as coffins, the hearse, the embalming kit and ice are absent throughout, as expected.
5. **Light is cool dusk, not warm low sun** (front `350 210 440 330`, yard `0 100 1280 380`): the paintings have golden late-afternoon sun raking across the clapboards, crisp cast shadows and warm grass; the renders have a blue sky, flat shaded walls, near-black ground and glowing yellow windows, so they read as twilight and the buildings look grey and lifeless. Also the yard's cellar doors are thin white flaps (yard `480 360 150 70`) and the shed door is a plain white rectangle (yard `940 250 105 180`) where the painting has weathered plank doors on stone. Cause: light (sun angle/colour and exposure) and material (untextured white panels).
