# Blind critic, 2026-11-13_valley_ground

1. **The dirt is a flat, smooth sheet; ruts are stains, not trenches** (render_street_noon, `300 440 700 280`, `render_street_noon_street_ground.png`; also render_sheriff_road, `400 560 560 160`, `render_sheriff_road_ruts_flat.png`): the street and the road read as one evenly lit tan plane with fine sand grain. The wheel ruts are long dark bands with soft edges and no raised berm, so they don't catch the light or cast any shading. There are no stones, clods or dried mud crusts, and the hoofprints are a few small identical rings dotted on top. The targets (6_road_target, 3_street_golden_target) are about the ground's relief: churned ridges either side of each rut, pebbles and clods throwing little shadows, prints pressed into it, and raking light picking all of it out. Cheapest change: give the ruts real relief (a height/normal offset that lifts a berm along each edge and sinks the groove), so that sun and shadow shape them.

2. **The open range is bare sand with plants spotted on it, not grassland** (render_sheriff_road, `0 360 420 260`, `render_sheriff_road_shrubs_dark.png`; also render_camp_approach and render_cabin): between the plants the ground is the same pale speckled sand all the way to the horizon. Shrubs and dry-grass tufts stand alone at roughly even spacing, each on bare ground, so the land looks like a desert test field. The RDR2 references (horizon_2, street_golden_1) carpet the ground with short grass that thickens and thins in drifts, softening every edge. Cheapest change: scatter a dense layer of short, dry, low grass in clumped patches (denser at road shoulders and around shrub bases, thinning onto bare patches), so that bare dirt is the exception.

3. **The shrubs are near-black cut-outs with detached hard shadows** (render_sheriff_road, `0 360 420 260`, `render_sheriff_road_shrubs_dark.png`; also render_cabin, `560 320 720 120`): at noon light the sage and brush come out blue-black, darker than their own shadows on the sand. They read as silhouettes pasted onto the ground, with a crisp shadow blob offset beside each and no contact darkening at the base. The lone rock beside them is the same: a dark sliver sitting on the surface. Real sagebrush in sun is a pale grey-green, brighter on top. Cheapest change: lift the foliage colour to a pale sage grey-green and give the leaves ambient/translucent light, so that a shrub in sun is lighter than the shade it casts.

4. **The town from the rise is grey boxes on a brown fog** (render_town_height, `380 300 760 360`, `render_town_height_town_mud.png`): from height the whole valley is one hazy brown with no fields, grass, fences or tracks to read scale by. The buildings are plain grey untextured blocks, and a large dark soft-ended smear (the road) runs across the foreground like a burn mark. Compare horizon_3, where the distant town sits in textured land with trees, colour changes and detail holding up through the haze. Cheapest change: thin the distance haze over the near and middle ground, and give the far-away buildings their own wall and roof colours instead of grey.

5. **The horizon is a ruler-flat line with cardboard landforms and lined-up trees** (render_cabin, `560 320 720 120`, `render_cabin_horizon_flat.png`; render_camp_approach, `0 280 1280 140`, `render_camp_approach_treeline.png`): the plain ends in a perfectly straight edge. The only landform is one flat-topped grey mesa silhouette (render_cabin) with no shading or texture. The "wooded high ground" is a single row of tall, similar trees with lollipop crowns standing evenly along the horizon, with a misty copy of the same row behind. The targets have layered distance: rolling ground, mountains fading into blue haze, trees in irregular clumps of different sizes. Cheapest change: add one or two layers of low rolling hills and a hazed mountain range behind the plain, and group the trees into irregular clumps of mixed heights.

Also dominant, outside the ground: the sky takes up half of every frame as the same marbled, high-contrast swirl of cream and slate, which in render_street_golden turns orange-and-purple like marble. It reads painted, not like the soft, sun-lit cloud of the references. A softer, lower-contrast cloud layer would do more for every shot than any one ground fix.
