# Blind critic, 2026-11-13_saloon_b_r1 (saloon B, the look)

## The five biggest differences a viewer would notice
1. **The inside is cold and unlit; the painting is all warm lamplight** (pair_bar, pair_dance, pair_door, pair_gallery; `crop_bar_light.jpg`, `crop_dance_roof.jpg`). The painting's rooms are lit by amber oil lamps: pools of warm light on walls and floor, dark warm ceilings, soft falloff, haze in the air. The render's rooms are lit only by flat grey-blue daylight from the windows: the bar view is nearly black with blown white window panes, the dance hall is a grey box with a murky roof, and the wall lamps that exist (a few sconces are visible) cast no light on the wall behind them. Kind of cause: **light**. Cheapest change: switch on a warm (about 2000 K) light at every sconce and hanging lamp with a visible pool on the wall, and render the interior views at the painting's hour (night/dusk) so window light stops fighting it.

2. **Wall material and colour inside: pale green-grey striped board against warm plaster and brown wood** (pair_door, pair_dance, pair_bar, pair_gallery; `crop_door_timber.jpg`, `crop_dance_roof.jpg`). The painting's walls are a warm ochre/cream plaster or aged horizontal board above a dark wood wainscot with a chair rail. The render's walls are a cool mint-grey with fine, even vertical stripes that read as corrugated sheet or wallpaper, uniform from floor to ceiling in the dance hall, and they moire at distance. This single surface gives every interior its cold, modern cast. Kind of cause: **material**. Cheapest change: retint the wall material to a warm, dirty cream/tan, widen and soften the board lines, and carry the dark wainscot band round every room.

3. **Siding runs the wrong way, and the outside trim is thin** (pair_rear, pair_front, pair_corner; `crop_rear_siding.jpg`, `crop_rear_painting.jpg`, `crop_front_facade.jpg`). The painting is horizontal lapped clapboard, each board catching a line of light and shadow, sun-faded to a dusty rose, with broad cream corner boards, a cream belt course between the storeys, heavy window casings with head caps, and eaves that overhang a pitched gable. The render shows faint vertical board lines on a flat, saturated oxblood wall, with no corner boards, no belt course, thin window frames, and a nearly flat dark roof edge with little overhang, so the rear reads as a painted shed. Kind of cause: **material** (with some **geometry** for the trim). Cheapest change: turn the siding texture to horizontal laps with a real shadow line per board and fade it; then add corner boards and the belt course as trim strips.

4. **The exterior light gives no sun on the wood** (pair_front, pair_corner; `crop_front_facade.jpg`, `crop_corner_dusk.jpg`). The painting's day view has low golden sun raking across the facade, lighting posts and floor and throwing long shadows; its dusk view is cool blue-violet outside with every window and sconce glowing amber and spilling onto the porch. The render's day front is entirely in its own shadow (sun behind the building), a dark blue-brown silhouette against a pale sky and bleached ground; its dusk is a single orange-red wash over building, ground and sky, with only weak glow in two windows and none on the porch. Kind of cause: **light** (and **camera**: the front render is a distant head-on elevation, the painting a close three-quarter view from the corner steps). Cheapest change: put the sun on the street side at a low angle for the day shot and move the camera to the painting's corner; for dusk, cool the ambient to blue and let the interior and porch lamps be the only warm source.

5. **Timber and railings: noisy grey posts and plain orange sticks against dark dressed beams and turned balusters** (pair_door, pair_gallery; `crop_door_timber.jpg`, `crop_gallery_rail.jpg`). The painting's posts and beams are dark, smooth, squared brown timber, and the gallery rail has turned balusters, a heavy moulded handrail and a ball-capped newel in worn pale wood. The render's posts and beams carry a high-contrast speckled grey texture that looks like bark or concrete, stretched on the beams, with oversized block caps at each post head; the gallery rail is square sticks in a bright, saturated orange with no grain or wear, the loudest colour in the building. The floors, too, are glossier and more orange than the painting's dry, dusty grey-brown boards. Kind of cause: **material**. Cheapest change: give posts, beams and rail one dark, low-contrast brown timber material (desaturate the orange by about half and darken it) and knock the floor's shine down.

## What already matches
- The massing: two storeys, a wrap-round porch on posts with a railed balcony above, the corner entrance, a false front with a bracketed cornice and a sign board (pair_front, pair_corner).
- The rear's parts are present: the outside stair to an upper door on a landing, back doors with small steps, cream-framed sash windows on red (pair_rear).
- The interior's structure: an open well with a railed gallery above the bar room, exposed joists overhead, posts on a grid, wide plank floors running the right way (pair_door, pair_gallery).
- The red of the outside, in the rear day view, is in the painting's family (only too saturated and too even).

## Any outright faults
- **Light leaks through the roof** (pair_dance, upper left and along the far eave; `crop_dance_roof.jpg`): rows of bright white dots and dashes where the roof meets the walls and between roof boards, like pinholes of sky. Also visible as a dotted bright line under the cornice in pair_front (`crop_front_facade.jpg`).
- **A flat, untextured orange slab** fills the right foreground of pair_dance (right edge, full height below the roof): an unshaded panel or door seen too close, with no grain and no shadow. The same flat orange is on the open doors in pair_front and pair_rear, which glow as if unlit by the scene.
- **Moire on the striped walls** (pair_dance, the far wall and the side wall: curved banding; pair_door, the left wall): the fine vertical stripes alias into swirls at distance.
- **Oversized block caps on the post heads** (pair_door, the three near posts; pair_bar, the row of posts): cubes wider than the beam they carry, reading as placeholder blocks rather than joinery.
- **The dusk ground and building are one colour** (pair_corner): the ground plane has no horizon separation from the building's base and the porch seems to float in an orange void; may be the unbuilt town, but the fog/ambient tint is swallowing the building's own colours.
- **The porch deck sits on tall bare stilts with a visible gap beneath** (pair_front, along the base; pair_corner, right side): no skirt or closed step sides, so daylight shows under the building; the painting's deck is low and skirted with broad corner steps.
- **A bright point of light by the rear right door** (pair_rear, lower right by the door): a small glowing dot in full daylight with no fitting around it.
