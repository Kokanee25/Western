The standing man at the wall comes with three different woods: 3/15/16 (light streaky pine), 5/6/14 (darker knotty pine) and 19/20/23 (soft broad boards).

## The five biggest differences

1. **The men's light falls in hard-edged posterised steps and the wood's does not.** Shirt sleeves, faces and the grey sleeve block are shaded in cel bands with jagged edges. The wood shades in smooth continuous gradients. Most visible in 1, 9 and 18 (sleeve), 11, 13 and 17 (face), and 3, 5, 14 and 19 (sleeve block).
2. **The wood's figure is photographic.** It has crisp concentric pine knots, fine sawn grain and end grain, where the men have broad painted mottles and soft cloth folds. Most visible in 5, 6 and 14, also 10, 2 and 4.
3. **The weathering reads as light or burn, not dirt.** Pale bleach patches look like glare or spray haze (19, 23, 12, 15). On the steps a dark blotch reads as a scorch (2, 4). Dark brown stains do read as grime where they appear (20, 9, 24).
4. **The wood is glossier, hotter and cleaner than the men's matte, grimy cloth.** The steps look lacquered (2, 4) and the walls nearly unweathered (11, 13, 17, 15), while the men's vests and trousers carry soft mottled wear.
5. **The wood is drawn mechanically.** Its black seam lines break into stepped dashes, the boards alternate light/dark like stripes (7, 8, 21, 11, 13, 17), and the softest walls are blurrier than the men's crisp outlines (19, 20, 23).

## Best and worst

- **Best:** 21 and 24 (SAME), then 9, 20 and 22. In these the stains are darker and dirt-like and the wood's detail level is closest to the men's.
- **Worst:** 5, 6 and 14 (photographic knotty pine), then 2 and 4 (lacquered, saturated steps with a scorch-like blotch).
