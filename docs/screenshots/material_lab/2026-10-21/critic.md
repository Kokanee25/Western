## The five biggest differences

1. **Detail size and frequency.** The wood carries fine photographic grain, knots, nail holes and
   speckle; the men are painted in broad shapes with little small texture (03, 05, 12, 15, 17).
2. **How shading is built.** The men are shaded in hard-edged posterised tone bands; the wood's
   light is a continuous gradient over its baked values (12, 15, 05, 10).
3. **Lightness, saturation, temperature.** The wood is often bleached cream or grey-white, or blown
   to hot orange-white; the men are mid to dark, warm and saturated (13, 16, 07, 06, 03).
4. **Edge flaws on the wood.** Square mosaic blocks and stair-stepped edges on fascias, steps and
   the table base (11, 18, 09, 08); thin black dashes along the wall's seams (02, 06, 10, 14, 18).
5. **Repetition and patchiness.** Single boards jump to another saturated colour (14, 16), knots
   repeat in obvious places (13, 15), noisy speckled surfaces on the post and chair (05, 17, 18).
