## The five biggest differences

1. **Boards striped by colour.** Each board gets its own colour, from near-white bleach to saturated orange-red, so walls read as loud stripes. Nothing on the men has that range: their cloth varies gently within one colour. Shown most in 15, 16, 18, 19 and 24, with milder cases in 03, 20 and 22.
2. **Stains read as light, not dirt.** The weathering patches are paler than the wood around them and soft-edged, so they look like dappled sunlight, glare or bleach clouds rather than grime. The men's dirt is darker than its cloth. Shown most in 04, 09, 14, 16 and 07.
3. **Photographic grain and gradients against hard-edged painted men.** The wood has fine, sharp grain lines, crisp knots and smooth airbrushed falloff, especially on posts and beams. The men are built from a few flat value steps: the faces are cut into planes and the sleeves are banded. Shown most in 01, 20, 08, 10, 23 and 06.
4. **Dark stains are airbrushed smudges.** Where the stains are dark, on beam ends, step faces and knots, they are soft blurry blots that look like soot or burn marks, not painted strokes or the men's cloth mottle. Shown most in 08, 10, 11, 02 and 13.
5. **The wood is brighter and more saturated than the men.** The vest man's near-black trousers and the dark vests sit against glowing orange walls, as if the man and the wood were lit by different lights. The dark decks (05, 12, 22) are the exception and match well. Shown most in 14, 16, 18, 07 and 15.

## Best and worst

- **Best:** 12 and 05 (the dark deck), then 21 (the calm wall and post), 11 (the muted beam and step) and 13 (the dark wall).
- **Worst:** 16 (the striped, blotchy wall), 15 (bleached stripes behind the old man), 24 (white boards and the rainbow-slat fence), 18 (cream and orange photo boards) and 19 (bleached bands behind two men).
