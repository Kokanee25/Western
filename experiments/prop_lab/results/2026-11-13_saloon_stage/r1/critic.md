# Blind critic, saloon B stage props, round 1

Looked at: mirror_noon/night, pegs_noon/night, trunk_noon/night, screen_noon/night, footlights_noon/night (960x540 JPEGs, downscaled and cropped); concept paintings saloon-painted.png and saloon-night.png.

| Prop | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| mirror | CLOSE | Reads as a dressing table with a tall framed glass and a shelf of pots. But the glass shows a dark lamplit room at noon, the "standards" are not visible (it looks hung on the wall), and the table is a cold slate blue, not painted pine; the table things are too small to name. |
| pegs | CLOSE | A dress and a long coat on a peg board read at once. The hat is a flat green disc like a bowler crown with no plume, and the coat is near-black at noon and red at night, never plum velvet; neither cloth looks like satin, lace or velvet. |
| trunk | CLOSE | Two flat-topped slatted trunks, one open, read as trunks. But they are blue-black all over: no painted canvas, no oak slats, no bright tin corners or brass; the costume inside is one thin red sliver. |
| screen | CLOSE | Clearly a three-leaf folding screen. The cloth is a cold blue-grey with a tiny dark print that reads as wallpaper or ticking, not faded cretonne; frames read black, not oak. Better at night, where it warms to a rose print. |
| footlights | DIFFERENT | They read as squat tin cans or buckets on the floor. The hood is a short curved band, the flame shows above it, and at night they throw no light on the floor or wall: nothing says "stage lamp". |

## Five biggest differences, most noticeable first

1. **Footlights look like tin cans, and are not lit** (footlights_noon, footlights_night; `critic_crop_1.png`). Each is a low drum with a lip, not a lamp behind a tall curved reflector. At night the flames sit on top in plain view and cast no pool of light; the wall glow comes from the stand's lamp. The fourth is cut off at the frame edge.
2. **Everything is cold blue-black at noon, nothing like the warm painted brown of the concepts** (trunk_noon worst; `critic_crop_2.png`). The trunks, table, screen and coat all share one slate-blue dark; the concepts are honey wood, brass and amber. Materials (canvas, oak, tin, brass, leather) can't be told apart; the trunks read as dark plastic boxes.
3. **The mirror reflects a room that isn't there** (mirror_noon; `critic_crop_3.png`). Out in noon sun the glass shows a dark interior with two lit lamps, so it reads as a window or a painting, not a mirror. The frame hangs flat on the wall; the two standards are not seen.
4. **The plumed hat and the plum velvet are missing** (pegs_noon, pegs_night; `critic_crop_4.png`). The hat is a flat dark-green disc with a band, no ostrich plume; the frock coat is black by day and red by night. The dress has no visible black lace flounce. Both garments look like flat cut-outs pressed to the wall.
5. **The screen's cloth reads as blue ticking, not faded cretonne** (screen_noon; `critic_crop_5.png`). The print is small, dark and regular on blue-grey; faded cretonne would be a larger floral on cream or pale rose, sun-washed. The frames read black, not oak.
