# Blind critic, saloon B stage props, round 2

Looked at: mirror, pegs, trunk, screen and footlights, each at noon and night (r2/*.jpg, downscaled to 800 px), and the concept paintings saloon-painted.png and saloon-night.png. Crops at full size.

| Prop | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| mirror | CLOSE | A painted table under a tall framed glass with a pediment, pots on a shelf and a hand glass: it reads as a dressing mirror. But no standards show, so the glass seems to hang on the wall, and the table is a bare slab with no drawer or rail; the glass's painted room looks like a dark window more than a reflection. |
| pegs | CLOSE | A peg board with a red dress and a long coat reads at once. The garments are flat cut-outs pressed to the wall, the plum coat turns the same red as the dress under the lamp, and the hat is a flat dark disc with a white blob: it reads as a plate or a sign, not a plumed hat. |
| trunk | CLOSE | Two slatted, flat-topped trunks, one open: the shape is right. At noon both are near-black with no canvas, tin or brass colour, so they read as generic chests; the open one is almost empty, with one red sliver where folded costumes should be. |
| screen | SAME | Three oak-framed leaves at angles with a small repeating print: clearly a folding dressing screen. The print reads more as a dark spotted pattern than faded stripes and roses, and the right leaf goes black at night. |
| footlights | DIFFERENT | Four plain upright boxes on the ground. At noon they read as pale tin cans or blocks; at night as lit candle stubs, glowing only on top. No reflector shape, no lamp, no light thrown forward or up: nothing says stage footlight. |

## Five biggest differences, most noticeable first

1. **footlights_noon / footlights_night: the footlights are plain boxes.** Pale grey cubes by day, candle-stub cylinders by night with a glow on their tops only. No curved tin reflector, no chimney, no pool of light on the floor in front or on the wall above. A viewer would call them cans or candles. Crop: `critic_crop_1.png` (noon).
2. **pegs_night: the hat is a disc and the coat matches the dress.** The woman's hat is a flat round plate with a white smear; no crown, no brim curve, no plume shape. Under the lamp the plum velvet coat goes the same red as the satin dress, so the two garments merge into one red mass. Both hang flat as paper with no folds or weight. Crop: `critic_crop_2.png`.
3. **trunk_noon: the trunks have no materials.** Painted canvas, oak slats, tin corners and brass lock all read as one near-black; only the small grey corner caps show. The open trunk's inside is an empty dark box with a single red curve, not folded costumes. Crop: `critic_crop_3.png`.
4. **mirror_noon: the glass hangs on the wall, not on standards.** The frame sits flat against the boards above a separate table; no uprights tie it to the table. The glass shows two lamps and a black lower half, which reads as a dark window rather than a mirror. Crop: `critic_crop_4.png`.
5. **screen_noon: the cretonne reads as a dark dotted pattern.** The panels are dark grey with rows of black blobs; no stripes, no rose colour, nothing faded or cotton-like. In the painting's warm interior this would read as a dark carved or woven panel. Crop: `critic_crop_5.png`.

Overall: the stand puts every prop in deep shadow at noon, so colour is lost; at night the lamp turns everything one orange. The concept paintings show warm lamp light with clear local colour (red, brass, white linen) still distinct; these renders don't keep that separation.
