# Blind critic, saloon B props S3, round 1

Looked at: kegs, bed and desk renders at noon and night (r1/*_noon.jpg, *_night.jpg, downscaled to 800 px), the two concept paintings (saloon-painted, saloon-night) and the two keg period photos (Columbia racking room 1919, Kelley's saloon 1894). Bed and desk judged from knowledge of 1870s–80s boarding-house beds and flat-top office desks.

## Five biggest differences, most noticeable first

1. **The strongbox reads as a trunk** (desk_noon.jpg, critic_crop_4.png). A black box with two faint straps. An iron strongbox needs a visible iron colour (grey-black with a cold sheen), a lock escutcheon, bail handles at the ends and rivet or strap-iron banding. Right now it looks like luggage.
2. **The kegs and rack are crushed to black at noon** (kegs_noon.jpg, critic_crop_1.png). Hoops, staves, bungs and the rack's timbers all fall within a few values of each other. The concept paintings keep warm mid-browns in the wood even in shadow. At night they look fine (warm, readable).
3. **The rack is built like a fence, not a stillage** (critic_crop_1.png). The upright posts and front rail sit across the lower kegs' faces, so the kegs seem penned in. Period cellars (Columbia photo) rest kegs on low parallel skids or rails, open at the front and heads out, with wedges between them. The top tier has no rails of its own; it sits on the frame's top bar.
4. **The desk's form is the wrong era** (desk_noon.jpg, critic_crop_4.png). It is a single pedestal of four modern drawers with flat slab sides, a thin top and a low gallery, plus a slatted Arts-and-Crafts chair. An 1880s owner's desk would be heavier: a double pedestal or a partners' desk with panelled sides, a moulded top edge and a bentwood or spindle-back chair. The chair is nearly lost in shadow against the desk.
5. **The bed is too clean and too modern** (bed_noon.jpg, critic_crop_2.png). The blanket is a single smooth sheet folded back in a crisp line, the pillow is an inflated pill shape, and the rails are machine-square with plain round post caps. Nothing sags. For a rented room above a saloon: a sagging tick, a rumpled or patched quilt, a stained pillow ticking and an iron or spool frame.

## Three things that already work

- The keg shape is a good squat beer keg (bellied, with six hoops), and the loose keg on the ground (critic_crop_5.png) has convincing worn staves and a little hoop rust.
- The bed's blue-striped mattress ticking is exactly right for the period and gives it a quick read.
- The night lighting on all three is warm, low and grainy, close to saloon-night's mood. The desk's papers pick up the lamp warmly at night (desk_night.jpg).

## Clean, new or toy-like

- The bed blanket and pillow are smooth and untextured, with no folds, wear or stains: toy-like.
- The ledgers and papers are blank pale rectangles with perfect edges and no writing or dog-ears.
- The desk's drawer pulls and panels are crisp and modern, and the wood shows no edge wear or scuffing.
- The strongbox is a featureless black block, with no rust, scratches or dents.
- The rack timbers are clean, sharp-edged and evenly dark, like painted MDF rather than damp cellar wood.
