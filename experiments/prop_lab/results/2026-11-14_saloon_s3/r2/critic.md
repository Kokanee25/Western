## Three biggest differences

1. **The chair is the wrong object** (`critic_crop_2.png`). Black tubular bistro chair, not a wood bentwood: no wood colour, no cane seat, no double-hoop back, legs too thin and straight. It's the most modern-looking thing in the shot.
2. **The strongbox reads as a trunk** (`critic_crop_3.png`). The domed lid, the straps and the flat black give "luggage", not "iron safe". It needs iron: rivets, banding with a sheen, a lock plate and handles. A square, heavy cube would help.
3. **The desk top and its things lack detail and age** (`critic_crop_1.png`). The ledger is a black slab, the inkstand is invisible, the papers are blank and clean, and the desk is one flat dark brown. Against the concept's warm, grained, worn wood, this whole group goes muddy (noon) or orange-flat (night). The stray kneehole panel also catches the eye.

## What already works

- The desk's silhouette and scale: two drawer pedestals, a centre drawer, a kneehole and a back rail. It reads as an office desk at a glance.
- The open ledger lying flat in the middle with loose sheets beside it is a believable working clutter.
- At night the lamp-warm wood and the dark shadows sit close to the concept's palette.
- The group's arrangement (chair pulled to the side, safe beside the desk) tells "owner's office".

## Clean, new or toy-like

- Every surface is clean and uniform: no scuffs on the drawer edges, no ink stains, no worn top, no dust. The desk looks freshly made.
- The chair's even black finish and perfect round tubes look plastic or toy.
- The papers are pristine, evenly grey sheets with sharp rectangles.
- The strongbox has no rust, wear or paint chips.
