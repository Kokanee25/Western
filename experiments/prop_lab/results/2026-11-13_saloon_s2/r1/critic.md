# Blind critic, saloon B props S2, round 1

Looked at: the 16 renders in r1/ (8 groups, noon and night), the saloon B panels (bar, dance, gallery, cutaway), saloon-painted.png and saloon-night.png, and the period references in refs/saloon/ (the S2 ones and the S1 glass, lamp, faro, scale and card ones). All viewed downscaled, with crops.

## Verdicts

| Prop | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Wheel of fortune | CLOSE | Big spoked wheel with a star hub, pegs round the rim and a pointer at the top: it reads as a gambling wheel at once, like the Evans wheel. But the rim band is plain blue-and-white stripes with no numbers, dice or pictures, so close up it could be a ship's wheel or a life ring. |
| Faro table | CLOSE | Green cloth, a spade layout, the dealing box and the case-keeper are right. It is a small square card table, not the long faro table of the references, and there are no chips on it at all, so it looks unplayed. |
| Lookout's high chair | CLOSE | A plain ladder-back chair set behind the table. It sits a little high, but it has no footrest and no tall legs, so it reads as an ordinary kitchen chair, not a perch above the game. |
| Shot glass | CLOSE | Right size and a plain thick tumbler at noon. No pressed pattern like the Met taster, and at night it sinks into the table and only the whiskey shows. |
| Beer schooner | CLOSE | Stemmed glass of the right shape. At noon the beer is a pale milky white-blue, so it reads as a glass of milk; at night the stem and foot fade out. |
| Decanter | CLOSE | Shouldered body and faceted stopper read well at noon. At night the neck and stopper all but vanish and it is a round bowl of red liquid. |
| Deck of cards and chips | CLOSE | Right things, but the deck is a thick red block with a round mark on top, like a small book or matchbox, and there are only three short chip stacks. |
| Till | CLOSE | A wooden box with a drawer and brass pull: it now reads as wood. It is a plain box with no lid, slot or bell, so it could be any drawer box. |
| Gold scales | CLOSE | A brass beam balance with hanging pans is right. It is far too big: it stands twice as tall as the decanter, where the Dawson scale is a small thing on the bar. |
| Hanging lamp (plain) | SAME | Smoke bell, wire harp, glass chimney and brass font, and at night the flame glows in the chimney where it should. Very like the barber-shop lamp. The chimney glows amber-brown at night, like coloured glass. |
| Hanging lamp (shaded) | CLOSE | The wide shade over the chimney matches the 1872 patent and its white underside is lit from the flame. The shade throws a hard, bright oval on the wall, like a stage spotlight, and its teal-green is a bit cold. |
| Wall bench | CLOSE | A plank seat, back rail on posts, and legs, the right plain build, much like the benches round the dance floor in the target. It is one flat, even slate-blue with no grain, wear or nail heads, so it looks like a painted park bench. |
| Handsaw | CLOSE | Blade and handle at the right size. The blade is a smooth blue-white slab with a hard white edge and no teeth, barely tapered, and the handle is an open brass ring, not Disston's closed wooden grip. It reads as a machete or scraper. |
| Fiddle and bow | DIFFERENT | The bow is fine, but the fiddle is shiny red like plastic, its body has one waist like a guitar, it has no f-holes, and the neck is a checkered strip like frets. It reads as a toy guitar or mandolin, not the worn violin in the Chalfant painting. |
| Beer kegs | CLOSE | Hooped wooden casks of about the right size. They are tall and narrow like flour barrels, with many thin hoops that make a grid; period beer kegs are fat and squat with a few heavy iron hoops and a bung on the side. |
| Keg rack | DIFFERENT | The kegs all stand on end, stacked two high, behind a plain rail fence. A keg rack holds kegs on their sides in a cradle, tap end out, as in the target cutaway and the racking-room photo. This reads as barrels stored behind a railing. |
| Cookstove | CLOSE | Four-hole top, oven door, firebox, curved legs and pipe are all right. It is a smooth, even black box with no cast panels, rails or warming shelf, and the fire shows at noon but is dark at night. |
| Stove board | CLOSE | Right idea, but it is a flat, pale grey sheet at noon and a tan card at night, with no edge, nails or scorch marks. |

## Five biggest differences, most noticeable first

1. **The fiddle is a toy guitar.** `saw_fiddle_night.jpg` (also noon). Shiny red, guitar outline, no f-holes, a fretted-looking neck and a round knob for a scroll. Next to the Chalfant violin it is the wrong instrument. Crop: `critic_crop_1.png`.
2. **The kegs stand on end behind a fence.** `kegs_noon.jpg`. Eight tall, narrow barrels stand upright in two rows behind two rails, and the hoops make a grid. Kegs on a rack lie on their sides, fat and squat, with few heavy hoops. Crop: `critic_crop_2.png`.
3. **The gold scale is giant.** `glasses_noon.jpg`. The scale towers over the decanter and the glasses and sits on a box as big as the till. The period scale is small enough to stand by a man's elbow on the bar. Crop: `critic_crop_3.png`.
4. **The handsaw is a blank slab.** `saw_fiddle_noon.jpg`. No teeth, no taper to speak of, a shiny blue-white face with a hard white edge, and a ring for a handle. It reads as a machete or a putty knife. Crop: `critic_crop_4.png`.
5. **The cookstove is a plain black box and its fire goes out at night.** `cookstove_noon.jpg` and `cookstove_night.jpg`. No cast ornament or rails like the York and Charter Oak stoves; the board under it is a flat grey slab; at night, when the fire should be the brightest thing, the stove is a dark lump. Crop: `critic_crop_5.png` (noon left, night right).

## Three things that already work

1. The plain hanging lamp is right in shape and, now, in light: the flame glows inside the chimney at night and the smoke bell, harp and font all read.
2. The wheel of fortune is now a proper big spoked wheel with a star hub, rim pegs and a pointer, and at night the lamplight on its brass and spokes looks good.
3. The glassware at noon (thick tumbler, stemmed schooner, stoppered decanter) is right for the period and sized well against each other.

## Clean, new or toy-like

- The fiddle's glossy red finish looks like a plastic toy.
- The wall bench is one flat slate-blue with no grain or wear, like a new park bench.
- The saw blade is spotless and mirror-bright, with no rust, teeth or wear.
- The cookstove is an even, clean black with sharp edges, and the stove board is a clean grey pad.
- The scales' brass is clean and even, and the faro cards are crisp and white with no chips on the cloth.
