# Blind critic: booze shelf r2 (night, 1 m and 3 m)

## 1. Legibility at 1 m: PASS
Read from the full 1280x720 frame: **CUTHBERT'S**, **HOLCOMBE'S STONE GINGER BEER DENVER**,
"This Bottle Is Holcombe's, Return It" (just), **PURE RYE**, "Old Sour Mash" (just), **RIEDL'S**,
**BELLE OF LA…** (cut by the post), **BURNT HOLLOW**, **CARRIBERS/CARRIERE'S** (unsure of the spelling).
Everything smaller (makers' lines, small print, the top arcs) is mush. Brand names read: pass.

## 2. At 3 m: PASS (with a caveat)
No words, only cream/gold blocks with a dark dot (the vignette). Not distracting as text, but the
labels are the brightest, most uniform thing on the shelves: rows of near-identical pale squares at
the same height on near-identical bottles read as stickers more than as a varied back bar.

## 3. Period: CLOSE
Layout (oval vignette, brand in caps, script sub-line, maker's line at foot, printed border) is right
for 1880s letterpress. Off: the labels are too clean and even (no foxing, tears, stains, glue-shadow,
peeled corners); too few colours (near all cream + red/black, no chromolitho colour); the bottles are
nearly all one dark claret-red, one shape, which reads like a row of modern wine bottles, not amber
quarts, flasks and square bitters. The stoneware ginger beer is the most convincing piece.

## 4. Five biggest problems (1 m frame unless noted; boxes x0,y0,x1,y1)
1. **Glass reads as opaque painted solid, and too red.** Lamps sit right behind the bottles but no light
   comes through; no liquid line, no amber glow, no transparency at edges. Every bottle is the same
   maroon. Box (140,185,750,425) and (1040,210,1215,415). Biggest tell that these are props.
2. **Labels too new and too alike.** Same cream stock, same brightness, same oval-vignette template,
   same placement height on every bottle; no aging, no wear, none crooked. At 3 m this becomes a
   regular row of pale squares. Box (145,290,750,395); 3 m: (80,180,1220,385).
3. **Bottle shapes too uniform.** Nearly all are tall round long-neck "wine" forms; the flask (BURNT
   HOLLOW, 1040,270,1110,415) is the only distinct shape in view; no square bitters, no squat
   quarts with blob tops. The green square gin is cut at the frame edge (1240,250,1280,415).
4. **Labels sit flat on the glass.** They look like cards pasted on a cylinder: edges straight, lettering
   doesn't wrap or darken toward the sides, no paper thickness or shadow. Most visible on CUTHBERT'S
   (145,290,218,385) and PURE RYE (433,295,493,385).
5. **Small print is noise, not print.** The secondary lines are smeared grey streaks rather than crisp
   tiny letterpress; it reads as blur, not as small type. Also the ginger-beer jug's top half is a flat
   tan with a hard seam to the pale body (295,268,355,425): looks like two materials, not salt glaze.
