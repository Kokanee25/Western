## The five biggest differences

1. **The work board is a navy kitchen counter.** `work_board_noon.jpg`. One flat blue-grey material on every face, no wood or wear, square modern box form. Nothing in the target bar or the Seattle back-bar photo looks like it. Crop: `critic_crop_1.png`.
2. **Cards and chips look like toys.** `glasses_noon.jpg`. A bright blue plastic-looking deck box and three tiny chip stacks. Period cards lay loose with white backs; chips were big flat discs. Crop: `critic_crop_2.png`.
3. **The glassware has the wrong shapes and dies at night.** `glasses_night.jpg`. The schooner is a pint tumbler, the decanter a plain bottle; at night both lose their glass and read as solid orange cylinders. Crop: `critic_crop_3.png`.
4. **The wheel of fortune is small, flat and on a modern board.** `wheel_noon.jpg`. No pegs or clapper, a printed look, on a navy square. The reference wheel is big, pegged, with ornate gilt. Crop: `critic_crop_4.png`.
5. **The slat mat is a plain board.** `work_board_noon.jpg`. A smooth dark rectangle with no slats or gaps. Crop: `critic_crop_5.png`.

## Clean, new or toy-like

- All the brass (lamps, scales, taps, arm and foot rails) is one even, unworn gold. Nothing is tarnished or dulled by handling.
- The work board, stove and stool are smooth and untouched, with no rust, soot or grime.
- The till's gloss gives a mirror-like stepped blue reflection at night (`glasses_night.jpg`).
- The cards and chips, and the navy wheel board, look like modern plastic.

## Three things that already work

1. The piano stool: the iron tripod and claw-and-ball feet look right for the period and fit the room.
2. The gold scales on their box: the shape is right and would sit well on the bar in the target.
3. The plain hanging lamp: its form and warm night glow match the lamps in the concept paintings and the gallery panel.
