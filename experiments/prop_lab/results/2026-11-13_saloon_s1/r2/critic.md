# Blind critic, saloon B props S1, round 2

Looked at: the 16 renders in r2/ (8 groups, noon and night), the saloon B panels (bar, dance, gallery, cutaway), saloon-painted.png and saloon-night.png, and the 14 period photos in refs/saloon/. All viewed downscaled, with crops.

## Verdicts

| Prop | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Hanging lamp | CLOSE | The harp frame, smoke bell, chimney and brass font are right for the period. But at night the light comes from the smoke bell at the top, like an electric bulb, and the flame in the chimney is only a dull glow. |
| Shaded hanging lamp | CLOSE | Green shade, white underside and smoke bell above match the 1872 patent well. Same fault: the glow sits above the shade, so the shade's top is lit and it reads as an electric fixture. |
| Shot glass | CLOSE | Right size and plain tumbler shape at noon. It lacks the heavy pressed base of the reference, and at night the glass disappears and only a red disc of whiskey shows. |
| Schooner | CLOSE | The stemmed goblet with a foam head is the right shape. At night the stem and foot vanish and a floating cylinder of beer is all that is left. |
| Decanter | CLOSE | Good shouldered shape and stopper at noon, though smooth where the reference is cut and ribbed. At night it becomes a brown bowl of liquid with no neck or stopper. |
| Work board | CLOSE | The parts are all there and laid out sensibly. The zinc is a clean, pale, even grey and the two sinks are flat brown panels with no depth, so it reads as a new kitchen counter. |
| Slat mat | CLOSE | The idea is right. It is a uniform charcoal colour with perfect gaps and no wear, so it looks like a plastic deck mat. |
| Box stove | CLOSE | Right size, legs, side door and pipe. It is smooth and blue-black with rounded edges and no cast panels, lid lifter or rust: a modern wood stove, not an 1880s casting. The sand box is a pale flat grey. |
| Faro table | CLOSE | The 13-card spade layout, dealing box and case-keeper are period-correct and read at a glance. The cards are bright white and crisp, and there are only three or four chips on it. It looks unplayed. |
| Lookout stool | DIFFERENT | It is a low, square, rung-less seat that reads as a small side table. A lookout sat high above the table. This one sits at table height. |
| Piano stool | CLOSE | Round plush seat on a short column is right. The four thin black splayed legs look like tubular metal, like a modern drafting stool. The references have turned wood or heavy cast-iron claw legs. The seat is bright, even red at night. |
| Wheel of fortune | DIFFERENT | A small flat disc with red, black and cream wedges and numbers round the rim, on a square board. It reads as a dartboard. The Evans wheel is large, spoked, with a star hub, pegs round the rim and a flapper pointer. |
| Gold scales | CLOSE | A brass beam balance with hanging pans is right. It is very large next to the glasses, and its box is the same high-gloss block as the till. |
| Till | DIFFERENT | It looks like a polished plastic box. Its top mirrors the sky: brown at noon and bright blue with orange streaks at night. No grain, joints or wear. |
| Cards and chips | CLOSE | A deck and three short stacks, all right in kind. They are tiny and few beside the trompe l'oeil reference, and the deck looks like a little red book. |
| Bar arm rail | CLOSE | A brass rail on the top's front edge, with a foot rail below, is right. It is a fat, even olive-gold tube on a stitched leather strip that looks modern, and it stops dead with no end caps or returns. At night the bar top turns a glossy orange. |

## Five biggest differences, most noticeable first

1. **The wheel of fortune is a dartboard.** `wheel_noon.jpg`. It is small, flat, with wedges and rim numbers, and has no spokes, pegs, star hub or flapper. Next to the Evans wheel it is the wrong object. Crop: `critic_crop_1.png`.
2. **Glassware vanishes at night.** `glasses_night.jpg`. The schooner's stem, the decanter's neck and stopper, and the shot glass all disappear. Whiskey and beer float as solid shapes. In a lamplit saloon the glass should catch the light. Crop: `critic_crop_2.png`.
3. **The lamps glow from the wrong place.** `lamps_night.jpg`. The smoke bell at the top is the brightest thing, like a bulb. The flame inside the chimney, which should be the brightest point, is a dim smudge. The shaded lamp's light sits above its shade. Crop: `critic_crop_3.png`.
4. **The till and scale boxes are mirrors.** `glasses_night.jpg` (also noon). Their tops reflect the sky in electric blue with orange bands. They look like lacquered plastic, not oak. Crop: `critic_crop_4.png`.
5. **The lookout stool is a low side table.** `faro_noon.jpg`. It is square, at table height and has no rungs or footrest, so the faro set loses the high perch that marks the lookout. The faro cards are also too white and new. Crop: `critic_crop_5.png`.

## Three things that already work

1. The shaded hanging lamp's shape, with its green shade, white underside, smoke bell and wire frame, is very close to the 1872 patent drawing.
2. The faro layout, dealing box and case-keeper read at once as a faro game, and the two-tone green cloth suits the target's card tables.
3. The schooner and decanter shapes at noon are right for the period and sized well against each other.

## Clean, new or toy-like

- All the brass (lamps, scales, rails, pumps) is one even, clean olive-gold with no tarnish or rubbed spots.
- The zinc top, the sinks, the slat mat and the stove are all spotless and even in colour.
- The till and scale boxes are high-gloss and toy-like.
- The faro cards are crisp white, and the piano stool's thin legs look like a modern toy or a drafting stool.
- The wheel looks like a toy dartboard.
