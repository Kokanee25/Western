# Blind critic: footlights r3

**Verdict: DIFFERENT**

Not a convincing period stage footlight, and not yet a polished-tin reflector lamp. At night it is a pleasant row of glowing lanterns that sits fairly well beside the painted lamps. By day it reads as four small blue-white plastic cups on dark trays.

## Five biggest differences (most noticeable first)

1. **Material reads as pale blue plastic or glass cups, not polished tin.** At noon the reflectors are flat light-blue and white with no bright metal highlight, no darker core and no warm reflection. The painted brass and glass lamps have saturated warm gold and amber glints. Fix: give the reflector a warm silver-tin or tarnished-brass palette with a hard specular streak and a darker interior, and drop the blue tint.

2. **Scale and presence are too small and weak.** Each unit is about the size of a coffee cup and shows no height, flame chimney or font. The painted lamps are tall glass chimneys on chunky brass bases, with the flame as the focal point. Fix: raise each unit and add a visible glass chimney and brass font, so the silhouette reads as a lamp and not a bowl.

3. **No stage-front context.** The lamps sit on a bare floor against a wall with no lip, apron or proscenium board. They are tiny, evenly spaced, and nothing hides the fonts or shields the audience. The row reads as tea lights, not a footlight trough. Fix: seat them in a continuous low tin or wood trough at the stage edge, with the reflectors angled up and back toward the performers.

4. **Flame and light behave unlike the painted style.** At night the glow is white-yellow and hazy, with the flame lost inside the cup. The painted flames are warm orange with a clear teardrop shape and glass bloom, and their light throws a visible warm pool on wood. Here the glow is a blown-out white blob with weak floor spill, and the wall above is lit oddly flat. Fix: warm the colour toward orange, show a distinct flame shape, and add a stronger warm spill on the boards and the wall behind.

5. **Dark flat bases and a flat texture mismatch.** The dark navy-blue base plates look like stickers and carry no brass or wood detail. At noon the wall wood is cold grey and the floor is a pale blotchy tile, both far from the warm honey wood of the paintings. Fix: swap the base plates for brass or dark iron with some rim detail and wear, and put the test stand's wood and floor into the warm saloon palette.

## What works

- The night render has the right mood: a warm row of lights in a dark space.
- The chunky pixel texturing on the floor matches the night concept's quantised look.
- Even spacing makes it read as a deliberate row.
