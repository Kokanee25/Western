# Blind critic, round 2: the Baron's coach (#782)

Judged only from the six renders against the street painting and period Concord coaches.

## Verdicts
- **The coach: DOESN'T.** It reads as an armoured box wagon (a prison van or freight car on wheels), not a Concord: the body is a straight-sided rectangular crate sitting low and level on its running gear, with none of the Concord's rounded, swung shape. The wheels, roof rail, rivets and opening door are good, and it does read as armoured.
- **The strongbox: CLOSE.** The shape works: an iron chest with straps, end handles, a hasp and a padlock. But it looks like a clean, painted footlocker rather than a heavy period treasure box. The black body is spotless while the straps are rusty, and the papers inside read as a blank pale block.

## Five biggest differences (most important first)
1. **The body is a box, not a Concord.** A Concord body is egg-shaped from the side: the bottom rounds up into the front and back, the lower panels curve inward, the top flares out a little and the corners are rounded. Here the sides are flat vertical slabs and the bottom is straight and square, so the silhouette is a crate or prison van. Even the plated treasure coaches of the time (the "Monitor" kind) kept the Concord's curved body under the iron. Crop: `coach_noon.png` (380,155,665,310); also `coach_door_open.png` (310,70,720,420).
2. **No visible thoroughbraces, and it doesn't look hung.** The body seems to rest straight on a flat frame just above the axles. There are no thick leather straps looped between standards at front and back, and no gap or sag under the body, so the coach's most typical engineering is missing and it looks rigid and heavy, like a wagon bed. Crop: `coach_noon.png` (400,285,665,345); front: `coach_front.png` (380,320,590,420).
3. **The driver's box and the boot don't read.** At the front there is a tall plain plank crate with a thin rail, lower than the roof (`coach_door_open.png` (700,110,885,440); `coach_noon.png` (595,190,668,290)). It doesn't read as a high seat with a back and an angled footboard; on a Concord the driver sits up level with the roof line, and his footboard juts forward. From the front, the big panel under the box has a brown diamond, quilted-looking texture that reads as upholstery or wicker, not iron or wood (`coach_front.png` (415,185,565,330)). At the back, the boot is a small low wooden chest with a sloped lid that looks bolted on (`coach_noon.png` (330,225,410,300)), not the Concord's leather-covered rack.
4. **The iron is too black, too even and too clean for the painting's street or for RDR2.** The plates are a near-uniform matte black with tiny rivets, with no dust on the lower panels, no rust streaks under the rivets, no worn edges and no sheen where the sun hits. Under the noon sun it reads as dark plastic. In the warm, dusty, sun-bleached palette of the painting it is the darkest thing in the frame by far. At night it becomes a featureless black silhouette with no highlight on the metal. The wooden parts (boot, driver's box) are not weathered to match. Crop: `coach_door_open.png` (520,90,710,410); night: `coach_night.png` (380,160,670,330).
5. **The strongbox is a clean trunk, not a heavy iron box.** It is sharp-edged and flat black with no corner plates or banding over the edges. The rusted straps don't match the pristine body, the padlock is small for the box, and the walls look thin when open. Inside, the "papers" are a single untextured pale block (`strongbox_open.png` (415,270,490,320)). Period treasure boxes were heavily strapped and riveted at every corner, often green-painted wood bound in iron, or a squat cast-iron box with a large lock. Crop: `strongbox_shut.png` (265,115,655,475).

Minor: the rifle slits are there but are so small and shallow that at street distance they read as handles or vents (`coach_door_open.png` (590,95,615,180)). The open door shows only blackness inside, so the guards' benches can't be seen (`coach_door_open.png` (440,90,520,410)).
