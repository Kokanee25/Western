# Blind critic: the doctor's office props, round 2 (2026-11-10_doctor_d1/r2)

Seen: the five game renders, the studio preview, the two target panels (surgery, sick room) and
the ten period references. Brightened crops are marked. Caveat that touches every prop: the game
renders throw a heavy orange-red cast over the rooms, while the targets are pale and grey-white.
The white china, the pillows and the leather pad all take that colour, so judging their material
is harder. I've judged the props anyway and noted where the cast stopped me.

## 1. The six props

|---|---|---|---|

## 2. The five biggest differences a viewer would notice

1. **The cots' bedding looks moulded, not soft** (`sick_room.png`, the near right cot, plus the far
   cot under the window; `critic_crop_1.png`, brightened x1.4). The striped tick is a neat stack of
   identical layers, the blanket a flat slab with a rigid edge, and the pillow a hard bar. A dark
   blot near the head of each blanket reads as a hole or a scorch. The target's sick room has
   slumped, rumpled grey wool and visible ticking. The cots are the biggest props in the set and
   this is the first thing that reads as fake.
2. **The lamp's reflector is a giant flat plate, and the light comes from the wrong place**
   (`surgery_night.png`, upper left by the window; `critic_crop_2.png`, not brightened). The disc
   dwarfs the font and chimney and is a featureless cream oval. The hot glow sits on the wall
   beside the reflector, not inside the chimney. The references have a small round tin dish
   tight behind the chimney, and the flame is the brightest point.
3. **The examining table reads as a dark padded bench, with no drawers and no leather**
   (`surgery.png`, centre; `critic_crop_3.png`, brightened x1.8). By day it is one dark brown mass.
   The pad has no sheen, no worn edges and no visible buttons, and it melts into the same-coloured
   walls. The buttons and raised head only show at night. The references' defining feature,
   drawers below the pad, never reads from any angle shown.
4. **The instrument tray looks like a modern clean tray of tiny parts** (`instruments.png`, the
   tray on the side table; `critic_crop_4.png`, not brightened). Cream with sharp corners, an
   unexplained black block, and instruments too small and flat to name. There is no white cloth,
   no wooden case and no velvet, and nothing is lying out where a doctor has been working, as in
   the target's linen-covered table.
5. **The white china isn't white and its edges step** (`wash.png`, the washstand left of the door;
   `critic_crop_5.png`, not brightened). The basin and pitcher come out orange terracotta under
   the room's light, with flat shading and jagged pixel steps on the pitcher's right side and
   spout. Its top reads as a closed lid. The towel on the side bar can't be seen, and the
   curtain beside it is what reads. In the target and the Davenport reference, the ironstone is
   the brightest, crispest white in the corner.

## 3. Overall

The props have the right period types and broadly the right silhouettes, but they read as clean
new kit pieces, not a lived-in 1882 surgery: the rigid bedding, the dish-sized reflector and the
toy instrument tray give them away first.
