# Blind critic: the doctor's dispensary and front office, props (D2, round 2)

Judged from the five renders only, against the target panels (`dispensary.jpg`, `front_office.jpg`)
and the period references in `experiments/prop_lab/refs/doctor/`. Rooms, walls and light not judged.

## 1. Prop by prop

| Prop | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Balance (scales) in its case | **SAME** | Matches the 1860-80 Wellcome cased balance closely: glazed case with a dark mahogany frame, central pillar, beam, two shallow pans on fine chains. Only nits: the brass reads dull bone-grey rather than yellow brass, and the base shows no drawer or knob. |
| Mortar and pestle | **CLOSE** | Right form (wide shallow bowl, rolled rim, pouring lip, dark turned handle) but small and reads ochre/orange rather than the Wedgwood's cream stoneware; the pestle's head is hidden, so it reads as a stick in a bowl. |
| Counter bottles (4) | **CLOSE** | Good silhouettes (round shoulder, short neck, dark stopper), believable colours (pale green-clear, plum-brown, cobalt) and cream paper labels with ruled text. Too few and too evenly spaced to read as a working counter. |
| Shelving bottles (~70) | **CLOSE** | Shapes and amber/clear/cobalt mix are right, but they are small for their shelves (half the gap or less), set out in sparse even rows, every one the same few shapes; many clear ones carry black rectangular labels. No jars, no wide-mouth or tall bottles. |
| Shelving | **DIFFERENT** | A single full-height dark open bookcase with plain boards and big empty bays; the panel wants crowded painted shelving with a drawer bank, and even the dark Remick cabinet has glazed doors and a cornice. It reads as a library case, not a pharmacy. |
| Counter (drawer chest) | **DIFFERENT** | Right idea (a druggist's bank of small drawers) but the drawer fronts have no knobs, pulls or label frames at all, so the front reads as a tiled wall of wood squares. Proportions are fine. |
| Desk (roll-top) | **CLOSE** | Recognisable roll-top with S-curved sides, two pedestals of drawers with brass cup pulls and pigeonholes with papers; matches the panel's desk. But the pigeonhole backs read pale, as if open to the wall, the writing bed is bare (no blotter, inkwell, books), and it is short and squat (pigeonhole tier barely above the bed). |
| Chair | **CLOSE** | A Thonet-style bentwood (hoop with inner loop) is period-right, but it is spindly: pencil-thin splayed legs, no leg ring, a flat plain disc seat with no cane. It reads as a café chair beside a heavy office desk; the panel and Remick have a wooden office armchair/spindle-back. |
| Wood (library material) | note | On every piece the grain is a large, high-contrast flame figure in saturated red-orange, one flame per drawer front; at this scale it shouts "mahogany texture" and flattens edges. |

## 2. The five biggest differences

1. **The shelving is sparse and reads as a bookcase** (`shelves.jpg`, `dispensary.jpg` back wall;
   `critic_crop_1.png`). Small bottles in thin even rows with tall empty gaps above them, all a few
   shapes, black labels on clear glass, no jars. The panel is shelves packed edge to edge with mixed
   sizes, labelled jars and a drawer bank.
2. **The counter's drawers have no hardware** (`counter.jpg` front; `critic_crop_2.png`). A grid of
   bare squares, no knobs, pulls or brass label holders; a druggist's drawer bank is all knobs and labels.
3. **The wood's grain is oversized and too red** (`desk.jpg`, `counter.jpg`; `critic_crop_3.png`).
   Big flame figure in saturated red on desk, counter and shelving alike; real period office walnut/oak
   is browner with finer grain, and the panel's wood is honey-brown.
4. **The chair is a spindly café chair** (`front_office.jpg`, right of the desk; `critic_crop_4.png`).
   Legs like wire, no ring, a bare disc seat; it looks too light and too small for the roll-top.
5. **The surfaces are under-dressed** (`counter.jpg` top, `desk.jpg` writing bed; `critic_crop_5.png`).
   The counter holds four bottles, a mortar and the balance spread far apart; the desk bed is empty and
   its pigeonholes pale-backed. The panel's counter is crowded (bottles, graduates, jars, a pill machine).

## 3. The single change that would help most

Fill the shelving: more bottles, mixed sizes and shapes (tall, squat, wide-mouth jars), closer
together and fitting their shelf heights, with cream paper labels instead of black ones.
