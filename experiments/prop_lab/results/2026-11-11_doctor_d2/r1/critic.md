# Blind critic: doctor's office D2, round 1 (2026-11-11)

Judged only the props, against the target panels (`dispensary`, `front_office`) and the period photos.

## 1. Prop by prop

| Prop | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Medicine bottles, counter (amber, brown, cobalt) | CLOSE | Shouldered bottles with short necks and cream paper labels in the right colours (amber, brown, cobalt as in the Remick pharmacy); but they read as dull solid objects, with no glass highlight or see-through edge. |
| Medicine bottles, shelves | DIFFERENT | About 50 bottles in four mirrored rows (big clear stoppered jars top and bottom, small mixed ones between, a gap down the middle), three lower shelves bare; the panel's walls are crammed floor to ceiling. The clear jars read as milky opaque plastic with black square labels, not glass with paper labels. |
| Mortar and pestle | CLOSE | Right size and idea, but the bowl is ochre/tan and tapered like a pudding basin, with no thick rolled rim or pouring lip; the pestle is a plain dark stick with no visible ceramic head. The Wedgwood reference and the panel are cream-white stoneware, twice as wide as tall. |
| Balance in its case | CLOSE | The best match: glazed mahogany case on a plinth, central pillar, beam, two pans on cords, as the 1860-80 Wellcome balance. Off: a large ball/globe at the pillar's foot that no balance has, pans hung right at the side glass, the panes read milky cream (a lit lightbox), the brass matte yellow like plastic, no drawer and knob in the base. |
| Dispensary shelving | CLOSE | A tall open dark bookcase is plausible (the Remick pharmacy's cabinets are dark stained), but it is one plain box with flat sides and no cornice, doors, drawers or ladder; the panel's is white-painted built-in shelving with banks of small labelled drawers. A light strip shows under its foot. |
| Dispensary counter | DIFFERENT | A blank box: one flat panel front, no drawers or doors, no top overhang or moulding, no marble or scrubbed working slab. The panel's counter has drawer fronts, knobs and a marble top. |

But it reads squat and plain: no tambour slats shown, the pigeonholes have no back (the wall shows through as pale slots), drawer pulls tiny and at the pedestal's edge, no panelled sides. |
| Chair | DIFFERENT | Square-section posts, a flat top rail and four flat vertical slats with H stretchers: a Mission/Arts-and-Crafts chair of c.1905. The panel's is a turned spindle-back with a curved crest; the references are a bentwood Thonet No. 14 or a curved low-back leather office armchair. |

Across all the furniture: one saturated red "flame" grain at a large scale, the same on counter, shelving, desk and chair (and the floor), so each reads as varnished laminate rather than walnut, oak or painted pine.

## 2. The five biggest differences

1. **The shelving is nearly empty and laid out like wallpaper** (`shelves.jpg`, the whole case; `dispensary.jpg` back wall). Four identical rows mirrored about a central gap, three empty shelves under them; the panel's dispensary is hundreds of bottles, jars and drawers, every shelf full and uneven. Crop: `critic_crop_1.png`.
2. **Every piece of wood is the same red flame-grain** (`desk.jpg`, desk top and sides; `counter.jpg` front; `front_office.jpg`). Swirling grain a hand wide, deep red-orange, glossy; the references are brown walnut and oak with fine straight grain, and the panel's counter and floor honey pine, its shelves white paint. Desk and counter merge into one material. Crop: `critic_crop_2.png`.
3. **The glass doesn't read as glass** (`shelves.jpg` top and bottom rows, `counter.jpg` the left bottle). The clear stoppered jars are milky white solids with black square labels; no highlights, no bottle seen through another, no dark liquid line. Crop: `critic_crop_3.png`.
4. **The counter is a blank crate** (`counter.jpg`, the whole front). No drawers, doors, pulls or top moulding, so a working dispensary counter reads as a packing box; the mortar, small and ochre, sits on it unnoticed. Crop: `critic_crop_4.png`.
5. **The chair is the wrong era and the desk too plain** (`front_office.jpg`, right of the desk). Flat Mission slats and square posts (20th century) beside a roll-top with see-through pigeonholes and no visible tambour. Crop: `critic_crop_5.png`.

## 3. The single change that would help most

Fill the dispensary shelving densely and irregularly with real glass bottles and jars (clear, amber, cobalt, green; mixed heights; cream labels) and add a bank of small labelled drawers, as in the panel.
