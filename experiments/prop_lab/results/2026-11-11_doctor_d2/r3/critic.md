# Blind critic, doctor's props D2, round 3

Judged from the five renders, the two target panels (dispensary, front office) and the period references
(bottles, mortar, scales, desk, chair). Only the props are judged, not the rooms.

## 1. Prop by prop

| Prop | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Cased balance (counter) | **SAME** | Matches the Wellcome 1860-80 cased balance closely: glazed box on a plinth, a central brass pillar, the beam nearly the case's width, two pans on chains. The glass is invisible (no edge or reflection, so it reads as an open frame) and the base has no drawer or weights beside it. |
| Mortar and pestle | **DIFFERENT** | The bowl is about the right size and shape, but it is orange-tan with dark specks and a lumpy, uneven rim, so it reads as a wooden or clay bowl. The Met mortar and the panel's are cream-white stoneware with a thick rolled rim and a pouring lip. The pestle's head can't be seen, only a dark stick standing upright in the bowl. |
| Counter bottles (8) | **CLOSE** | The shapes are right (shouldered vials, corks, paper labels, one cobalt, one amber with a yellow label). But the clear and aqua glass renders smoky grey, and the big stoppered jar looks full of black ink, so the group reads dull and dirty, not like glass catching window light. |
| Shelving and its bottles | **CLOSE** | A tall, dark built-in case like the Remick pharmacy's (a dark case is fine). But about 200 bottles stand in a perfect grid: the same two or three sizes, evenly spaced, each with an identical bright cream label at the same height. That reads as wallpaper. The panel mixes big labelled jars, small vials, cobalt, green and amber, with gaps and clusters. The case has no glazed doors and no drawer bank, and the back board's coarse red grain shows behind every bottle. |
| Counter (drawer chest) | **CLOSE** | A bank of small apothecary drawers is right for the period and the panel. But every front is identical, with dark recessed label plates (they should be bright brass or white card holders), knobs that are pin dots, a plain top with no marble slab or wear, and a very long, low proportion. |
| Desk | **CLOSE** | A roll-top on two pedestals is the panel's choice and right for the period. But the roll is a smooth curved side panel with no tambour slats. The interior is one row of eight empty slots holding a few sheets, not graded pigeonholes and small drawers. The green blotter is a flat square. The brass cup pulls are good. |
| Chair | **CLOSE** | A Thonet No. 14 type (hoop back with an inner loop, round seat), which is period-plausible. But the seat is a flat plain disc with no cane weave, there is no leg ring, and it is spindly next to the heavy desk. The panel (and Remick) show a sturdy wooden spindle-back or leather office armchair. |
| Wood on all furniture | (note) | The approved wood reads saturated cherry-red with big flame figure at this light, the same on the desk, chair, counter and shelving. All the furniture looks to be one piece of stock, and the figure is too large for the drawer fronts and the chair's thin bentwood. |

## 2. The five biggest differences

1. **The bottle wall is a uniform grid** (`shelves.jpg`, the whole case; `dispensary.jpg` right of centre). Identical
   rows, identical labels and even spacing make the case read as a texture, not a doctor's stock. There are no
   big show jars, coloured glass, gaps or leaning bottles as in the panel. `critic_crop_1.png`
2. **The mortar isn't stoneware** (`counter.jpg`, left of the balance). It is orange-tan and lumpy with specks, so it
   reads as a bread bowl or wooden bowl. It should be cream-white with a thick rolled rim, a lip, and a pestle with a
   pale head on a turned handle lying at a slant. `critic_crop_2.png`
3. **The roll-top has no roll** (`desk.jpg`, top). The curved sides are smooth panels with no tambour slats. Inside is
   a single row of empty slots with no small drawers or clutter (papers, ink, books), and the blotter is a flat
   green square. `critic_crop_3.png`
4. **The glass reads dark and smoky** (`counter.jpg`, the group at the left end). The clear and aqua bottles are grey,
   and the big jar is near-black inside with only an outline at its top. There are no highlights or light through
   the glass, even under a window. `critic_crop_4.png`
5. **The chair is a thin café chair** (`front_office.jpg`, `desk.jpg` bottom right). It is a Thonet hoop back with a
   plain flat disc seat, in the same red flame wood. It looks weightless beside the desk, where the panel has a
   solid spindle-back chair. `critic_crop_5.png`

Lesser: the counter's dark label plates and pin knobs; the balance's invisible glass.

## 3. The single change that would help most

Break up the shelving's bottle grid: mix sizes (big show jars, small vials), colours, gaps and label placement.
It is the biggest prop in the dispensary and reads as wallpaper.
