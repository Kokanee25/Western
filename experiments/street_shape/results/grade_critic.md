# Blind critic: Main Street, Set A vs Set B

## 1. Flat or sloped?

**Set B** is flat as a table in all four views. In east_down and jog_west the road runs dead level to a ruled line at the far end (crops `B_far_east_down_17_6.png`, `B_jog_mid.png`).

**Set A** has a gentle slope:
- **south_fronts**: the clearest. Past the walking man the road tilts up toward the far end, and the right-hand fronts step up with it.
- **east_down**: the far (west) end, with its wagon, sits higher than in B, so the street climbs away from you (`A_far_east_down_17_6.png`).
- **jog_west**: the hotel, the bank and the wagon all sit higher in frame than in B, so the ground rises west.
- **west_back**: the near porches sit higher in frame and the street falls gently away east.
- **aerial**: you can't see it from this height. Only the far-west buildings shift (in the diff).

The slope is modest: in one frame it reads as "not quite level" more than as a hill.

## 2. Set A: sitting on the ground

Fronts, porches and boardwalks mostly meet the dirt well. The south_fronts boardwalk has a proper step down, the west_back hotel posts land on the dirt, and the building backs in side_16 run into the ground. The faults are props and people left at the old flat height:

1. **market_16, centre wagon (x 390-540, y 300-380)**: sunk. The front wheel is buried nearly to the hub, the rear wheel's lower third is in the dirt, and the bed almost touches the ground. `crops/A_market_wagon.png`. The left wagon is fine.
2. **jog_west, wagon (x 540-650, y 270-345)**: sunk the same way, wheels hidden. B's stands on full wheels. `A_jog_mid.png` vs `B_jog_mid.png`.
3. **jog_west, man at left (x 10-80, y 270-415)**: his feet are buried, with no boots showing (B shows them). `A_jog_left.png` vs `B_jog_left.png`.
4. **west_back, hotel boardwalk**: a dark gap under the deck edge, the same in B. It reads as a shadowed crawlspace, so it's acceptable.

Nothing is clearly floating, and no floor is below the ground in these views.

## 3. Which reads more like a frontier street?

**Set A**, once its props are fixed. Frontier towns sat on whatever ground they were given. A street lifting slightly toward the far end, with fronts stepping up beside it, gives depth and looks laid on land. Set B's dead-level road looks like a stage set ending in a ruled horizon line. But A's slope isn't followed by what's placed on it: the sunk wagons and the man standing ankle-deep are what a viewer notices first, and they undo the realism. Settle the wagons and people onto the new ground and A clearly wins.
