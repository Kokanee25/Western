# Blind critic: ruled street or grown street

## 1. Which set reads as a real frontier street

**Set 1.** In Set 2 the street is a long straight strip running to a vanishing point. In `aerial_16` it is a clean, wide plank-coloured rectangle with every building squared to it. In `east_down_12` and `mid_west_17_6` you see straight down the whole length to open desert, and the fronts step back in one perfect perspective line. In `west_back_17_6` the camera even ends up beside a blank side wall. Set 1 breaks that up. In `aerial_16` the street is narrower and bends at the far (west) end, and the last building on the left sits turned toward the bend. In `mid_west_17_6` and `east_down_12` the view down the street ends against buildings, not a vanishing point. The Telegraph block sits proud of its neighbours and the fronts vary in height. In `west_back_17_6` the bend shows clearly: the left side closes in and the far fronts swing across the view. In `jog_west_16` a corner lot lies empty and the hotel shows its blank side. That reads as a town that grew. Set 2 reads as one that was drawn.

## 2. Set 1: the five biggest things that still read as ruled or wrong

1. **The ground is dead flat, like a table** (`aerial_16`, the whole frame; crop `crops/aerial_street.png`). The street, the lots and the desert are all one plane. A real street has a crown and drainage ditches, a wash or gully across one end, a slight rise the town climbs, and porches stepping up and down with the grade.
2. **The left side is five near-identical boxes in lockstep** (`aerial_16`, left of the street; crop `aerial_street.png`). They have the same depth, the same roof pitch, flush back walls and equal spacing. From the street, `east_down_12` shows the false fronts beyond the saloon falling away in an even run. A real block mixes one and two storeys, board and brick, different depths, lean-tos and back additions, and a vacant lot or a burned gap.
3. **The ruts are ruler-straight and the grass grows in hedges** (`east_down_12`, `mid_west_17_6`, `jog_west_16`, foreground). Two or three parallel ruts run unbroken the whole length. Tidy bands of grass line both road edges. A real street has ruts that wander and braid, churned hoof-mud down the middle, ruts that swing into the livery and wagon yards, puddles, manure and trampled bare ground in front of the hitch rails. Grass grows only where nobody walks.
4. **Nothing stands behind or between the buildings** (`aerial_16`; `jog_west_16`, the bare lots either side). The town is a single strip set into empty desert. Real back lots hold privies, woodpiles, sheds, corrals, wagons, trash heaps, clotheslines and paths worn between back doors.
5. **The town's edges are cut clean** (`aerial_16`, top: the dirt patch ends in a crisp edge at a lone pole; the right-side lots end in straight lines). A real town frays out. The road carries on as a trail, scattered shacks and a corral sit at the edge, and the dirt is trodden out unevenly.
