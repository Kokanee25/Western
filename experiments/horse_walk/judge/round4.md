# Blind judge, round 4: the final walk (2026-10-28)

A fresh agent, the same instructions plus a score. Reported as it came; no tuning after it.

**Score: 4/10.** "The rhythm reads as a walk, but the high head, the stiff toe-down swing legs and the
over-stretched, flat-footed trailing hind make it look like a stilted, shuffling horse rather than a
relaxed working walk."

1. **Head and neck too high (large; all pairs).** Real: neck about level, head low, poll near or below
   the withers. Ours: the poll well above the withers in every frame, "alert or being held up". (The
   fourth round in a row to rank this first, after the neck was lowered 16, 26 and 34 degrees.)
2. **The swing legs barely flex and the hooves point toe-down (large).** Real: the carpus folds
   clearly and the fore hoof is carried roughly level, sole tipping back; the hind's hock and fetlock
   lift it clear. Ours: nearly straight swinging legs, a fetlock bend only, the hoof "like a dancer's
   toe", skimming: shuffling, dainty.
3. **The trailing hind stretches too far back and stays flat at lift-off (clear to large).** Real: the
   hind leaves a little behind the point of the buttock, heel up, pastern near upright, hock angled.
   Ours: far behind the croup, the leg near straight, the hoof still flat.
4. **Little hind clearance, so mid-stride frames look like a horse standing square (clear;
   LF contact, RF contact).**
5. **The topline tilted (clear):** our croup sits lower than the withers, the back sloping uphill (the
   model's standing pose); the croup's rise and fall too small to see.

Kept: the four-beat lateral footfall order; the planted forelegs at mid-stance upright and well
placed; the body travelling smoothly.

## Reading it with rounds 2 and 3

The swing's fold has gone back and forth between "prancing" (round 2, at a 50 degree knee fold) and
"stiff, toe-down" (rounds 3 and 4, at 35 and 42): stills at eight moments catch the fold at
different points, and neither reading was "right". The next pass should judge the films (the swing
as it moves), and give the fore a fold that peaks early (~0.3 of the swing) with the hoof carried
level, not tipped.

Points 1, 3 and 5 share a cause in the model's own build: a high, upright neck and a croup lower than
the withers, with hind legs too short for the stride his trunk wants (README, "The stride length").
The neck was lowered by its bones to 34 degrees (the throat then shows ~11 folded triangles at
worst); lower wasn't tried this session. Whether the bones can take it further, or the neck's mesh
and rig need it (more neck bones, a lower neck in the build), is the next session's first test.
