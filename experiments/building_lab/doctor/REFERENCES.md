# The doctor's office: period references (step 1, #765)

Found by a research sub-agent on 2026-11-10. **None of these pages could be opened from the session**:
archive.org, loc.gov, gutenberg.org and the others are outside its network. Each item is checked
only against the search engine's record of it. Open each one before the spec (step 2) relies on it.

## Buildings and layout
- **South Park City Museum, "The Doctor's Office"** (Fairplay, Colorado): a three-room pioneer building
  furnished as a mining town's doctor's office. https://southparkcity.snappages.site/doctors-office
  Supports: a few rooms (waiting, consulting/surgery, dispensary). Its size isn't given.
- **Fort Davis NHS, the post hospital** (NPS bulletin, 1880s): the surgeon's office, dispensary and
  steward's room side by side, then the kitchen and an isolation ward.
  https://www.nps.gov/foda/upload/Post-Hospital-Bulletin-8-5-X-11-2-col.pdf
  Supports: the dispensary beside the office, a separate room for the sick.
- **St. Joseph's Miners' Hospital, 1887** (Ouray County Museum, Colorado): a preserved operating room in
  a mining town. https://ouraycountyhistoricalsociety.org/museum
  (Its "doctor's office" is from the 1940s: not a reference.)
- **History Colorado, "False Front Commercial"**: the type: frame, one or two storeys, rectangular,
  a front-gable roof behind a parapet front, often a cornice. https://historycolorado.org/node/3911
- **HABS, St. Elmo, Colorado**: the Russell-Thorp Building (CO-152, c.1880-81, a false front;
  photographs and data pages) https://www.loc.gov/item/co0326; the historic district (CO-72, which
  probably holds 4 measured drawings) https://www.loc.gov/item/co0182. The best lead for a measured
  plan of an 1880 Colorado false front.
- **Lake City, Colorado, the O.K. Store (1876)**: 35 ft front by 50 ft deep, a size check for a big
  store. https://www.townoflakecityco.gov/files/d09f5fc7a/35-+223+Silver+St.+O.K.+Store+%28Russ+Brown+Gallery%29+1876+c.pdf

## Photographs
- **Dr. Maghee's office, Rawlins, Wyoming, 1880s** (Carbon County Museum 1955.282.0025): a small
  clapboard building with a **gable front** (no false front), the door off centre, signs "Doctor
  Maghee" and "Dental Parlor". The closest exterior found.
  https://hub.catalogit.app/carbon-county-museum/folder/entry/3687a4d0-21d3-11ef-bb7d-131824c975fa
- **Dr. Heine's house and office, Philipsburg, Montana, c.1894** (Montana Historical Society PAc
  2017-47): the porch and the office door in a mining town. https://mhs.libraryhost.com/repositories/2/resources/1143
- **"Doctor's Office", c.1890** (Historic New England): an interior, a rough guide only (the East).
  https://www.digitalcommonwealth.org/search/commonwealth-oai:h989v734s

## What the rooms held
- **D. W. Cathell, *The Physician Himself*, 1882** (NLM): the office neat, his library, journals and
  instruments on show, no family in it; later editions advise two doors, so a patient leaves without
  crossing the waiting room (the surgery's side door). https://collections.nlm.nih.gov/ocr/nlm:nlmuid-101480177-bk
- **Tiemann & Co., *American Armamentarium Chirurgicum*, 1879** (Wellcome Collection): instruments and
  cases. https://wellcomecollection.org/works/cj8syhm8; 1889 edition (NLM)
  https://collections.nlm.nih.gov/dc/nlm:nlmuid-101494109-bk
- **The operating table:** factory tables come too late for 1882. The "Allison" table is 1890s
  (ECU https://digital.lib.ecu.edu/13885), and the "Perfection" chair-table is c.1890 (Dittrick
  https://artsci.case.edu/dittrick/?p=2303). So: a plain wooden table, padded, or a couch.
- **Light:** surgeons wanted daylight from big windows, ideally from the north, with oil lamps for
  work at night. Saint Marys Hospital's 1889 operating room had a north bay window
  (https://news.mayocliniclabs.com/?p=31301); Maine General, 1895
  (https://knowledgeconnection.mainehealth.org/photographs/71); Farrar 1880 on north light
  (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6984469/).

## Framing (for the spec, step 2)
- **Bell, *Carpentry Made Easy*, 1858** (HathiTrust 011542204): balloon frames, studs sill to plate,
  upper floors on a ribbon. Its sizes weren't extracted. The saloon's spec already uses it.
- **Woodward's *National Architect*, 1869** (HathiTrust), and ***Woodward's Country Homes*, 1865** (Gutenberg
  26354, "Balloon Frames" p.151). Second-hand figures: sills 9x4, corner posts 8x4, partitions 3x4 at 12 in.
- **Palliser's *American Cottage Homes*, 1878** (archive.org palliser.cottagehomes.1878): its
  specifications. Not seen.
- The Wood Handbook (USDA) for the member sizes, as the saloon and farmhouse specs do.

**Not found:** a measured drawing of a western doctor's office; an interior photograph of one from
the 1880s in the West; the size of any museum doctor's office.
