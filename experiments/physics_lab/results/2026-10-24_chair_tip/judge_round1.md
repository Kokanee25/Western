## B. The kick (single sheets)

**Run 1 (sheets 1–2): 4/10**
- I can't see a kick. The kicker stands right behind the chair and his leg barely moves. The chair legs just fly out (0.15–0.28 s).
- The sitter throws both arms out straight to the sides and holds them there, a symmetrical T (three-view 0.28–0.48 s).
- He goes over backwards onto the kicker's feet. In the side view his body ends up under or through the kicker's legs (0.78–1.18 s, sheet 2 1.98–3.98 s).
- The kicker stands like a statue for the whole 9 s. He doesn't step back, look down or react to a man landing on his boots.
- Getting up: he rises in front of and through the kicker's legs, stands, goes back down into a crouch beside his hat (5.98 s) and gets up again without it. The hat is still on the ground at 8.98 s.
- He ends standing shoulder to shoulder with the man who kicked him, both facing the same way with their arms hanging.

**Run 2 (sheets 3–4): 4/10**
- The kick is a small foot movement behind the chair at 0.15 s. The legs that break off spin away through the air (three-view 0.28–0.78 s).
- The drop onto the broken chair is reasonable: he lands sitting on the wreck and his hat flies up (0.78–1.18 s). His arms are again flung out stiff and level (0.78 s).
- He lies back with his legs over the seat (1.98–2.98 s), then slides well toward the camera (3.98 s). One second later he is kneeling back at the chair (4.98 s). The move between those two frames is too big to be believable.
- He steps up over the chair frame (6.98 s), and his foot seems to pass through it.
- The hat is left on the ground. He ends standing bolt upright beside the kicker, who never moved.

## The five biggest faults a viewer would notice

1. **Getting up pops and floats.** In Y (rise 2 and 3, 4–5 s) he holds a sitting pose in mid-air with the broken chair through his legs, and his arm stays out stiff. X (rise 3, 2.98–3.98 s) flings both legs into the air, then jumps into a crouch with his knees off the ground.
2. **There is no visible kick, and the kicker is a mannequin.** His leg hardly moves before the chair legs fly (sheet 1 0.02–0.28 s, sheet 3 0.15 s). He never reacts. The fallen man lands on and through his feet and gets up through his legs (sheet 1 0.78–1.18 s, sheet 2 1.98–3.98 s). Both end standing side by side (sheets 2 and 4, 7.98–8.98 s).
3. **The chair breaks like a toy.** Broken legs shoot one to two metres away and spin in the air (fall 3 three-view 0.43–1.48 s, sheet 3 three-view 0.28–0.78 s). A loose leg lands and stands upright on its end (fall 1, 0.43 s, both versions). The chair rests at a steep angle with nothing holding it up (rise 1 Y, every frame; fall 1 Y, 1.5 s).
4. **His arms are rigid.** In the startle he throws both arms straight out to the sides, perfectly level, and holds them (fall 2 and 3 three-view 0.30–0.43 s in both versions, sheet 1 three-view 0.28–0.48 s, sheet 3 0.78 s). On the rise, Y holds one arm straight out forward. Once he is standing, his arms hang dead at his sides.
5. **The recovery looks unnatural and unfinished.** He takes a huge lunging stride out of shot just after getting up (rise 1, 5–6 s, both versions). He bobs down a second time but often leaves his hat on the ground (rise 1, rise 2/3 Y, sheets 2 and 4 at 8.98 s). He ends at attention with no look back at the chair, no dusting off and no glance at the man who kicked him. In Y fall 2 and 3 (0.60–1.0 s), he bows, then sits up, then lies back.
