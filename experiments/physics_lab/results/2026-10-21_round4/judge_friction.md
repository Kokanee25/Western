**What runs through the pairs.** On flat ground (pairs 1, 4, 10) the hidden setting makes almost no visible difference. It shows on slopes, stairs and the chair: in each pair, one version keeps sliding or restarts after he has nearly stopped, and the other grips and stops where he lands. The sliding version glides down carpeted stairs at a steady speed (009), comes out of the chair (002), restarts after nearly settling (011), or is still going at 6 s (012). I nearly always preferred the version that grips and stops sooner. A real body snags on stair nosings and stops part way far more often than it toboggans. The exception is the wall (005), where sliding down onto his backside looked better than staying balanced on his heels. Some faults show in both versions and are not caused by this setting:
- he stands rigid against the wall for 1.5 s before going down (005);
- he ends in a butt-up, face-down kneel on the slope and in the chair fall (003, 002);
- he teeters on the edge at the top of steep stairs for 1-2 s before going over (007, 008);
- he goes forward off the low steps as a stiff plank (004).
