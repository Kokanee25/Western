## The three biggest problems

1. **No momentum carries through.** Whenever the railing breaks, the man and the debris fall almost straight down beside the deck edge and land at the post's foot. A full charge or a dead run reads no harder than a shove (shove_front and charge_front are nearly the same clip). The dead runner loses all his speed and kneels. The force of the hit can't be read from the fall.
2. **Held, motionless poses, with the railing giving way late.** He hangs draped over the cap (shove_back and charge_back, 0.38 to 0.98 s), kneels upright against it (run_dead, 0.38 to 1.48 s), or leans in as a rigid plank (both fronts). In charge_back and run_dead the railing breaks under a body that has already stopped, so the break looks unmotivated. The squat-and-recover in shove_back and the knees straightening after landing in blackout look alive, not limp.
3. **The railing breaks as a tidy unit.** A whole bay (cap and balusters together) drops out in one clump, like a ladder section, and leaves a clean rectangular gap with single balusters standing free at each end. There are no splintered stubs, no sagging or hanging cap rail, and the rest of the railing never bends. It also breaks on an ordinary shove, so a sound railing looks flimsy.
